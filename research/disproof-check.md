# Disproof check: fires vs. surviving records

Added 2026-11-04, at Moshe's prompt: for events like fires, test every
destruction claim against the records that demonstrably survived.

## The argument

"If the 1914 fire destroyed the records, why do we have 1902– birth records
on FamilySearch?"

- Czernowitz Jewish birth books **1902–1907** survive and were read entry
  by entry on FamilySearch (DAChO Fond 1245, opis 15, the Chernivtsi
  metrical-books record, catalogue 1378569; title pages and entries
  inspected 2026-11-04). Czernowitz was occupied three times in the same
  war. WITHDRAWN CITATION: an earlier draft of this file, following the
  deep-research report §3.1, cited "Kotzman (Kitsman) Jewish births
  1902–1907 in Fond 1245-1/941" from FamilySearch. The page-level check
  showed the FamilySearch 1902–1907 births in Fond 1245 are Czernowitz
  city books (opis 15); the opis 1 file 941 is not in that record at all,
  and catalogue searches for Kitsman and for Wiznitz returned no titles.
  Moshe's objection stands: a fire in Wiznitz is not tested by another
  town's survival unless the comparison answers the fire itself, which is
  why the lead survivor here is Sadagora (below), named in the same fire
  sentence as Wiznitz. What opis 1 file 941 actually holds, and whether
  any pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish volume survives mislabelled, remain
  on-ground questions (shelf check, Fond 1245-1 files 925–975). No
  surviving 1902 Wiznitz records were found; that claim is not made.
- Czernowitz Jewish records survive in depth (Fond 325, 1780–1944; city BMD
  series from 1841/1856), through three occupations.
- Sadagora selected vital records survive 1855–1930, though Sadagora burned
  alongside Wiznitz in September 1914 and its community president was
  murdered by Cossacks.
- Wiznitz's own Christian books survive pre-1918 (transcript chain into Fond
  1245; town-level ranges still to be verified on the ground).
- Even the one documented Wiznitz fire that touched a Jewish institution,
  the burning of Rabbi Israel Hager's court in the second occupation
  (Schmelzer, Gold vol. I), was partial on its own telling: the prayer room
  and Rabbi Mendele's chair survived it. No register is mentioned in the
  account.

## The rule that follows

A fire attested for a town, a synagogue, or a court is evidence about
**buildings**. It becomes evidence about **registers** only if a source
says so, or if the survival pattern leaves no alternative. Where comparable
registers survived the same fires and occupations, "the fire destroyed the
records" is not an explanation; it is a claim that itself needs proof, and
for Wiznitz none exists. The Wiznitz register loss therefore stays where
the evidence puts it: LOSS proven (index-book testimony, Burg testimony,
the 1924 permit case), mechanism unknown, with the broken custody chain
and the 1918–24 transfer interval as labelled inference, and mislabelled
survival in Fond 1245 (files 941–944, 960–971, opis 15) as a live,
testable possibility.
