# Vizhnitz / Wiznitz / Vyzhnytsia — Jewish Records and Community in WWI (Bukovina, Eastern Front)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Sources were gathered through the web index; items marked **[opened]** were read in full text during this research, items marked **[index]** are taken from search-index text/snippets of the cited page. Exact URLs are given in the Sources list, keyed [1]–[37]; timeline rows cite those keys.

## Terminology rule applied throughout (per requesting agent's correction)

- **LOSS** = the proven category wherever registers are shown to be gone by re-creation programmes, catalogue gaps, missing volumes, or an index entry for a book that can no longer be found. Loss is consistent with destruction, removal, seizure, evacuation without return, or later inaccessibility — it does not choose among them.
- **DESTRUCTION** = used only where a source documents the burning/destruction of the books themselves (mechanism, eyewitness, or loss inventory), or — for *buildings* — where burning of a synagogue/town is itself documented. Building destruction never, by itself, proves register destruction.
- **SOURCE'S ASSUMPTION** = where a source (e.g. JRI-Poland/Gesher Galicia town notes) says records were "destroyed during World War I" without naming a fire, unit, date, eyewitness, or inventory, that word is quoted as the source's honestly-made assumption, not as proven fact.
- **Applied to Vizhnitz itself: LOSS of the pre-1918 Jewish registers is proven. Their DESTRUCTION is not proven by any source found.**

## Inventory / mislabel rule applied throughout (Addition 5)

- **Catalogue/inventory absence is NOT shelf absence.** DAChO fond 1245 is **not Jewish-only** — it holds the metrical books of all denominations (including Roman Catholic Wiznitz vols 960–961 and Orthodox volumes), and its opis labels are known to be unreliable at file level. Documented examples (from the requesting agent's own file-level work, relayed with this tasking): **FamilySearch film 1378569 bundles Jewish BMD together with voter/member lists under the heading "civil transcripts"**; a Wiznitz birth volume **labelled 1918–1920 actually begins in September 1917**; files **941–944, catalogued as Kitsman, are reported to contain Wiznitz entries**. Neighbouring/intermediate volumes — fond 1245‑1 files around 933–944, files 960–971, and the files of **opis 15** — may therefore be mislabelled by town, denomination, or dates. **Every inventory-based claim in this report carries a "mislabel risk" qualification, and only a file-level content check (on the ground, §15) can settle what is actually on the shelf.**

## Summary

- **Vizhnitz the town** was overrun in the first Russian invasion of Bukovina (autumn 1914), was briefly an Austro-Hungarian divisional headquarters town in late November 1914, was evacuated by the Austrians in **December 1914**, and — on the strongest Vizhnitz-specific secondary account (Schmelzer, in Gold's *Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina*) — **the court of Rabbi Israel Hager was destroyed in the second Russian occupation (Nov–Dec 1914)**, while Hager's prayer room and an ancestral chair survived; Hager himself left with Russian permission, travelled through Romania, and was **directed by the Hungarian government to reside in Nagyvarad/Oradea**, where his court operated until his death on **2 June 1936** [1][2]. The town was "nearly destroyed" in WWI and its Jews fled, many to Vienna (Encyclopaedia Judaica) [5].
- **The registers**: Wiznitz Jewish birth/marriage/death registers demonstrably **existed before WWI** (a register index book listing them, with reference numbers, was seen in the Chernivtsi archive by Charles Rosner) and are **not findable today under their own labels**; the surviving DAChO/FamilySearch series (fond 1245‑1/933–940) is **labelled** 1918–1930, though its first birth volume in fact begins **September 1917** (file-level observation relayed by the requesting agent — see mislabel rule) [6][7]. That is **LOSS, proven at inventory level**. **No source found — in DAChO's published history, EHRI/USHMM descriptions, JewishGen/Gold, the JDC finding aids, or the Kriegsarchiv catalogue — states that the Wiznitz Jewish registers were burned, seized, or destroyed, and no transfer receipt or war-loss inventory for them was found.** A specifically "missing 1897 register" could **not** be verified in any source. *Mislabel risk: pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish material could still sit on DAChO shelves inside volumes labelled for other towns/denominations/dates (notably files 941–944, 960–971, opis 15); only the on-ground shelf check in §15 settles this.*
- **Why the Jewish series is missing while Christian series survive** is best explained structurally, not by a documented act: after 1875 Jewish *Matriken* in Galicia/Bukovina were kept by state-approved metricants whose annual duplicate went to the **civil district office (Starosta)**, whereas Christian parish registers ran in duplicate through **deanery → bishop's consistory** channels with institutional continuity [18]. Add custodian flight (the rabbi and community fled/expelled; no Jewish custodian remained in Vizhnitz after Dec 1914) and the region-wide pattern (Czernowitz's Jewish records, protected by a resident community and the provincial archive, largely **survived** [19]) — and the differential is explained by custody chains, which is **inference**, labelled as such below.
- **Church safeguarding of Jewish sacred property in WWI is documented once in Bukovina**: in Czernowitz, Orthodox Archbishop **Vladimir von Repta** took the Temple's **63 Torah scrolls** into his official residence for the whole war and returned them to the Chief Rabbi afterwards [1]. **No WWI case was found of any priest or parish hiding Jewish vital registers** in Galicia/Bukovina; the famous scroll-hiding cases (Buczacz, Cuhca, Lviv/Sheptytsky) are **WWII (1941–44)** and are kept separate below.
- **Leaders**: the only anti-Jewish measures in this sector that are documented as *ordered* are Russian and Stavka-level: Grand Duke Nicholas's order of **4 February 1915** — beginning "from Bukovina" — to evict Jews before the retreating enemy and take hostages from the prosperous/influential, with Yanushkevich's clarifications of **5 and 27 March 1915** [8]. No order by Lechitsky, Kaledin, Kornilov, Pflanzer-Baltin, Böhm-Ermolli, Arz, Joseph Ferdinand, Mackensen, Ludendorff or Hoffmann concerning Jewish registers/synagogues in Bukovina was found; for each, the report states what is documented and marks the rest unknown.
- **Movement records** exist in three layers (front / people / records themselves) and are catalogued in §8 with repositories and digitisation status; the single most promising unexploited series for placing the front at Vizhnitz on a given day is the Kriegsarchiv's **Neue Feldakten (NFA)**, which hold the AOK/army *Lagekarten* and unit war diaries, plus Pflanzer-Baltin's own diary-like operations notes for 1 Oct 1914–15 Sep 1916 [10][26].
- **Unverified lead (not a finding)**: a report relayed from researcher Alex Denisenko (Oct 2023, relayed Oct 2026) that a Ukrainian vital-statistics department in/near Vyzhnytsia holds **more** records but requires a power of attorney — office, holdings and PoA wording all unverified. See §16.

## 1. Front movement timeline for Vizhnitz specifically

### 1.1 The sector and the armies
Vizhnitz sits on the Cheremosh, at the junction of Bukovina and Galicia, south-west of Czernowitz. The Bukovina sector was held for Austria-Hungary first by improvised forces (Col. Eduard Fischer's gendarmerie/volunteer group, HQ Gura Humora/Kimpulung), then by **Armeegruppe Pflanzer-Baltin** (created 26 Sep 1914; became **7th Army** in May 1915; Pflanzer-Baltin to 8 Sep 1916, then Kirchbach briefly, then **Kövess** from 20 Oct 1916) [10][12]. Against it stood the Russian South-Western Front (Gen. **Nikolai Ivanov** to Mar 1916; then **Brusilov**): the **8th Army** (Brusilov, later Kaledin) in Galicia and the **9th Army** (**Lechitsky**) on the Prut/Dniester/Bukovina wing. Russian civil rule in occupied territory was the **Military General-Governorship of Galicia and Bukovina** (Count Bobrinsky, 5 Sep 1914–14 July 1915; in 1916–17 Fyodor Trepov for Ternopil/Chernovtsy provinces, then Dmytro Doroshenko as krai commissar from 2 Aug 1917) [8][11].

### 1.2 What can be said about Vizhnitz itself, in sequence
1. **Aug–Sep 1914**: Russian forces overran northern Bukovina; Czernowitz was abandoned by the governor on 31 Aug and occupied 2 Sep 1914 [1]. Rosner's Czernowitz-L history (family/memoir-based) states that in this first occupation **Sadagora and Wiznitz were plundered and set on fire, as were the synagogues of the area**, and the Bojan Hasidic court razed [4]. Schmelzer independently records the razing of the Bojan rabbi's court and synagogue burnings in the Czernowitz district in the first occupation [1]. *Category: burning of synagogues/town in the Wiznitz area — documented by secondary/memoir sources; no eyewitness document for Wiznitz's own synagogue was found.*
2. **Oct 1914**: Fischer's volunteers retook Czernowitz (Schmelzer: 21 Oct 1914) [1]; the front oscillated along the Prut. Vizhnitz's status in these weeks is not documented town-specifically.
3. **Late Nov 1914**: a contemporary primary source — *Jüdisches Archiv* No. 1 (May 1915), the organ of the Viennese Jewish War Archive committee — places the **Austrian command in Wiznitz (Gen. Schüller, Maj. Papp, k.u.k. 54th Infantry Division)** at the end of November 1914, and states **"Im Dezember mußte Wiznitz geräumt werden"** — in December Wiznitz had to be evacuated [3]. The same issue documents Cossack murder of Sadagora's community president Leib Kalman Retter in mid-October 1914 [3].
4. **26 Nov 1914**: Czernowitz was evacuated again and reoccupied by the Russians (Schmelzer; Rosner dates the hostage deportations of Mayor Weisselberger and Dr Menczel to this second occupation; British-style timelines give variant dates — 20/26/28 Nov appear in different sources; flagged, not harmonised) [1][4]. **Schmelzer places the destruction of the Wiznitz rabbi's court in this second occupation**: "Strozinetz, Czudyn, Sereth, Radautz, Gurahumora and Kimpulung were also occupied by the Russians and **the court of the Rabbi of Wiznitz was destroyed**. Miraculously though, the room in which Rabbi Israel Hager used to pray escaped the flames… [the chair of Rabbi Mendele] was eventually transported to Grosswardin, to where the Rabbi had fled" [1]. *Category: destruction of the court BUILDING by fire — documented by Schmelzer. It says nothing about registers.*
5. **1915**: Gelber dates Hager's permitted departure under Russian occupation to "(1915)" [2]; Schmelzer's narrative implies late 1914. The conflict is flagged; no exact departure date exists in any source found.
6. **Jan–Feb 1915**: Pflanzer-Baltin's offensive cleared Bukovina; Kolomea taken 16 Feb, Czernowitz retaken **17 Feb 1915**, Stanislau 20 Feb [12]. Vizhnitz thereby returned behind Austro-Hungarian lines (sector-level fact; no town document).
7. **1915–June 1916**: the front stabilised north-east of Bukovina along the Dniester/Prut; Vizhnitz was in the Austro-Hungarian rear. On reconquest, AH military authorities imposed martial law in Bukovina: **re-registration of the entire population, movement only with military passes, special food requisitions, reprisals against suspected collaborators** [8].
8. **June 1916**: Brusilov's offensive (from 4 June) — Lechitsky's 9th Army broke the AH 7th Army at Okna (10 June), took **Czernowitz on 18 June (Schmelzer) / 17 June (other sources — conflict flagged)** and Kolomea, and overran Bukovina within days [1][27]. Vizhnitz returned to Russian occupation until summer 1917 (sector-level; no town document found).
9. **1916–17 occupation administration**: Trepov (from 4 Oct 1916) governed the Chernovtsy province; after the February Revolution a Ukrainian commissar (Lototsky, per Encyclopedia of Ukraine) and from 2 Aug 1917 Doroshenko [11].
10. **July–Aug 1917**: after the Kerensky offensive (1–19 July; Kornilov's 8th Army took Halicz 10 July, Kalusz 11 July — in Galicia, not Bukovina), the German/AH counterstroke retook Tarnopol (25 July) and **Czernowitz on 3 Aug 1917** under Kövess's 7th Army; AH forces "drove the Russians almost entirely from the Bukovina" [12][27]. Vizhnitz's return to AH control in Aug 1917 is **inference** from the sector outcome.
11. **1917–18**: AH rear area; armistice with Russia 9 Dec 1917; Brest-Litovsk 3 Mar 1918.
12. **Oct–Nov 1918**: Jewish National Committee conference in Czernowitz 22 Oct 1918; Jewish self-defence organised late Oct; mutiny 31 Oct; Ukrainian takeover 6 Nov; **Romanian troops under Gen. Zadik entered Bukovina and took Czernowitz 11 Nov 1918**; General Congress proclaimed union with Romania **28 Nov 1918** [1][11]. Schmelzer records that the Romanian entry coincided with **forced evacuation of Jews from south-Bukovinian villages (Dorna-Candren, Pojona-Stampi, Teodoresti), homes burnt and property plundered**, and that martial-law Romania then evicted Jews from homes and confiscated property until Jewish National Assembly interventions secured returns [1]. Vizhnitz (Storojineţ county) passed to Romania with the rest of northern Bukovina by 24 Nov 1918 [11].

## 2. Jewish community of Vizhnitz in WWI

**Baseline.** Jews in Vizhnitsa from the mid-18th century; 1774: 60 families/191 persons; **by 1900: 4,738 Jews**; 1930: 2,666 [5]. Total town population 1910 ≈ 5,300 (so Jews were the overwhelming majority; a "95% Jewish" figure is also quoted for 1922 [20] — discrepancy flagged, sources differ). The community became independent only in the mid-19th century. Its centre was the **Vizhnitzer court of Rabbi Israel Hager** (rebbe from 1893), of the Kosov dynasty; he founded the Ahavat Yisrael/Bet Israel yeshiva in **1903** (YIVO-based sources) or **1905** (other Hasidic sources — conflict flagged); the yeshiva closed in 1914 [5][20]. Court staff named by Gelber: lieutenants Jechiel Steinmetz and manager Nisen Just [2].

**War.** Encyclopaedia Judaica's whole WWI notice is three sentences: "During World War I the town was nearly destroyed. The Jews fled to Vienna and some did not return. The rabbi at that time, Israel Hager of the Kosov dynasty, moved to Oradea, where he established his court. Vizhnitsa now ceased to be a hasidic center" [5]. Detail comes from Gold's two Bukovina volumes: Schmelzer (Vol I) for the war narrative (§1.2) and **Gelber (Vol I, pp. 89–90) for the court**: the Russians allowed the Tzadik to leave with his entire court for Romania; he travelled through Romania to Hungary, where the government directed him to live in **Nagyvarad (Oradea)** [2]. Hasidic tradition adds that the Russians wanted to imprison him and that he escaped (auction/Hager-family accounts — tradition, not documentation). His son **Eliezer lived in Vienna during WWI** with his father-in-law the Kopishnitzer Rebbe and was ordained there [20]. The Rosner family memoir adds a named Wiznitz family trajectory: the family left Wiznitz "during the November lull" (1914) and reached a **refugee camp in Bohemia**, the father joining the army as an NCO [4].

**Violence against Bukovina Jews in the same weeks** (context, all documented): hostage-taking of Czernowitz's Jewish mayor and notables and their deportation to Siberia [1][4]; murder of Sadagora's community president Retter by Cossacks, mid-Oct 1914 [3]; Sadagora pogrom Sept 1914 (An-sky) [15][28]; the Stavka hostage/expulsion system of Jan–Apr 1915 (§7). **No Vizhnitz-specific pogrom, hostage list, or expulsion order was found** — the town's documented harms are burning/plunder (Rosner), destruction of the rabbi's court (Schmelzer), evacuation (Jüdisches Archiv), and mass flight (EJ, Schmelzer). A Keren-Kratz statement that the *New York Times* reported Russian soldiers killing many of Vyzhnytsia's Jews is **secondary and unverified against the NYT original** — flagged, not relied on [20].

**Return.** Schmelzer: thousands returned and rebuilt; Siberian hostages returned in 1917 [1]. In **1922** the community asked Israel Hager to return; he declined and sent his son **Eliezer**, who re-established the yeshiva (1922/23) [20]. One concrete documentary trace of the register LOSS: Rosner reports that an elder brother of his father needed a **special permit to marry in Czernowitz in 1924 because no Wiznitz birth certificate could be produced** [7] — the practical consequence of the loss, visible in the Czernowitz wedding register.

**No standalone *Sefer Vizhnitz* Yizkor book was found** in JewishGen's Yizkor project or the Yiddish Book Center collection searches; Vizhnitz coverage is through Gold's *Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina* (Vols I–II). Recorded as *not found*, not as non-existent.

## 3. Fate of Jewish vital / community records

### 3.1 Wiznitz (Vizhnitz)
- **Surviving series (as catalogued)**: DAChO fond **1245‑1/933–940**, Jewish births/marriages/deaths for Wiznitz and surrounding villages, **labelled 1918–1930** (deaths begin 1920 — no 1918–19 deaths volume in the series), filmed by FamilySearch (films 2426184, 2446436, 2446437) [6]. A private index (Zeydele) counts 20,897 names for "Wiznitz & Waschkoutz 1918–1930" in fond 1245.
  - **Mislabel risk (Addition 5)**: the first birth volume, **labelled 1918–1920, actually begins in September 1917** (file-level observation from the requesting agent's own work). Fond 1245 is **not Jewish-only** — it also holds, e.g., Roman Catholic Wiznitz vols **960–961** and Orthodox volumes — and **files 941–944, catalogued as Kitsman (Kotzman), are reported to contain Wiznitz entries**; FamilySearch film **1378569** bundles Jewish BMD with voter/member lists under "civil transcripts." Files **960–971** and **opis 15** remain unchecked at file level. Therefore "the series starts in 1918" is a statement about *labels*, and the absence of pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish volumes *from the opis* is not proof of their absence *from the shelf*.
- **Existence before WWI — proven**: Rosner (post of 7 Mar 2006, reporting an archive visit) saw in the Chernivtsi archive "a book listing all registers with their reference number — **including those about Jews, meaning they existed before WWI — but it's impossible to find them now. In fact they have disappeared long time ago**"; writer Josef Burg (born in Wiznitz) told him that in the 1940s, needing replacement ID, he was told "the archives have disappeared"; and the 1924 special marriage permit (§2) corroborates unavailability by 1924 [7].
- **Category: LOSS — proven (at inventory level). Mechanism: UNKNOWN.** Rosner's own explanation — "My **guess** is that those archives about Jews were taken — and used; possibly even destroyed? — by the Rumanians when they took over Bukowina in 1918‑20" — is explicitly a guess by its author and is reported here as such [7]. *Mislabel risk applies to the loss claim itself: what is proven is that the pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish books cannot be found under their own labels; §15 task 1 is designed to test whether they survive mislabelled.*
- **Not verified**: (a) a specifically missing "1897 register" — no source mentions it; (b) any statement by DAChO, EHRI, JewishGen, Gesher Galicia, Routes to Roots or Miriam Weiner attributing the Wiznitz loss to wartime destruction. DAChO's own published history says only that the Land Archives ceased activity in WWI and were liquidated in Nov 1918 [24]; the *Encyclopedia of Ukraine* says Bukovyna's archives "sustained **major losses** during the First World War and the dissolution of the Austro-Hungarian Empire" — loss language, no itemisation [25]. (The September 1917 start, asserted in the original tasking, is **supported at file level** per the relayed observation above, though the catalogue label reads 1918 — a textbook instance of the mislabel rule.)
- **Contrast inside the same archive**: Czernowitz Jewish records largely **survived** — DAChO fond **325** (Jewish community of Chernivtsi, 1780–1944; USHMM/EHRI: 51,165 digital images) and city BMD series from 1841/1856 [19]; Sadagora selected vital records 1855–1930 survive; and Kotzman Jewish births **1902–1907** survive in fond 1245‑1/941 — proof that pre-1918 Jewish registers from Bukovina towns *can* survive in the same fond in which Wiznitz's do not appear under their own labels. *Mislabel risk: the 941–944 "Kotzman/Kitsman" files are precisely the files reported to contain Wiznitz entries — the contrast above must therefore be re-tested at file level before it is treated as final.*

### 3.2 The Galicia/Bukovina pattern (with the correction rule applied)
- **Re-creation programmes prove LOSS, not destruction.** For **Buczacz** and **Gwozdziec**, Gesher Galicia/JRI-Poland notes state that pre-WWI Jewish vital records were lost and civil marriage/birth records were **re-created after the war** at the request of individuals, families and courts; for **Halicz/Pomorzany** the notes say pre-WWI records are missing and war-year records curtailed. The word "destroyed" in those notes names no fire, unit, date or inventory and is classified here as the **sources' assumption**; the re-creations themselves prove only loss.
- **Sniatyn** (the requesting agent's example): JRI-Poland announcements say most Sniatyn Jewish vital records were "destroyed during World War I" and that re-created births/marriages for **1863–1914** were recorded after WWI for people needing proof (AGAD fond 300); marriages 1905–1914 in fact survive [23]. Classification: **LOSS proven (by re-creation); "destroyed" = JRI-Poland's assumption, quoted as such.**
- **Documented DESTRUCTION exists for buildings, and is town-specific**: Brody — An-sky's eyewitness account of the burned Jewish centre and the scorched synagogue wall [31]; Sadagora/Wiznitz area synagogues burned 1914 [4]; Bojan Jewish quarter incl. rabbi's residence and prayer house burned (Gold Vol I) [1]; Stanislav — synagogue burned, town twice occupied and destroyed by the Russian army (Encyclopaedia Judaica) [29]. None of these documents the fate of *books/registers*.
- **No Bukovina-specific document was found** of Russian authorities seizing/removing Jewish community registers, or of AH authorities evacuating Jewish registers from Bukovina towns. The *institutional* evacuation that is documented is of the provincial archive itself: the Bukovina Land Archives (founded 1907) ceased work at the outbreak of WWI [24].
- *Mislabel risk (general)*: every "survives / does not survive" statement in §§3.2 and 5 rests on published inventories of AGAD, TsDIAL, DAChO and regional archives, all of which are subject to the same town/denomination/date mislabelling documented for fond 1245. They are the best available evidence, not shelf-level proof.

### 3.3 How armies implanted themselves in a captured town
Documented mechanisms for this sector: billeting and living off the land with inhabitants quartering troops (Bukovina under occupation: "the occupying forces lived off the land, plundered at will, and quartered with the local inhabitants"); tribute demands secured by hostages (Czernowitz: 600,000 roubles in 24 hours, 23 hostages [1]); military administration through the General-Governorship with Russification (Bobrinsky) [8]; on AH reconquest, martial law with **total re-registration, pass system, requisitions, reprisals** [8]; in Czernowitz the Temple and Great Synagogue were *spared* by Repta's intervention — evidence that synagogue fate could turn on a single protector, not on a general order [1]. Synagogue requisition as hospital/barracks is documented elsewhere on the Eastern Front (e.g. German use of synagogues as hospitals in Congress Poland, 1915, in contemporary Jewish press) but **no Bukovina-specific synagogue-requisition document, and nothing for Vizhnitz, was found** — recorded as unknown for this sector. Register *seizure for intelligence* is not documented for Bukovina; what is documented is the opposite flow — armies *creating* population records (re-registration, passes, censuses), on which see §7–§8.

## 4. Local preservation by other denominations; differential survival (Addition 2)

### 4.1 Documented church safeguarding — WWI
- **Czernowitz, 1914–1918 (PROVEN, secondary source)**: Schmelzer records that the Czernowitz Temple and Great Synagogue were spared "thanks to the intervention of the Archbishop of Bukowina, Dr **Vladimir von Repta**," head of the Orthodox Church, whom the Russians entrusted with protecting the population: "**He transferred the sixty-three Holy Scrolls from the Temple to his official residence. They remained there until the end of World War I when they were returned to the Chief Rabbi**" [1]. This is the one WWI case found, anywhere in Galicia/Bukovina, of a church hierarch safeguarding Jewish sacred property — Torah scrolls, **not registers**, and in Czernowitz, **not Vizhnitz**.
- **Jewish self-preservation, WWI (for contrast)**: in 1915 the **Lutsk** community entrusted An-sky with a chest of synagogue antiquities (18th-c. manuscript, silver, Torah ornaments, valued 20–30,000 roubles), conveyed to the Jewish museum in St Petersburg expressly to save them from theft and wartime destruction [15-route source: shtetlroutes.eu An-sky itinerary].
- **No WWI case found** — despite targeted English/German searches — of a priest, parish or monastery hiding Jewish **vital registers** in Galicia/Bukovina, and none for Vizhnitz clergy protecting Jews, property, scrolls or the cemetery. Vizhnitz interfaith help in WWI: **UNKNOWN / no source found.**

### 4.2 WWII cases — clearly labelled comparison only (do not conflate)
- **Buczacz, 1943**: 45 Torah scrolls were carted at night to the **Greek-Catholic Basilian monastery**; later 20 scrolls were given to the **Roman Catholic priest** to hide and were returned after liberation (Sefer Buczacz, 1956, testimony of Shmuel Rosenthal).
- **Cuhca (Maramureş), 1944**: the village priest hid **eight Torah scrolls** and returned them to survivors (Pinkas Maramureş via JewishGen).
- **Lviv, 1941–44**: Metropolitan **Andrey Sheptytsky** and Greek-Catholic monasteries hid ~150 Jews incl. rabbis' families (Center for Urban History, Lviv Interactive).
These show the *mechanism* existed in the region's culture a generation later; they are **not evidence about 1914–18**.

### 4.3 Why do Christian registers survive pre-1918 where Jewish ones do not? (the differential question)
- **First, a verification caveat**: in FamilySearch's town-level catalogues, pre-1918 Christian series in DAChO fond 1245 are verified for the *district* — e.g. **Waschkoutz** (on the Cheremosh, Vyzhnytsia district) Orthodox books 1801–1928, **Banilla** Orthodox 1840–1938, **Sadagora** Roman Catholic civil transcripts 1871–1943 [6-catalogues]. The exact Wiznitz-town ranges quoted in the tasking (RC 1871–1913, Orthodox 1840–1926, GC 1876–1921) could **not** be independently verified at town level in this research; a DAChO fond 1245 opis check is still required (§15 task 2). The differential itself — Christian series in the same fond reaching back decades before 1918 while the Wiznitz Jewish series starts (at file level) in Sept 1917 — is real at fond/district level. A Ukrainian study of DAChO's Orthodox metrical books finds 1840–1944 preservation "more than satisfactory" [18-thesis]. *Mislabel risk: fond 1245 holds all denominations side by side (RC Wiznitz vols 960–961 etc.), so denomination attributions in the opis are exactly the kind of label the mislabel rule warns about — the differential must be confirmed file by file (§15).*
- **(a) Duplicate systems — the tasking's premise needs correction.** Jewish registers were **not** single-copy in law: after the Interior Ministry decree of 6 Dec 1873 and the 1875 metricant reform, Jewish *Matrikenführer* (state-approved registrars, supervised by the Starosta) kept the books and **had to deliver one of two copies to the district office (Starosta) after each calendar year** (JRI-Poland/Polish State Archives introduction; Halgal) [18]. The real asymmetry is the **destination and redundancy chain**: Christian parish duplicates went **parish → deanery (one copy retained) → bishop's consistory (second copy)**, inside a permanent ecclesiastical bureaucracy that outlived 1918; Jewish duplicates went to a **civil district office** whose wartime fate (evacuation, capture, Romanian-era transfer) is exactly the undocumented link. So: duplicates existed on both sides; only the Christian chain had a second, church-run repository.
- **(b) Custodian continuity**: in Czernowitz and Sadagora, Jewish custodians/communities remained or returned and their books survive; in Vizhnitz the custodian chain broke completely in Dec 1914 — the rabbi left for Oradea and never returned, the community fled or was displaced, and registration only resumes in Sept 1917 under late-AH/Romanian transition [1][2][6]. Whether Vizhnitz's priest stayed is **not documented**; parish continuity for the town is unknown. Building material (stone church vs rabbi's house/synagogue) is **not documented for Vizhnitz** and is not used as an explanation here.
- **(c) Evacuation of Wiznitz church records**: no record found that Wiznitz church registers were evacuated or specially stored during the Russian occupations — **unknown**.
- **(d) Does the differential rule out "the whole town burned"?** **Yes, as a *sufficient* explanation of the register pattern.** If a single town-wide fire explained everything, the Christian series in the same fond and district would not systematically pre-date 1918 while the Jewish series alone begins in 1917/18; and Czernowitz — occupied three times — kept its Jewish records. The pattern points to **custody-chain and transfer history**, i.e. to *where each series physically was* between 1914 and the Romanian-era consolidation of fond 1245 — which is precisely the undocumented interval. This is an analytical conclusion from catalogue evidence, stated as inference, and subject to the mislabel caveat above.

### 4.4 Where a church-safeguarded Jewish register would be today (checklist)
1. **DAChO (Chernivtsi), fond 1245** (metrical books) — including volumes labelled for other denominations/towns where a stray or mislabelled Jewish book could sit (files 941–944, 960–971, opis 15; §15 task 1); and **fond 325** (Czernowitz Jewish community) for community papers [19].
2. **Chernivtsi Orthodox Consistory / Metropolitanate archive** (Czernowitz was the seat of the Metropolitan of Bukovina and Dalmatia; Repta's residence was there) — no online catalogue found.
3. **TsDIAL, Lviv** — Galician consistory and parish duplicates (fond 201 Greek-Catholic consistory series) for Galician (not Bukovinian) cases.
4. **DAIFO (Ivano-Frankivsk)** — Stanislaviv eparchy/district material (fond 631 metrical series filmed by FamilySearch).
5. **CAHJP, Jerusalem** — community archives microfilmed from Eastern Europe (for Vizhnitz see §9.4 on file HM2/9176.6, contents unverified).
6. **Romanian National Archives, Suceava/Chernivtsi-era district holdings** for the 1918–24 transfer interval (Romanian Commission of State Archives of Bukovina, 1924–38, consolidated the records [24]).

**What the pattern predicts for Vizhnitz — and what it does not.** Front passage alone predicts *nothing* about registers: Czernowitz (3 occupations) and Sadagora (burned, president murdered) kept Jewish records; Sniatyn and Buczacz lost theirs and re-created them; Warsaw/Ober Ost show occupiers *creating* records. The variables that track loss in the documented cases are (i) small-town custody by a single registrar whose chain broke, (ii) absence of a provincial-archive or consistory duplicate destination, and (iii) the 1918–24 transfer interval. Vizhnitz scores high on all three risk factors — which makes its loss *unsurprising* but does **not** prove any mechanism. Conversely the pattern *refutes* two easy stories: "the Russians burned all Jewish records" (Czernowitz, Sadagora) and "the town burned so the books burned" (§4.3d).

### Occupation systems compared
- **Russian (Stavka/General-Governorship)**: ideologically antisemitic **expulsion + hostage + property-sequestration system ordered at the top** (Jan–Apr 1915, §7), Russification administration, *plus* uncontrolled Cossack looting/pogroms at capture and retreat (Lohr; Goldin; Gatrell, *A Whole Empire Walking*, 2005; Sanborn, *Imperial Apocalypse*, 2014; Prusin 2005; Holquist 2010).
- **German Ober Ost**: *Verkehrspolitik* — closed districts, movement bans, total registration/documentation; *Kultur* programme; economic exploitation; Jews harmed chiefly as traders by movement bans; records were **created and seized for administration, not burned** (Liulevicius, *War Land on the Eastern Front*, 2000). Warsaw GG was milder still and culturally permissive toward Jews (Nalewajko-Kulikov 2016) [36].
- **Austro-Hungarian Etappe/reconquest**: pre-war disloyalty lists (1912), internment (Thalerhof — overwhelmingly Russophile Ruthenians, ~30,000 sent, deaths in the low thousands; estimates vary), military courts; on reconquest of Bukovina: martial law, **re-registration of the whole population**, passes, requisitions, reprisals against suspected collaborators [8]. Jews were officially emancipated and heavily represented in the reserve officer corps, but locally scapegoated; the system's violence fell mainly on Ruthenians/Serbs.

## 7. Leader profiles (Addition 1) — dated orders/events only

**Russian side**
- **Grand Duke Nicholas Nikolaevich, C-in-C (Stavka)** — **Ordered**: 4 Feb 1915 order, quoted verbatim in the Carpathian Front synthesis from Nelipovich/Zapolovsky: "Starting from Bukovina, the Jews should be evicted following the retreating enemy and hostages should be taken from among the most prosperous and occupying public or other positions of the Jews"; hostages sent under guard to Kyiv province [8]. Nov 1914 he had already authorised hostage-taking "to secure the army from the harmful activities of the Jewish population" (Zavadivker). *Destruction/expulsion in his sector: ordered at the top.*
- **Nikolai Yanushkevich, Stavka chief of staff** — **Ordered/systematised**: circular of **25 Jan 1915** authorising expulsion of "all Jews and suspect individuals" from the front region (approval not retracted until 1917); **5 Mar 1915** clarification (evict all Jews in the combat area toward the enemy; take influential hostages); **27 Mar 1915** telegram (deportation deep into Russia "unacceptable… drive them forward behind the Austrians"; hostages to Poltava/Chernigov, property sequestrated); Apr 1915 General Staff circular collecting "facts" on Jewish treachery. Gen. Mavrin confirmed strict implementation 7 Apr 1915; 8th Army Corps reported execution 21 Apr 1915 [8][9].
- **Nikolai Ivanov, South-Western Front (to Mar 1916)** — implemented the system: ordered Bukovina/Galicia Jews deported **≥200 miles from army HQ**; his front HQ issued the Feb 1915 bans (no Jewish entry into Galicia, no movement between provinces, Yiddish press/correspondence bans) on an espionage pretext [8]. Planned the Jan 1915 Carpathian offensive. No personal memoir evidence.
- **Aleksei Brusilov (8th Army 1914–16; SW Front from Mar 1916)** — mixed record, documented both ways: his first Galicia proclamation promised continuity of Austrian law/officials; with Bobrinsky he **sought to moderate** Russification to avoid unrest behind the front; yet the hostage/expulsion system operated in his 8th Army's sector in 1914–15 and he co-formulated early Jewish policy with Yanushkevich (Lohr). His name attaches to the **4 June 1916 offensive** that retook Bukovina. His memoirs were **not** found to contain a Bukovina-Jewish-policy passage in this research — no claim made.
- **Platon Lechitsky (9th Army)** — the Bukovina army commander in both invasions' second phases: counterattacks that drove Pflanzer back (Mar 1915); broke Benigni at Okna **10 June 1916**, took Czernowitz and Kolomea, reached the Carpathian foothills by 20 June 1916. **No Lechitsky order on Jews, civilians or records found — UNKNOWN.** His army executed front/Stavka policy in the sector.
- **Aleksei Kaledin (8th Army from Mar/Apr 1916)** — Lutsk breakthrough June 1916; resigned May 1917. **No dated Kaledin order on Jews found.** (Cossack *units* were the main pogrom perpetrators of 1914 under other commanders — Mick; Kaledin's Don Cossack identity is not evidence.)
- **Lavr Kornilov** — **not a Vizhnitz/Bukovina town commander**: 48th Division in Galicia/Carpathians 1914–15, POW 1915–16, **8th Army from May 1917** for the Kerensky offensive (Halicz 10 July, Kalusz 11 July 1917, south of the Dniester in Galicia). Czernowitz fell 3 Aug 1917 on his front's flank during the retreat he resisted. No Jewish-policy order found.
- **Dmitri Shcherbachev (7th Army 1916; Romanian Front 1917)** — Strypa/Jazłowiec operations 1916; in 1917 his front was disintegrating (desertions during Mărăşeşti period). No Jewish-specific order found.

**Austro-Hungarian side**
- **Karl von Pflanzer-Baltin (Armeegruppe 26 Sep 1914 → 7th Army; Bukovina)** — retook Kirlibaba (22 Jan 1915) and Czernowitz (17 Feb 1915), June 1915 Pruth offensive; June 1916 collapse (ill; Seeckt took effective command; relieved Sep 1916). The martial-law/re-registration regime in reconquered Bukovina operated under his army group [8]. **No Pflanzer order on Jews/synagogues/registers found — personally UNKNOWN.** His papers (NL 557) are the key unexploited primary [10].
- **Hermann Kövess (7th Army from 20 Oct 1916)** — summer 1917 "made himself master of Czernowitz and Radautz and drove the Russians almost entirely from the Bukovina"; field marshal 5 Nov 1917 [12]. No civilian/Jewish-policy order found.
- **Eduard von Böhm-Ermolli (2nd Army)** — recaptured Lemberg 22 June 1915; Brody recapture (2 Sep 1915) attributed to his forces; army group in the 1917 counteroffensive. No Jewish-specific order found.
- **Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf (Chief of Staff to Mar 1917)** — author of the Carpathian winter offensives (from 23 Jan 1915); the system under him ran the 1912 disloyal-persons lists, military courts and Thalerhof internments (targeting chiefly Russophile Ruthenians). Anti-Jewish remarks are attributed to him in standard army histories; **no Conrad order on Jewish registers/synagogues was found**, and he is not quoted here beyond the documented system.
- **Arthur Arz von Straussenburg** — VI Corps to Aug 1916; **1st Army (Romania) 16 Aug 1916–Feb 1917**; **Chief of the General Staff from Mar 1917** — the clearing of Galicia and Bukovina (summer 1917) occurred under his tenure, executed locally by Kövess. No Jewish-policy order found.
- **Archduke Joseph Ferdinand (4th Army, 1 Oct 1914–June 1916)** — Volhynia/Carpathians, **not** the Bukovina sector; relieved after the Lutsk collapse June 1916 (4th Army shrunk catastrophically). AH forces in Galicia in 1914 conducted a terror against suspected Ukrainian/Ruthenian "spies" (Watson, *The Fortress*; review literature estimates up to ~30,000 victims by end 1914, sporadic and not centrally organised) — a system-level finding, not a personal Joseph Ferdinand order; no Jewish-specific order by him found.

**German side**
- **August von Mackensen (11th Army/Army Group, Gorlice-Tarnów, 2 May 1915)** — the breakthrough that ended the first Russian occupation of Galicia; later Romania. German practice in his advance: military requisition/exploitation; destruction in the zone is attributed by scholarship chiefly to battle and to Russian scorched-earth retreat. A Mackensen-attributed quote on looting/murder by troops circulates without verified date/context and is **not used**. No Mackensen order on Jewish registers found.
- **Erich Ludendorff / Max Hoffmann (Ober Ost)** — Ludendorff's administration (under Hindenburg; Hoffmann chief of staff 1916–18 after Ludendorff's move to OHL): *Verkehrspolitik* (closed districts, passes, registration), *Kultur* programme, district statute of 26 June 1916, exploitation for the Reich; Jews used as intermediaries and simultaneously harmed as itinerant traders [35]. **No evidence of German burning/seizure-destruction of Jewish registers in Ober Ost; the regime created records.** Hoffmann's *War Diaries* (1929) exist as a primary source; no Ober Ost/Jewish-administration passage from them was retrieved in this research — flagged, not paraphrased.

## 9. Oradea / Nagyvarad / Grosswardein (Addition 4)

### 9.1 Hager's exile — dates and documentation
- **Departure/route (PROVEN, secondary)**: Gelber — the Russians allowed the Tzadik to leave Wiznitz "with his entire court" for Romania; he travelled through Romania to Hungary, where "he was directed by the government to live in Nagyvarad (Oradea)" [2]. Yiddish tradition adds a first stop at **Kuty** (across the Cheremosh) before the move to Hungary in 5675 (1914/15). Schmelzer implies the court was destroyed around him in Nov–Dec 1914 and that he "fled" to Grosswardein [1]. **Exact arrival date in Oradea: NOT documented in any source found** (Gelber's parenthetical "(1915)" and the Center for Jewish Art's "fled… to Oradea in 1915" are the most precise statements available) [21].
- **Assigned residence & court operation (PROVEN, secondary)**: Oradea had Orthodox Jews but no Hasidim; grain dealer **Hermann Schwartz** provided a **six-room dwelling and a tavern for use as a synagogue**; a purpose-built Vizhnitz synagogue followed (CJA: built by a benefactor of the Rebbe; it served as the ghetto hospital in 1944) [21]; wealthy Hasidim later bought a large house on **Nico-Filipescu Street**, remodelled as the Tzadik's residence [2]. Sons **Baruch** (in Oradea during WWI) and **Haim Meir** (remained with his father, helped run the court and Agudat Israel work) and a son-in-law joined him; the court **expanded rapidly** from Oradea (Keren-Kratz 2024) [20].
- **Duration/departure/death (PROVEN)**: he **remained in Oradea after the war** and never returned to Vizhnitz; in 1922 he sent his son Eliezer back instead [2][20]. He **died in Oradea on 2 June 1936** (2 Sivan 5696), aged 76, and was buried after a 1 a.m. funeral attended by thousands of Hasidim from Bukovina, Czechoslovakia and Romania; Haim Meir succeeded him as head of the court **in Oradea** [2][20]. In **1949** his remains were transferred to Israel (Zichron Meir, Bnei Brak).
- **Press**: no Nagyvarad Hungarian/Jewish press item on the arrival was located (Hungarian-language searches returned only Gelber/MDPI material) — **unknown**, not evidence of absence.

### 9.2 Oradea as a records location
- **Bihor County branch, Romanian National Archives (Oradea)**: holds **Nagyvarad Jewish vital records up to 1914** — JewishGen's Hungary-SIG announced "up to 50 new registers, both synagogue and civil" released from the Oradea branch; a researcher report explains the chain: rabbinical registers (1850–1895) were kept by the Oradea Jewish Community and in the Communist period were gathered, with all Bihor county registers, into the State Archives branch, where they are preserved and researchable; 1895+ civil registers sat at the City Hall registry and were transferred periodically [22]. Individual 1890–96 Nagyvarad birth records were obtained from the archive in 2004 (documented success story) [22]. *Mislabel risk: these holdings statements are inventory-level; a shelf check is §15 task 6.*
- **Oradea Jewish Community archive today**: per the same researcher report, it preserves **only the Rulikovsky cemetery register** (still in use) [22].
- **CAHJP (Jerusalem)**: the requesting agent notes a known Vizhnitz file **HM2/9176.6**. This reference **could not be independently verified** in this research (exact-number searches of the web/NLI returned no record), and its contents, dates and provenance are **unknown from open sources** (on-ground/reading-room check: §15 task 5). No Oradea-specific CAHJP file on the wartime Vizhnitzer court (residence registrations, refugee lists, correspondence) was located.
- **Did court papers travel with the court?** **No indication found.** What demonstrably travelled was *movable sacred property* — Rabbi Mendele's chair, transported to Grosswardein [1] — and the court's *correspondence network* (e.g. a Hager letter datelined Grosswardein, Kislev 1933, survives in private/auction hands). No source states that Vizhnitz communal registers or court archives accompanied the exile; given §3.1, the pre-1918 registers were in the metricant/district custody chain in Bukovina, not in the Rebbe's household — so there is no reason to expect them in Oradea, and none are reported there. If any Vizhnitz-court papers survive institutionally, the plausible homes are CAHJP (cf. HM2/9176.6, unverified), the Hager family/Vizhnitz institutions in Bnei Brak, or DAChO fond 325-adjacent community files — all **unverified**.

### 9.3 Oradea as comparison
Oradea was a **Hungarian-interior city, never occupied in WWI**. Its own Jewish registers **survive** (proven at inventory level for the Bihor series above) through an unbroken custody chain: community → (Communist-era centralisation) → county state archive. Front-line Vizhnitz's registers are **LOST** (loss proven at inventory level; mechanism unproven — correction rule applied). The contrast is consistent with the custody-chain explanation in §4.3 and inconsistent with any claim that Jewish registers per se were targeted for destruction in 1914–18: where the chain held (Oradea, Czernowitz, Sadagora, Warsaw), the books are there; where it broke in the front zone amid flight and regime change (Vizhnitz, Sniatyn, Buczacz), they are not findable under their own labels. **Inference, labelled.**

## 11. Proven vs Inferred vs Unknown

**PROVEN for Vizhnitz itself** (source in §§1–3): pre-war Jewish majority and Hager court; town plundered/burned and area synagogues burned in 1914 (Rosner — memoir-based secondary); AH divisional HQ in Wiznitz late Nov 1914 and town evacuated Dec 1914 (*Jüdisches Archiv*, contemporary primary); rabbi's court destroyed in the second occupation (Schmelzer); Hager's permitted departure via Romania and government-directed residence in Oradea, court operating there until his death 2 June 1936 (Gelber; Keren-Kratz; CJA); Jews fled to Vienna and some did not return (EJ); pre-1918 Jewish registers **existed** (Rosner's index-book testimony) and are **lost at inventory level** (no pre-Sept-1917 Wiznitz Jewish volume findable under its own label; Burg testimony; 1924 permit case).

**INFERRED from pattern (labelled, not fact)**: that the register loss is explained by the broken custody chain (metricant → Starosta copies vs Christian consistory duplicates) plus custodian flight and the 1918–24 transfer interval; Vizhnitz's control status in Oct 1914, June 1916 and Aug 1917 (sector-level inference); that the same risk profile explains Sniatyn/Buczacz-type losses; the Oradea contrast (§9.3); leader-pattern implications (§7 table).

**UNKNOWN / no source found**: whether any pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish volume survives **mislabelled** on DAChO shelves (files 941–944, 960–971, opis 15 unchecked at file level — §15 task 1); the mechanism, date, and agent of the register loss; any Wiznitz-specific pogrom, hostage list, expulsion order, or synagogue-requisition document; a specifically missing 1897 volume; Hager's exact arrival date in Oradea and any Oradea residence/refugee registration for him; contents of CAHJP HM2/9176.6; any WWI priest-safeguarding of Jewish *registers* anywhere in Galicia/Bukovina; Wiznitz church-record evacuation; personal orders on Jews/registers by Lechitsky, Kaledin, Kornilov, Pflanzer-Baltin, Böhm-Ermolli, Arz, Joseph Ferdinand, Mackensen, Hoffmann; a standalone *Sefer Vizhnitz*; digitised Kriegstagebuch for the Wiznitz sector units; the Denisenko vital-statistics lead (§16).
