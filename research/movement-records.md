# Movement Records around Vizhnitz / Wiznitz, Bukovina, in WWI

**Scope.** Three kinds of "movement" records: (A) records that date the *front's* movement at
Vizhnitz (Vyzhnytsia, Vijnița, Wiznitz) on the Cheremosh; (B) records of *people* moving —
Bukovina Jewish refugees, evacuees, hostages, returnees; (C) records of *records* moving —
inventories, transfers, evacuations, war-loss documentation for the Wiznitz Jewish and civil
registers.

**Method / honesty rules.** Every factual claim below carries a source. Claims are tagged
**[PROVEN]** (a source states it for Vizhnitz itself), **[BUKOVINA]** (proven for Czernowitz /
Bukovina generally, applied to Vizhnitz only as context), **[INFERRED]** (my inference from
dated neighbouring events — the reasoning is shown), **[UNVERIFIED]** (a source asserts it but
I could not corroborate it, or sources conflict), **[NOT FOUND]** (searched, no source found —
the search is named). Dates for Czernowitz sometimes differ by 1–2 days between sources
(17 vs 18 June 1916; 17 vs 18 Feb 1915); both are given where they occur.

Geography that matters for every inference below: Vizhnitz sits on the Cheremosh river,
the historic Galicia–Bukovina border, south-west of Czernowitz, on the road/rail corridor
Czernowitz – Waschkoutz – Vizhnitz – Kuty – Kolomea. An army holding Czernowitz and advancing
on Kolomea had to pass through or past Vizhnitz; an army retreating from Czernowitz toward
the Carpathians in July 1917 had to cross the Cheremosh at or near Vizhnitz. Town coordinates
and the Cheremosh-border position: JewishGen discussion summarising the JewishGen town data,
"Wiznitz [48.15 – 25.11] … on the Cheremosh River (the border between Galicia and Bukovina)"
— https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/messages?page=12291&before=1262275920000000000&subsort=1

## B. People movement records

### B.1 The camp system — where Bukovina Jews were sent, and what records it made

- In September 1914 the Austrian Ministry of the Interior decided to create sorting stations
  on the railway lines and to house large numbers of refugees in camps [PROVEN]. Refugee
  camps **for Jews** were created in Moravia at **Nikolsburg (Mikulov), Gaya (Kyjov) and
  Pohořelice (Pohrlitz)** (21,000 inmates at the Moravian group) and in Bohemia at
  **Deutsch Brod (Havlíčkův Brod)** (20,000); camps at **Gmünd** (27,000, chiefly Ruthenians),
  **Choceň/Chotzen** (12,500, Poles) and **Wagna** (25,000, Poles, until May 1915) were *not*
  Jewish camps in this classification — a correction to the common assumption that all five
  named camps held Bukovina Jews. Francesco Frizzera, "Refugees (Austria-Hungary)",
  *1914-1918-online* (2017), DOI 10.15463/ie1418.11171, as reproduced at
  https://www.istrianet.org/istria/history/1800-present/camps/austria-hungary/frizzera_refugees.htm
  (canonical article URL given there: https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/refugees-austria-hungary/)
- Scale: about **360,000 Jews fled the eastern regions in 1914–1916**, 300,000 Ruthenians fled
  Galicia and Bukovina (1914 and 1916), and at least **30,000 Jewish refugees from Bukovina
  and Galicia fled to Hungary through the Carpathians**; by mid-1915 about 1.1 million people
  had sought refuge in the Empire's interior (Frizzera, as above).
- Camp education/administration records: the Baron Hirsch Foundation, with the Israelitische
  Allianz zu Wien and the state, ran schools in the Nikolsburg, Pohořelice, Gaya and Deutsch
  Brod camps, hiring teachers "brought in from Galicia or Bukovina … or refugees themselves"
  [PROVEN] — i.e. these camps generated school/personnel paperwork naming Bukovinians.
  Doina Anca Cretu, "War is destructive, but it reconstructs anew…", *Nationalities Papers*
  (2024): https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/FD674216B4B34CE50577D7ECC4DA79F5/S0090599224000643a.pdf/war-is-destructive-but-it-reconstructs-anew-refugee-education-and-state-consolidation-in-imperial-austria-during-the-first-world-war.pdf
- Camp-to-camp transfers and epidemics generated Ministry of Interior reporting (e.g. the
  March 1916 transfer of 2,400 Jewish refugees from Gaya to Ungarisch-Hradisch spreading
  measles; typhus carried from Wagna to Gmünd in spring 1915) [PROVEN] — "Child Assistance
  and the Making of Modern Refugee Camps in Austria-Hungary", *Central European History*:
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/central-european-history/article/child-assistance-and-the-making-of-modern-refugee-camps-in-austriahungary-during-the-first-world-war/9AB3BFDEB67491E8914D26D53A8456FF
- The Hungarian half: Galician/Bukovinian refugees in Hungary, incl. the 5 Oct 1914 military
  order removing civilians from Máramaros county within 48 hours, are documented from ÖStA
  Kriegsarchiv files (KA, Kriegsministerium, HR Abt. 10, Kt. 516, Zl. 1914/10/A.10, cited in
  the article) [PROVEN as a cited archival reference]: "Fellow citizens or aliens? Galician
  refugees during the First World War in Hungary", *Prace Historyczne* 148/4 (2021):
  https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/server/api/core/bitstreams/598dfb3c-d7c5-4685-a174-5299b0178f16/content

### B.2 Evacuation machinery in the Cheremosh district itself

- The Bukovina gendarmerie (Landesgendarmeriekommando Nr. 13, Czernowitz, under Eduard
  Fischer) had five district commands in 1914 — Czernowitz, Radautz, Suczawa, Kimpolung and
  **Nr. 5 Vijnița (Wiznitz)** [PROVEN]. Its wartime tasks expressly included "**evacuation of
  the population and livestock**, rescue of the wounded … and monitoring/control of persons
  transferred from one war zone to another part of the Monarchy" [PROVEN as the corps' stated
  tasks]. So an evacuation *apparatus* existed in Vizhnitz itself; its district-level paperwork
  has not been located online [NOT FOUND — searched for LGK Nr. 13 / Bezirksgendarmerie
  Wiznitz records]. Romanian Wikipedia article on LGK Nr. 13 (sourced):
  https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comandamentul_Provincial_de_Jandarmerie_Nr._13_din_Bucovina
- The autumn-1914 evacuation of civilians near the war zone was an empire-wide programme of
  the Joint War Ministry and the Ministry of the Interior; the Ministry's instructions of
  **6 September 1914** told local and police authorities how to classify refugees (those with
  ~200 crowns to live dispersed; the destitute to barrack camps) (Cretu 2024, as above)
  [BUKOVINA]. **No Vizhnitz- or Cheremosh-district-specific evacuation order was found**
  [NOT FOUND — searched English/German/Romanian web for Fischer/Gendarmerie evacuation
  orders, Wiznitz Räumung, and district evacuation decrees].

### B.3 Vizhnitz-specific people movements (named evidence)

1. **The Rosner family of Wiznitz** [PROVEN — family chronicle, single source]: they leave
   Wiznitz during the **November 1914 lull**; having no contacts in Vienna they "are directed
   to a refugee's camp in Bohemia"; the father, Haim Schmiele Rosner, is mobilised as an NCO;
   by **1917 the family is still in a refugee camp in Bohemia near Mährisch Budwitz
   (Moravské Budějovice)**. Ehpes Czernowitz-L archive:
   https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/czernowitz12/testfile2010-2/1319.html?
2. **R. Israel Hager, the Vizhnitzer Rebbe, and his entire court** [PROVEN — Gelber]: permitted
   by the Russian occupiers to leave Wiznitz ("1915"), via Romania to Hungary; the Hungarian
   government directs him to reside in **Nagyvárad (Oradea/Grosswardein)**, where a rich grain
   merchant, Hermann Schwartz, provides a 6-room dwelling and a tavern for use as a synagogue;
   he remains in Oradea after the war and does **not** return to Vizhnitz, later sending his son
   R. Eliezer back (Eliezer's own rabbinic installation in Vizhnitz is dated 1922 in the
   biographical tradition). Gelber, Gold vol. I, pp. 89–90 (URL in A.2). A secondary Hasidic
   biography says the Russians "wanted to imprison" him and that he "escaped" to Grosswardein,
   while his son Eliezer went to Vienna to his father-in-law [UNVERIFIED against Gelber —
   the two traditions conflict on *permitted departure vs escape*]:

   Oradea, not Vienna)** — a Meldezettel search in Vienna (WStLA) would be for *Eliezer* Hager
   and other Vizhnitz Jews; no online Meldezettel database names them, and no Hager entry was
   found in the sources searched [NOT FOUND].
3. **Hostages to Siberia (Czernowitz, the Bukovina pattern)** [BUKOVINA]: on the Russian
   retreat/return in November 1914 the Russians took prominent citizens as hostages, incl.
   Mayor **Dr. Salo Weisselberger**, Zionist leader **Dr. Mayer Ebner** and journalist
   **Dr. Philipp Menczel**, plus Austrian medical-corps soldiers left in the city as POWs
   (Gold vol. II, p. 28, URL in A.2). Ebner's biography dates his deportation to Siberia as a
   hostage by order of the Russian occupation governor Gierowsky, with return only after

   https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/Bukowinabook/buk2_125.html — while Weisselberger's
   biography dates his hostage exchange to **November 1915** and his return in 1916
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salo_Weisselberger — the two exchange dates conflict
   [UNVERIFIED which exchange freed which man]. The Romanian chronology (A.2) dates
   Weisselberger's arrest/deportation earlier, to the night of 16 Sept 1914 [conflict noted].
   **No hostage or expulsion list naming Vizhnitz residents was found** [NOT FOUND].

### B.4 The printed refugee directory — the single best name-level source

*Księga pamiątkowa i adresowa wygnańców wojennych z Galicyi i Bukowiny 1914–1915*
("Commemorative and address book of war refugees from Galicia and Bukovina 1914–1915"),
ed. Antoni Chmurski, Vienna/Poznań 1915 — three parts: I. Lwów; II. Kraków; **III. Province
and Bukovina**. Each entry gives the refugee's name, occupation, home town, arrival date, and
Vienna (or other) address with household size ("os." = osoby, persons — confirmed by
cross-checking an entry against the man's Vienna Meldezettel) [PROVEN].
Digitised, free, at the Silesian Digital Library (ŚBC):
- Part III (the Bukovina part — where a Vizhnitz refugee in Vienna would appear):
  https://sbc.org.pl/dlibra/publication/990/edition/929
- Part I: https://sbc.org.pl/dlibra/publication/988/edition/1013/content?ref=struct
- Part II: https://sbc.org.pl/dlibra/publication/989/edition/1021
Worked Bukovina example from the directory, discussed on JewishGen: "Klinger Juda, kup.
[merchant], **Kuty**, 4.VIII.914 [arrived 4 Aug 1914], Wien XX, Karl-Meissl-Strasse 1 …
7 os." — Kuty is Vizhnitz's immediate Cheremosh neighbour, and the entry shows the format a
Wiznitz entry would take: https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/messages?expanded=1&msgnum=646633
Caveat: the directory records refugees who registered **in Vienna / the named cities in
1914–15**; camp inmates in Moravia/Bohemia and later (1916) waves are not its coverage.

### B.5 Relief and return, 1917–1919

- **JDC Archives, New York Office 1914–1918 collection, Record Group 4-3: Bukovina** consists
  of a single thin file found in the finding aid: **File 102, "Bukovina, General, 1917"**
  (0.10 inch) — Michael Ringer's 1917 report on relief for Jews in Galicia and Bukovina
  (Lemberg Relief Committee), incl. occupational/social/political history [PROVEN]. The
  companion **File 106, "Galicia, 1915–1919"** (Record Group 4-6) contains the same Ringer
  report, a 1919 report of the Society for the Rescue of Abandoned Jewish Children of Galicia
  and Bukovina, and Dec 1918 cables on pogroms [PROVEN]. **No JDC file naming Vizhnitz /
  Wiznitz as a locality was found in the 1914–1918 finding aid** [NOT FOUND — the finding
  aid's Bukovina coverage is the single "General" file above]. Finding aid (digitised files
  are linked file-by-file from it):
  https://archives.jdc.org/our-collections/finding-aids/new-york-office/1914-1918/countries-and-regions/
- Post-war JDC in Romania is a *1919–1921 collection* matter: the JDC organised in Romania in
  1919 (Central Relief Committee, Bucharest; Hetty Goldman as representative), and Baruch
  Zuckerman later "mainly investigated living conditions in Bukovina and Bessarabia" and
  wrote detailed reports on the Jewish institutions of the towns he visited [PROVEN as a
  summary of the published JDC-Romania study; the town-level reports themselves were not
  located online]. Study text: https://www.scribd.com/document/445447710/INTRODUCTORY-STUDY-from-Natalia-LAZAR-Lya-BENJAMIN-Joint-Tipar-10-octombrie-FINAL
- **Return/rebuilding in Vizhnitz specifically** [PROVEN]: after the Romanian occupation, the
  repatriation of Jewish war refugees from Austria "ran into difficulties, since most of the
  Jewish communities had been seriously damaged"; the JDC's representative, **Lt. Becker** of
  the American Army, toured Bukovina, and the Czernowitz inter-party Joint Committee under
  Karl Klueger "had sufficient money to help rebuild Jewish houses in **Sadagura, Waschkoutz
  and Wiznitz** in such numbers that all the residents of these three cities were given the
  opportunity to reestablish normal business activity" (Reifer, Gold vol. II, p. 1):
  https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/Bukowinabook/buk2_001.html
- Empire-level return: refugees were progressively repatriated from the camps from 1915
  (e.g. Bruck an der Leitha's Jewish refugees "were repatriated by 1915" — Cambridge, Child
  Assistance article above); no Vizhnitz-specific repatriation list was found [NOT FOUND].

## Proven / Inferred / Unknown — summary

**PROVEN (Vizhnitz-specific)**
- Wiznitz was plundered and burned, with the area's synagogues, during the first Russian
  invasion (autumn 1914) — Ehpes Czernowitz-L chronicle (single, non-official source).
- There was fighting *at* Wiznitz between 17–28 June 1916 (Britannica 1922 war history).
- On 30 July 1917 the front ran along the Cheremosh at Wiznitz, Russians on the east bank
  (German official communiqué, contemporary press).
- The Bukovina gendarmerie's district command No. 5 sat in Wiznitz; evacuation of population
  was among its corps' wartime tasks (Romanian LGK Nr. 13 article).
- At least one Wiznitz Jewish family (Rosner) evacuated in the Nov 1914 lull and was still in
  a Bohemian refugee camp (near Moravské Budějovice) in 1917 (Ehpes).
- R. Israel Hager left Wiznitz with his court under Russian occupation ("1915", Gelber) and
  was settled by the Hungarian government in Oradea, never returning (Gelber, Gold vol. I).
- Post-war JDC-linked funds rebuilt Jewish houses in Wiznitz (Reifer, Gold vol. II).
- The surviving Wiznitz Jewish registers at DAChO begin in 1918 (Fond 1245-1/933–940)
  (FamilySearch catalogue of the DAChO originals).

**PROVEN (Bukovina/Czernowitz, context for Vizhnitz)**
- Czernowitz's three Russian occupations: 2 Sept–21 Oct 1914; 28 Nov 1914–17/18 Feb 1915;
  17/18 June 1916–3 Aug 1917 (multiple sources, A.2).
- Hostage deportations of Czernowitz notables to Siberia, 1914 (Gold vols. I–II).
- The Jewish refugee-camp system (Nikolsburg, Gaya, Pohořelice, Deutsch Brod) and its
  scale (Frizzera, *1914-1918-online*).
- The 1914–15 printed refugee address directory exists, is digitised, and Part III covers
  Bukovina (ŚBC).
- JDC's wartime Bukovina coverage is one thin 1917 file (File 102) (JDC finding aid).

**INFERRED (reasoning shown in the tables)**
- Vizhnitz fell to the Russians within days of Czernowitz in Sept 1914, Nov 1914 and June
  1916, and was retaken around 3 Aug 1917 — from the corridor geography + the dated
  Czernowitz/Kolomea/Kimpolung anchors. No source found gives the town's own capture dates.
- Gelber's "1915" for the Rebbe's departure = the second occupation (Nov 1914–Feb 1915).
- The pre-1918 registers were lost *in* Vizhnitz (rather than in transit or in Czernowitz):
  inferred only from the town's attested burning in 1914 + the registers' absence — **this is
  the weakest link in the whole dossier and is not proven by any source found.**
  Correction rule (Moshe, 2026-11-04), applied across this dossier: LOSS is the proven
  category; DESTRUCTION is one possible mechanism for a loss and needs its own source.
  A re-creation programme, a gap, or a missing volume proves loss only, and a source's
  bare statement that records were "destroyed in the war," with no fire, unit, date,
  eyewitness, or loss inventory, is a historian's assumption, honestly made, not proof.

**UNKNOWN / NOT FOUND (searched, named)**
- Any inventory, transfer act, evacuation order, or war-loss statement for the Wiznitz Jewish
  or civil registers — searched DAChO data via FamilySearch/EHRI/USHMM/JewishGen, ÖStA
  Archivalienschutz history, German archive-administration scholarship. **Nothing.**
- Any Austrian Archivschutz evacuation of Bukovina registers in 1914–17.
- Any Cheremosh-district-specific evacuation/expulsion order; any hostage/expulsion list
  naming Vizhnitz residents; any JDC locality file for Vizhnitz; any EHRI/CAHJP-described
  Wiznitz community fond; any digitised Kriegstagebuch or Russian 9th Army war diary for the
  Cheremosh sector; Fischer's *Krieg ohne Heer* in digitised form.
- Conflicting points left unresolved: Fischer's recapture date for Czernowitz (Sept vs Oct
  1914 in secondary summaries); Weisselberger/Ebner arrest date (16 Sept vs Nov 1914) and
  exchange date (Nov 1915 vs after 3 years); Czernowitz capture dates ±1 day in 1915/1916;
  the Bukowinafreunde armistice date of 9 Dec 1917 vs the conventional 15 Dec.
