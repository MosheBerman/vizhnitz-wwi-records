# Comparative Patterns: Jewish Communal and Vital Records on the WWI Eastern Front
## Towns other than Vizhnitz — what is documented, what survived, and what predicted loss

**Compiled:** 2026-11-04 (America/New_York)
**Purpose:** A comparative baseline for Vizhnitz/Wiznitz, Bukovina. If Vizhnitz's pre-1918 Jewish registers are missing, is that the normal fate for a front-line Bukovina/Galicia town, an exception, or neither? This file answers that from other towns, town by town, with a cited source for each claim.

**Method and honesty rules used throughout**

- Every factual claim about a town carries a source (title/author/date) and a URL copied from a live search result in this research pass.
- Where no source was found for the specific fate of a town's *registers* (as distinct from its synagogue or its people), the entry says **"no source found"** rather than inferring register loss from violence against people or buildings. Violence against a community and destruction of its paper are related but are **not the same claim**.
- Three grades are used: **PROVEN** (a source says it directly), **INFERRED** (the archival inventory pattern is consistent with it, but no source states the cause), **NO SOURCE FOUND**.
- Dates sometimes conflict between sources (e.g. Julian vs. Gregorian, or a memoir vs. an official chronology). Conflicts are flagged, not smoothed over.
- Wikipedia and community-history pages are used only for capture/recapture dates and are flagged as such; claims about Jews, synagogues and registers are, wherever possible, sourced to Yad Vashem community histories, Hugo Gold, S. An-sky, JRI-Poland / Gesher Galicia archival inventories, Encyclopaedia Judaica, or scholarly work (Lohr, Gatrell via Motta, Watson, Richter, Weeks).

## 1A. Cross-denomination column — did the Christian books survive where the Jewish books did not?

**This section is the cross-denomination companion to the master table above, keyed town by town.** Three honesty rules: (1) an inventory run that *ends* in e.g. 1913 is **not** evidence of wartime destruction — current registers often stayed in the parish, or continued in a different fond; only a run that *spans* 1914–1918 in one volume, or a stated loss, proves anything. (2) Most Christian evidence here is at parish/district level, not always the town proper — the level is stated in each row. (3) "Differential: YES" means the *inventory pattern* differs by confession; it does **not** by itself prove the occupier targeted Jewish paper — the custody systems differed (see §3A).

### 1B. Documented safeguarding of Jewish scrolls, buildings and people by Christian clergy and others — and the counter-cases

**PROVEN cases**

1. **Czernowitz — Metropolitan (Archbishop) Vladimir de Repta, Orthodox Metropolitan of Bukovina — the one fully documented clergy-safeguarding case in this entire comparison, and it is Bukovina's own.** Three source types agree:
   - Hugo Gold, *Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina*, vol. II, p. 28 (Czernowitz chapter): after the Russian commander's extortion order against the Jews was nullified, "The protector of the Jews was the Archbishop Dr. von Repta, who had stored the Torah scrolls in his palace for safekeeping." — https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/bukowinabook/buk2_028.html
   - Rosner's Bukovina narrative: in the second occupation (from 26 Nov 1914), with the mayor and notables deported to Siberia and hundreds of flats plundered, "most of the town and all buildings of Jewish worship are spared, thanks to the courageous intervention of Dr Vladimir von Repta… He will even have the Tempel's Torah rolls removed into his residence, where he will protect them till the end of the war; he will then give them back to the Great Rabbi of Czernowitz."    - Contemporary press: *Die Wahrheit* (Vienna), Mar 1915, reprinting Dr. E.    - Scholarly treatment: Ioan-Paul Valenciuc, "Mitropolitul Bucovinei, Vladimir de Repta, și comunitatea evreilor din Cernăuți," *Analele Bucovinei* XVII:1 (2010), pp. 109–116. — https://www.diacronia.ro/ro/indexing/details/A32527/pdf
   Note precisely what Repta is documented safeguarding: **Torah scrolls, synagogue buildings, and people — not register books.** No source in this pass documents any cleric, anywhere in these towns, safeguarding Jewish *metrical registers*.
2. **Sadagora — a (lay) pharmacist hid ~100 Jewish women in his cellar for three days during the Sep 1914 pogrom** (An-sky). Safeguarding of people, by a layman, not clergy, and not of records. — https://www.jhom.com/calendar/elul/personalities/ansky/galicia.htm
3. **Husiatyn — Jewish soldiers serving in the Tsarist army initially saved the tzadik's castle and the synagogue** (Virtual Shtetl). Safeguarding by co-religionist soldiers; it held only until the next change of hands. — https://sztetl.org.pl/en/node/935/99-history/137367-history-of-community
4. **Tłumacz (Galicia) — tradition, unverified:** Helsinki synagogue's tradition that Jewish soldier David Mirmovitsch rescued a Torah scroll from a destroyed synagogue in Tłumacz during WWI and carried it in an ammunition box until 1918. Included only as a flagged, low-grade tradition of a *soldier* (not clergy) saving a scroll. — https://www.youtube.com/shorts/IvxCgFWFv9g

**Counter-cases (must sit beside the Repta case)**

- **Czernowitz, same occupation:** Gold vol. II also records that Christian fellow-citizens "had hung holy pictures in their windows in order to direct the plundering Russian soldiers to the Jewish homes." The same city produced the archbishop who hid the scrolls and householders who marked Jewish homes for looters. — https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/bukowinabook/buk2_028.html
- **The Greek Catholic Church was itself a persecution target, not a protected one.** Russian occupation policy aimed at the "liquidation" of the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church: Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky was arrested and deported to Russia on **19 Sep 1914** (imprisoned at Suzdal), bishops and 30+ priests followed, hundreds of GC priests were exiled to Siberia and replaced by Orthodox priests, and GC churches were forcibly transferred to Orthodoxy — though in nine months only 86 of 1,873 GC parishes became fully Orthodox. So "Christian" was not one category under Russian occupation: Orthodox = the occupier's church; Greek Catholic = an enemy church whose *clergy* were deported like Jewish notables, even while its *books* survived in Lviv. — https://eprints.zu.edu.ua/48404/1/1.pdf ; https://ojstest.lnu.edu.ua/collections/index.php/snote/article/download/784/789 ; summary — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Eastern_Galicia,_1914%E2%80%931915

## 2. Town notes — evidence, conflicts, and what is *not* proven

### 2.1 Czernowitz — negotiated surrender, a protector, and surviving books
Rosner's Bukovina narrative (Czernowitz-L archive, 2010, retelling Philipp Menczel's wartime experience) is the most granular source found for any Bukovina town: a partial mobilisation posted 26 Jul 1914; a first battle 8 km away on 23 Aug; on Wednesday 2 Sep 1914 the Russians, fearing a trap, demanded unconditional surrender at the sugar factory under threat the town "will be destroyed and razed down to earth," and the Jewish mayor, Dr. Salo von Weisselberger, went out with lawyer Philipp Menczel to surrender it; Russian troops occupied it that evening. Jews arriving from Sadagora described Cossack terror there, and in the countryside "Sadagura and Wiznitz are plundered and put on fire, as well as all the synagogues in the area; the Hassidic court of Boja[n] is razed down to earth" — while the capital itself was not burned. On the second Russian entry (26 Nov 1914) Weisselberger, Menczel and other notables were taken hostage and deported to Siberia and hundreds of apartments were plundered, "but most of the town and all buildings of Jewish worship are spared, thanks to… Archbishop Dr Vladimir von Repta. He will even have the Temple's Torah rolls removed into his residence, where he will protect them till the end of the war." The Austrians recaptured Czernowitz 17 Feb 1915; the Russians retook it 18 Jun 1916; the final Austrian recapture was 3 Aug 1917.
**Date conflict:** the *Encyclopedia of Ukraine* entry for Chernivtsi gives the three Russian occupations as 30 Aug–21 Oct 1914, 26 Nov 1914–18 Feb 1915 and 18 Jun 1916–2 Aug 1917 — i.e. a first entry about three days before Rosner's surrender scene and an extra Austro-Russian exchange in Oct–Nov 1914 that Rosner's narrative compresses. Both are reported in the table; neither is suppressed.
**Registers:** FamilySearch's catalog describes civil transcripts of Jewish births, marriages and deaths for Czernowitz filmed at the Chernivtsi State Archive as "Metrical books, 1856–1940," with birth volumes e.g. 1860–66, 1866–71, 1871–77, 1877–79, 1879–81, 1881–83, 1886–88, 1889–94 in Fond 1245. The Routes to Roots inventory (as reproduced by the Czernowitz ehpes group) lists Czernowitz births 1855–1860 and 1860–1930, deaths 1875–1914 and 1916–1925, and "Kahal (Jewish community) records 1780–1944; 1867–1929." That is survival *through* three occupations. **Caveat recorded in the same ehpes post:** an archivist-side remark that a pre-WWI register list exists "but it's impossible to find them now," with a correspondent's guess that Jewish archives were taken/destroyed by the Romanians in 1918–20. That is an unverified anecdote about *some* registers, set against a catalogued, filmed, surviving Fond 1245 — the anecdote is noted, not adopted.

### 2.2 Sadagora and Boyany — the Hasidic court as a loot magnet
Sadagora is the closest Bukovina parallel to Vizhnitz: a Hasidic court town a few kilometres from the capital, hit in the same first invasion. Three independent source types converge: (1) Hugo Gold's *Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina* (vol. II, Bruckenthal's "Sadagura") — after the battle of Rarancze in summer 1914 Czarist soldiers "looted and burned the town," named merchants were among the victims, and "many old Jews were forced to go as hostages to Siberia on foot. Most of them did not live to reach their destination." (2) Gold vol. I (Schulsohn) on Boyany: "At the beginning of the First World War, the Jewish quarter of Boyany including the residence and the prayer house was burned to the ground by the Russians," while "the prayer house still stands in Sadagura, but it is empty and desolate." (3) An-sky, who saw Sadagora in the winter of 1916–17, found the rebbe's court and synagogue standing but gutted and in use as a military typhus hospital, and a town of "smoking chimneys, skeletons of burned houses" after a September 1914 pogrom in which the community chairman and three others were killed. The Sadigura court itself had left for Vienna in 1914 (Chabad's history of the Kloyz Kadisha synagogue) — the dynasty evacuated, its buildings did not.
**Registers:** no source found stating the fate of Sadagora's own register books. The Czernowitz inventory above does list some Sadgura material (births 1900, 1921–22; deaths 1886–1904; Sadgura Kahal 1855–1890) — partial, gappy, and held in the capital's archive, which is itself a pattern (see §3).

### 2.3 Sniatyn — loss proven, "destroyed" asserted (correction, Moshe, 2026-11-04)
The JRI-Poland announcement says: "most of the Sniatyn Jewish vital records were destroyed during World War I," and the post-war re-created births and marriages (1863–1914) exist because citizens "required proof of birth and marriage documents that were destroyed during the War." Two different things must not be merged. (1) **The re-creation programme proves LOSS**: after the war the originals were not available to the citizens or the authorities who would otherwise have issued extracts, so entries for 1863–1914 had to be recorded anew. Loss is also consistent with removal, seizure, evacuation without return, or the books sitting in an archive the post-war authorities could not reach. (2) **"Destroyed" is the announcement's word for the mechanism, and on the evidence given it is a historian's assumption, honestly made, not a proven fact**: the announcement names no fire, no unit, no date, no perpetrator, and no eyewitness or inventory showing the books' destruction. So Sniatyn is classified here as: wartime LOSS — proven (re-creation programme); DESTRUCTION — asserted by JRI-Poland, mechanism unproven. Any Vizhnitz argument must be built the same way: a re-creation programme, a gap, or a missing volume proves loss, never destruction by itself; destruction needs its own source (an eyewitness, a loss inventory, a contemporary report).

### 2.4 Przemyśl and Stanisławów — synagogues and registers decouple
These two towns break any simple "occupation = records lost" rule in opposite-looking but actually identical ways. Przemyśl suffered a siege, starvation, a pogrom at the synagogue door, and (on Watson's account) a mass deportation of its Jews in April 1915 — and its Jewish registers survive from 1789 to 1900, because register books lived in the civil registration system and were in due course delivered by the town USC to the state archive. Stanisławów had its synagogue *burned down* (Encyclopaedia Judaica) and was "twice occupied and destroyed" — and its registers survive from 1789 (marriages, TsDIAL) and in long AGAD runs through 1904/1912. **In both towns the building burned or the people were deported, and the paper survived.** The converse also occurs (Sadagora's walls survived, its interiors and contents did not). Building fate, population fate and register fate are three separate variables.

### 2.5 Kovno — evacuation decided everything, in both directions
Gircytė's archivist account is the only source in this pass that describes the *archival mechanics* directly, and it describes a fork: institutions fleeing Kovno in summer 1915 took "their most important" records into the Russian interior — those mostly survived and were repatriated under the post-war peace treaty from 1921 over about a decade — while records judged less essential "were left abandoned… almost totally damaged and never reached the Kaunas archives." Loss here was not caused by a German shell or a Cossack torch; it was caused by a triage decision made under time pressure, plus abandonment. This is the strongest documented mechanism available for thinking about a register that is simply *not there* after the war — and it cuts both ways: evacuation is also how the Czernowitz-adjacent and Galician duplicate books survived elsewhere.

### 2.6 The general Russian-army pattern (context, not town evidence)
Three works frame all the town cases. Eric Lohr's research, as summarised in Giuseppe Motta's JDC-documents study, phases the Russian army's anti-Jewish measures: chaotic expulsions Jul 1914–Jan 1915; a 25 Jan 1915 Stavka order (Yanushkevich) to expel Jews from the whole zone of military operations; organised mass deportations by train in Apr–May 1915; then a shift to **hostage-taking** in place of wholesale deportation. *The Jews in the Eastern War Zone* (American Jewish Committee, 1916) states the Galician baseline: during "the ten months of the Russian occupation of Galicia the Jews of that section suffered even more severely than did the Jews who dwelt in the Russian Pale," with synagogue desecration witnessed by Russian Jewish soldiers themselves, and a July 1915 Interior Ministry order expelling Jews from towns near the Austro-Hungarian frontier. A 2026 survey of religious life in western Ukrainian lands 1914–1918 likewise records that "hundreds of synagogues and Jewish prayer houses were desecrated, looted, and destroyed," Governor-General Bobrinsky's 13 Feb 1915 segregation decree for occupied Galicia, and the town of Ustia (Borshchiv district), where Russian troops "drove out the entire Jewish population and destroyed their houses down to the foundations." None of these general works names Vizhnitz; they establish base rates, not a Vizhnitz verdict.

Sources for this section: Motta, "The Great War and the Jewish Refugees in Russia. Research in the documents of the Joint Distribution Committee," in *Unknown Fronts* — https://iris.uniroma1.it/bitstream/11573/927614/1/Motta_Great-War-Jewish-Refugees_2017.pdf ; *The Jews in the Eastern War Zone* (1916) — http://easteurotopo.org/articles/eastern-war-zone/eastern-war-zone.pdf ; "Religious Life in Western Ukrainian Lands from 1914 to 1918," *Occasional Papers on Religion in Eastern Europe* XLVI:5 (2026) — https://eprints.zu.edu.ua/48404/1/1.pdf ; Lohr, "The Russian Army and the Jews: Mass Deportation, Hostages, and Violence during World War I," *Russian Review* 60:3 (2001), 404–419, and Semion Goldin, "Deportation of Jews by the Russian Military Command 1914–1915," both as cited in Frank M. ## 3. Pattern synthesis (one page)

**The pattern is not "the Russians came, so the records burned." It is narrower, and it is testable against Vizhnitz.**

**What predicted LOSS**

1. **Fire at a change of hands — above all, a burning retreat.** The single most destructive moment in case after case is not the occupation but the *retreat*: Rohatyn burned by retreating Russians in June 1915 (synagogues destroyed, after the town had already been ransacked in Sep 1914); Brody half-devoured by fire at the Russian onslaught; Husiatyn 600 of 699 houses burned in the first days; Sadagora looted and burned after Rarancze. The great Russian retreats — June 1915 (Gorlice-Tarnów) and July 1917 (after the Kerensky Offensive) — are the two peaks. A town the front *passed through twice* had roughly twice the exposure.
2. **Small town, no negotiated surrender, Cossack first contact.** Every total-burning case here is a small town or shtetl (Sadagora, Husiatyn, Rohatyn, Brody, Ustia). The first-contact units — Cossack patrols and divisions (1st Terek Cossack Division in the Nadworna sector; Cossacks at Przemyśl, Czernowitz's hinterland, Lemberg) — are the named perpetrators of the pogrom-and-loot phase in source after source, before any occupation administration existed to restrain them.
3. **Wholesale expulsion of the community — the registrar leaves, the books stay.** Zaleszczyki (all Jews expelled 15 Apr 1915), Kovno province (May 1915), Przemyśl (Apr 1915, on Watson's account), Rohatyn's 570 hostage-men (Jun 1915). An expelled community takes nothing bulky; its register books are left ownerless in a looted town, which is exactly the Kovno "left abandoned… almost totally damaged" mechanism.
4. **A Hasidic court or a rich synagogue as a standing loot target.** Sadagora's court (looted, then a typhus hospital), Boyany's court (burned), Husiatyn's Friedmann mansion (saved once by Jewish soldiers in Russian uniform, destroyed at the next change of hands). Requisitioning for hospitals/barracks preserved walls and destroyed contents — books, scrolls and furnishings included.
5. **Post-war successor-state sorting as a second, silent filter.** Brody's surviving runs stop in 1871/72 — *before* the war — and Tarnopol's birth books are missing for most of the late 19th century with no wartime explanation found. Gaps have more than one possible birthday: Romanian takeover in Bukovina (1918–20, per the unverified Czernowitz anecdote), Polish AGAD/Zabużańskie transfers in Galicia, and plain pre-war loss all competed with wartime destruction. Attributing a gap to WWI requires a Sniatyn-type source, not just a gap — and even a Sniatyn-type source proves wartime loss, with destruction only as its asserted explanation.

**What predicted SURVIVAL**

1. **Size, a negotiated surrender, and a protector.** Czernowitz was surrendered by its mayor under ultimatum, was not burned in three occupations, had an archbishop physically remove and guard its Torah scrolls — and its Jewish metrical books (1856–1940) and Kahal records survive in Fond 1245. Lemberg, taken in a single advance, lost dozens of Jews to a pogrom but kept the archive that now holds all of eastern Galicia's registers (TsDIAL Fond 701).
2. **Registers living in the civil system, with duplicates.** Przemyśl (1789–1900, via the town USC) and Stanisławów (runs in both AGAD Warsaw and TsDIAL Lviv) show the Galician duplicate/central-copy system absorbing a burned synagogue and even a deported community. Where a second copy sat in a district or crownland repository, the town copy's fate stopped mattering.
3. **Evacuation *with* the books — and a treaty to bring them back.** Kovno's evacuated "most important" records survived in Russia and returned from 1921; its abandoned records perished in place. Evacuation is not loss or survival in itself; the Kovno fork shows it is both, decided by what was put on the train.
4. **A German (Ober Ost) occupation without a preceding Russian expulsion or a battle for the town.** Vilna was entered "without a shot" on 18 Sep 1915; its WWI Jewish grievances in the sources are hunger and levies, and its Great Synagogue and Strashun Library came through WWI whole. Length of occupation, by itself, predicts almost nothing: Tarnopol endured the longest Russian occupation in this set (1914–17) with a looting retreat at the end, Przemyśl one of the shortest (73 days) with a deportation — severity tracked *how* a town changed hands, not how long it was held.

**Bottom line for Vizhnitz.** Vizhnitz sits in the highest-risk cell of this table on every loss predictor: a small Bukovina shtetl (not a capital with a surrender negotiation), a famous Hasidic court (a documented loot/requisition target at Sadagora, Boyany and Husiatyn), on the Prut/Cheremosh front that changed hands repeatedly, in the sector where Rosner's narrative — the only Bukovina narrative source in this pass — states in so many words that "Sadagura and Wiznitz are plundered and put on fire, as well as all the synagogues in the area." Sniatyn, one valley away, has proven wartime *loss* of most of its Jewish vital records (re-creation programme), with "destroyed during World War I" as JRI-Poland's asserted, unproven mechanism. That makes wartime *loss* of Vizhnitz's pre-1918 registers **the expected outcome, well supported by pattern and by one direct Bukovina statement about the town itself being burned — but destruction specifically remains, for the registers, an inference**: no source found in this pass says "the Wiznitz register books were burned/seized/taken," and the Czernowitz counter-example (15 km of archive shelf surviving three occupations in the same province) is the standing warning against treating the inference as proven. The two findings that would settle it are a Sniatyn-type statement for Wiznitz (a re-created-records programme, a Chernivtsi archive loss list, or an EHRI/DAChO fond history), and the Fond 1245 file-level inventory for Wiznitz itself — what volumes the Chernivtsi archive *catalogues* as having once existed versus what it holds.

## 3A. Synthesis addendum — the cross-denomination pattern

**Does differential survival exist? Yes, in specific places, and the file now proves it at inventory level in two: Sniatyn and Sadagora.** In the Śniatyn district a Greek Catholic birth book (Demycze, 1890–1928) runs straight through the war in the same successor archive that holds the district's records, while most of Sniatyn's Jewish vital records were lost during WWI (re-creation programme; JRI-Poland's "destroyed" is an asserted mechanism, not a proven one). In Sadagora, Roman Catholic *civil transcripts* in Chernivtsi Fond 1245 run 1871–1931 with no wartime break, while the Sadgura Jewish runs in the same fond are fragmentary. Against that, in Czernowitz, Lemberg and Przemyśl there is **no** differential: Jewish and Christian books alike survived. And in Brody the Christian Lviv-held runs *also* break off in the 19th century, which forbids a purely confessional reading there.

**Three mechanisms explain the differentials that do exist, and only the first two are documented:**

1. **Custody architecture (documented as a system, INFERRED as the cause in each town).** Both Catholic churches ran a consistory-copy pipeline into Lviv: TsDIAL today holds 7,421 Greek Catholic parish books (Fond 201, 1776–1944) and 2,657 Roman Catholic books (Fond 681, 1600–1948), and AGAD holds thousands more Catholic units (Fonds 298/301/437) than any single-town fire could touch. Bukovina's Fond 1245 likewise holds deposited transcripts of *all* confessions, Orthodox volumes included, spanning the war. A book already copied into, or deposited in, a provincial repository was effectively fire-insured. Jewish registers had a district system and AGAD Fond 300 duplicates too — which is exactly why Przemyśl's and Stanisławów's Jewish books survived — but the coverage was patchier, and a small Bukovina town's Jewish book was likelier to exist as a single town copy.
2. **The occupier's confessional map (documented).** For the Russian occupier, Orthodox was its own church (its Bukovina transcripts continue uninterrupted), Jews were a suspect enemy population (expulsion, hostages, pogroms), and Greek Catholics were a rival church to be liquidated at the level of *clergy and allegiance* — Sheptytsky deported, priests exiled, churches transferred — but not, on the evidence here, at the level of *burning its deposited books* in Lviv. Persecution of people and destruction of paper again decouple, now by confession.
3. **Individual protectors (documented, but only for scrolls/buildings/people).** Repta in Czernowitz is the proven case, corroborated by a Jewish community history, a Bukovina narrative, contemporary Viennese Jewish press, and a Romanian scholarly article. No case was found — in any town, by any cleric — of safeguarding Jewish *register books*. Registers were protected, when they were protected, by systems (duplicates, deposits, evacuation), not by rescuers. That asymmetry matters for Vizhnitz: nobody's palace was going to save a birth register, and there is no Repta figure in the Wiznitz sources; what could have saved the Wiznitz books is a deposited transcript in Czernowitz — which is a checkable archival question (the Fond 1245 file-level inventory), not a narrative one.

**Revised bottom line for Vizhnitz.** The cross-denomination evidence sharpens, and slightly redirects, the §3 conclusion. Fond 1245 demonstrably survived the war with Christian and Jewish Czernowitz volumes intact, and small-village Orthodox volumes in the same fond run through 1914–1918. So if the pre-1918 Wiznitz Jewish books are absent from that fond, the likeliest explanations narrow to: (a) the town copies were destroyed in the town (fire/looting, as Rosner describes for Wiznitz itself) *and* no deposited transcript existed or survived for those years; (b) the volumes were removed in a later sorting (Romanian 1918–20, Soviet, or post-war) — the unverified Czernowitz anecdote's claim; or (c) they were never deposited. The Sniatyn parallel makes wartime loss entirely credible, with (a) destruction in the town as one candidate mechanism among (a)–(c); the Fond 1245 control group makes "the archive burned" untenable. The discriminating test remains the fond's own inventory: whether Wiznitz Jewish volumes are *catalogued as once existing* (with a loss note or a gap in the numbering) or were never in the deposit series at all.
