# Reaching the Vyzhnytsia / Chernivtsi vital-statistics (DRACS) offices independently — and the on-ground shelf-check plan

Compiled 2026-11-04. Purpose: the user traces an 1897 Jewish birth record from Wiznitz (now Vyzhnytsia, Chernivtsi oblast). His Chernivtsi researcher Alex Denisenko reported that a Ukrainian department of vital statistics (відділ ДРАЦС) holds additional/older records but that access requires a power of attorney. This file identifies that department from **official Ukrainian sources**, gives the independent routes to it, and sets out the shelf-check an on-ground researcher would run because **inventories may be mislabelled**.

**Status tags used throughout:** PROVEN = stated by the cited source. INFERRED = my reasoning from proven facts, shown. UNVERIFIED = found only in unofficial/secondary sources, or not confirmed in this pass — do not use without re-checking.

**Headline conclusions**

1. There are **two different offices**, and Alex's report fits the **oblast-level** one, not the small town office: the Відділ ДРАЦС у Чернівецькій області in Chernivtsi (вул. О. Кобилянської, 31), the unit that physically held the oblast's older civil books and is still handing them to the state archive — in **October 2026** it transferred the **1944–1945** books to DAChO (PROVEN, DAChO's own news). The Vyzhnytsia town office is a working registry office for current acts.
2. The power-of-attorney requirement is **real and written down**, but it is DAChO's own rule as much as DRACS': DAChO's published procedure says a person searching the reading room on behalf of another must produce either the customer's consent statement + passport copy **or a notarised power of attorney** (PROVEN, cv.archives.gov.ua). Nobody needs Alex specifically to hold that PoA — any representative the user appoints can.
3. By the 75-year rule, an 1897 metric book should **never** be in a DRACS office at all: DRACS holds Soviet/Ukrainian civil act books for 75 years, then transfers them to the state archive. Pre-1918 Wiznitz books are Austrian-era religious registers / civil transcripts, and the surviving ones are PROVEN to sit in DAChO as Fond 1245. So if anything relevant still sits on the DRACS side, it can only be (a) an **un-transferred backlog** volume, (b) a **Romanian-era (1918–1940) civil register** that passed to Soviet DRACS in 1940/44 and was never inventoried into Fond 1245, or (c) a **stray/mislabelled volume**. That is exactly why §3 is a *shelf* check, not a catalogue check (INFERRED from the proven rules and holdings).

### 1.3 What the oblast department (Office A) actually is

Office A is the oblast-level DRACS department under the Registration Directorate (Управління державної реєстрації) of the interregional administration. It supervises the district offices **and holds the oblast DRACS book archive from which transfers to DAChO are made**: DAChO's news of 15.10.2026 states it "completed acceptance into state storage of the civil registration books of Chernivtsi oblast for 1944–1945 — 68 books of the Sokiryany, Storozhynets and Khotyn districts," handed over by deputy head Ганна Новікова of Office A, after joint preparation (file titles, foliation, certification inscriptions, inventories). Source: DAChO official site, http://cv.archives.gov.ua/

Historically this DRACS-side archive in Chernivtsi held major Jewish material: the Routes to Roots inventory (Miriam Weiner, *Jewish Roots in Ukraine and Moldova*, 1999), as summarised for the 2006 Czernowitz reunion, listed Czernowitz Jewish births/marriages 1926–1929 at the "Chernovtsy ZAGS Archive" and noted that at that time "the bulk of the Cz. Jewish records were held by the ZAGS office," with the position already changing by 2006 (https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/reunionposts/archives/chernivtsiarc.html). **That is the institutional memory behind a claim like Alex's** — but it is a 1999/2006 picture, not a 2026 one (PROVEN as a historical statement; UNVERIFIED for today).

### 1.5 The 75-year rule — and what it does and does not imply (PROVEN rule; INFERRED application)

The State Archival Service's official FAQ: RAHS/DRACS archives **hold their records for 75 years; after that, by law, all documents must be transferred to the state archives**; a book containing entries spanning many years stays at DRACS until the **last** entry in it is over 75 years old. Personal-data access to records under 75 years is restricted (written permission of the person/relative concerned). Source: https://old.archives.gov.ua/Eng/q-a.php

Arithmetic for 2026:
- A DRACS office should normally hold only books whose last entry is from **after ~1951** (2026 − 75).
- Chernivtsi is demonstrably running **late**: DAChO accepted the **1944–1945** books only in October 2026 — an ~81-year lag (PROVEN, §1.3). So the oblast DRACS archive in 2026 plausibly still held books into the late 1940s.
- **Pre-1918 material is outside this system entirely.** An 1897 Wiznitz Jewish birth book is an Austrian-era religious register (civil transcript). Such books are not "civil act books" created by DRACS; the surviving Wiznitz set is in DAChO, Fond 1245, opis 1, files 933–940 (births/marriages/deaths 1918–1930), filmed by FamilySearch at DAChO (catalogue 1461615: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1461615). Nothing in the 75-year rule puts an 1897 book in Vyzhnytsia town hall.
- **Interwar (1918–1940) Romanian-era books** sit between the systems: civil registration in Romanian Bukovina was kept by the primăria (mayor's office), with the community's religious registers continuing alongside; after 1940/1944 the Soviet DRACS inherited local books, and over decades they were transferred to DAChO — the Wiznitz Jewish 1918–1930 books demonstrably completed that journey into Fond 1245. But the transfer record is book-by-book, not town-by-town, so an un-transferred or mis-inventoried interwar volume on the DRACS side is possible (INFERRED) — and a pre-1918 volume could only be there as a stray bound into a later book or mis-shelved (INFERRED).
- **Metric books vs civil registers:** for the shelf check the distinction matters physically. Metric books (метричні книги) = church/synagogue registers, pre-1918 Austrian and interwar religious; civil act books (книги державної реєстрації актів цивільного стану) = Soviet/Ukrainian DRACS books from 1940/44 on. A researcher asking only for "книги ДРАЦС" will be shown the second kind and truthfully told there is nothing older. The ask must name **метричні книги** explicitly (§3.3).

## 2. HOW A FOREIGNER REACHES THEM INDEPENDENTLY

### 2.1 Route map (pick by what is being asked)

| Route | Use it for | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct written request to DAChO** (post or archive_cv@arch.gov.ua) | Anything pre-1946; a genealogical request is a paid archive service; also the free way to get a *written statement of what DAChO holds* | Slow; answer in Ukrainian; a shelf check (§3) is beyond a standard request — ask for it explicitly as a paid genealogical/thematic request |
| **Direct written request to Office A / Office B** (post; phone first) | Asking what books the DRACS side still holds, and for extracts from books still there | DRACS answers about its own holdings; for a >75-year record it should redirect to DAChO |
| **Diia / DRACS web portal** | Ordering a повторне свідоцтво (duplicate certificate) or витяг (extract) from the electronic Register | Requires Ukrainian e-signature/BankID — in practice **not usable by a US-based foreigner without Ukrainian banking/e-ID** (INFERRED from the Diia service conditions). Also useless for 1897: the electronic Register holds modern acts |
| **Ukrainian consulate** (vitreb uvannia dokumentiv — requesting documents from Ukraine) | A foreigner abroad asking DRACS/DAChO for a document through official channels | US State Department confirms consulates/MFA accept such requests and that **a third party such as a lawyer can apply with a valid power of attorney**: https://Travel.State.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/Visa-Reciprocity-and-Civil-Documents-by-Country/Ukraine.html . | **Appointed representative in Ukraine with a PoA** | Everything, including reading-room shelf checks | The PoA must be built correctly (§2.4) |

The Ministry's DRACS web portal (https://dracs.minjust.gov.ua) and Online House of Justice are described in the Ministry's own technological card for duplicate-certificate issuance: https://novagromada.gov.ua/sites/novagromada.gov.ua/files/inline-files/tekh.-z-pov.-vidachi-svidoctva.pdf

### 2.2 What documents are issued, and the kinship rule

- **Повторне свідоцтво** (duplicate certificate) — issued by any DRACS office regardless of where the act was registered, on the basis of the act record; state duty **0.51 UAH** (0.03 of the non-taxable minimum). **Витяг з Державного реєстру** (extract from the State Register) — fee **73 UAH**. Both figures from the Kyiv City Administration's official procedure page: https://shev.kyivcity.gov.ua/news/poryadok-vidachi-dokumentiv-pro-derzhavnu-reestratsiyu-aktiv-tsivilnogo-stanu ; underlying Ministry of Justice clarification: https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/en-sv/n0068323-11 . Paid DRACS counter services (drafting the application etc.) add roughly up to ~200 UAH total in recent secondary reporting (UNVERIFIED for Chernivtsi; the two statutory figures above are the proven core).
- **Kinship:** a person aged 16+ may obtain an extract about themselves **and about their relatives on presenting a passport and documents proving the family relationship** (same Kyiv official page and Diia guide). In practice the chain must be paper-complete: the applicant's birth certificate naming parents, then each linking birth/marriage certificate up the line, with name-change documents where surnames differ. For an act record **over 75 years old the archive route applies instead** — DAChO issues archival certificates (архівні довідки) and copies as a genealogical service, and the kinship/permission restriction in the State Archival Service FAQ is framed around the 75-year personal-data window (https://old.archives.gov.ua/Eng/q-a.php). Note the practical catch for 1897: an 1897 record, if found in a *book still at DRACS* beside later entries, may still be handled by DRACS staff under DRACS habits (kinship + PoA) even though its age puts it in archive territory — one more reason the representative should carry both the PoA **and** the kinship chain (INFERRED).
- **Language:** correspondence with Ukrainian state bodies is in **Ukrainian**. A request in English will usually still be registered, but expect the answer in Ukrainian and possible delay; file in Ukrainian (templates in §3). Under the Law on Citizens' Appeals a state body must answer within 30 days (general knowledge of the statute; not separately sourced in this pass — UNVERIFIED here).

### 2.3 Requesting through DAChO directly (the independent baseline — do this regardless)

DAChO confirms genealogical requests are accepted at **archive_cv@arch.gov.ua** and executed as a paid service (DAChO news, 09.05.2022: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/archive_news_2022.html ; procedure: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/poryadki.html). The State Archival Service's English guidance says genealogical requests to Ukrainian archives are priced per archive price list, historically fluctuating **200–2000 UAH** depending on complexity (old page, indicative only): https://old.archives.gov.ua/Eng/Requests/index.php . DAChO's current price list was not captured in this pass — ask for the рахунок (invoice) in the request itself.

### 2.4 Power of attorney (довіреність) for a representative in Ukraine — build it once, build it right

Why it is needed (PROVEN): DAChO requires a notarised PoA (or consent + passport copy) for a third party to search on the customer's behalf (§1.4); DRACS issues documents to representatives "за довіреністю" (per the official procedure pages above); the US State Department's Ukraine civil-documents page states a third party/lawyer may apply with a valid PoA.

Two ways to make a US-signed PoA valid in Ukraine:
1. **US notary + apostille (standard route).** Sign before a US notary public; obtain an **apostille from the Secretary of State of the state of notarisation** (for Florida: Florida Department of State) — both the US and Ukraine are Hague Apostille Convention parties, so no consular legalisation is then needed. Then, **in Ukraine**, get a **certified Ukrainian translation with the translator's signature notarised** (Ukrainian notaries certify translations under Art. 79 of the Law on the Notariate; a foreign document is accepted for this only with its apostille). Steps summarised from practitioner sources (secondary, consistent across sources): https://www.apoling.com/how-to-prepare-your-power-of-attorney-for-ukraine/
2. **At a Ukrainian consulate.** A PoA certified by a Ukrainian consul needs **no apostille and no translation** and is valid in Ukraine immediately (secondary: https://visitukraine.today/blog/1844/how-to-dispose-of-property-in-ukraine-while-abroad-drawing-up-a-power-of-attorney). Consular jurisdiction for Florida and current appointment rules must be checked on the MFA/consulate site before relying on this route (UNVERIFIED in this pass).

Scope wording — the PoA should name, in Ukrainian, at minimum (draft; have a Ukrainian notary or lawyer settle the final text):
- the representative's full name, passport details, address in Ukraine;
- the institutions: «Державний архів Чернівецької області; відділи державної реєстрації актів цивільного стану Чернівецької області та міста Вижниця; інші державні архіви та органи державної реєстрації актів цивільного стану України»;
- the powers: «подавати від мого імені запити, заяви та звернення; проводити пошук у читальних залах архівів; замовляти та отримувати архівні довідки, архівні копії та витягів, витяги з Державного реєстру актів цивільного стану громадян, повторні свідоцтва про державну реєстрацію актів цивільного стану; сплачувати державне мито, плату за послуги та інші обов'язкові платежі; підписувати необхідні документи; отримувати документи поштою та пересилати їх мені»;
- the subject matter: «щодо мене та моїх родичів, зокрема щодо Йосефа Бермана (Лергаупта), народженого 1897 року у місті Вижниця (Віжниця)…» — naming the target person and the kinship makes DRACS counters markedly less likely to refuse (INFERRED, practitioner logic);
- a validity term (e.g. 3 years) and the right of substitution (передовіра) only if wanted — omit substitution by default.

Practical notes: send the representative the **original** apostilled PoA (copies are routinely refused for document issuance); keep a scan; the representative should also carry the kinship-chain copies (§2.2) and a copy of the user's passport.

## 3. ON-GROUND RESEARCHER PLAN — the shelf check

**Operating principle.** Every task below asks for the **physical volume and its first and last entries**, not the inventory line. Fond/opis/file numbers are the *address*, not the *contents*. For each site: what to ask, the Ukrainian to ask it in, what a complete answer looks like, and what to photograph.

**Photograph at every site (standing list):** the inventory (опис) pages consulted; each volume's spine and title page; the first and last written pages of each volume opened; the certification inscription (засвідчувальний напис) at the end of each volume (it states the number of sheets and is signed/dated at binding — often the only place a volume's true span is written); the archive's written reply. Photography rules in Ukrainian reading rooms are set by the archive's Порядок (DAChO's current Порядок is Order № 65 of 27.12.2024, https://cv.archives.gov.ua/poryadki.html) — the researcher must confirm the current copying/photography fee and permission procedure on arrival (UNVERIFIED detail).

### 3.1 DAChO — Fond 1245, opis 1: the neighbour run around the Wiznitz Jewish volumes (files 933–971)

Known anchors (PROVEN from the FamilySearch catalogue, filmed at DAChO): file **933** = Wiznitz Jewish births "1918–1920"; **934** = marriages 1918–1923; **935** = births 1920–1922; **936** = births 1922–1925; **938** = deaths 1922–1930; **940** (cont.) marriages 1926–1929 — i.e. files **933–940** are the Wiznitz (& Waschkoutz) Jewish set (https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1461615). The user's own image inspection (from the parent dossier) found file 933's births in fact **begin in September 1917**, despite the 1918–1920 label — the single most important fact in this plan: the label understates the volume by months, so neighbouring volumes may equally over- or under-state theirs.

Tasks:
1. Read opis 1 entries for **files 925–975 in full** (not only the Wiznitz lines) and transcribe every title exactly.
2. Physically open, in this order: **941–944** (catalogued under Kitsman/Kіцмань; the parent dossier reports these contain Wiznitz entries — treat that report as a lead to verify, PROVEN only that the files exist in the same opis run), **937, 939** (the gaps inside the Wiznitz sequence), then **945–971**, including **960–961** (catalogued as Wiznitz Roman Catholic) and **971** (reported in the parent dossier as a standalone certificate file).
3. For every volume: record fond/opis/file as labelled; **actual** first entry (date, name, denomination/language) and **actual** last entry; whether the volume is a transcript (цивільний примірник) or an original; any inserted loose certificates.
4. Ask for the **описи of Fond 1245 in all its opysy (1, 2, 4, 9, 13, 14, 15)** filtered for Вижниця/Віжниця/Vijiniţa in any denomination — Fond 1245 is PROVEN multi-opis and multi-denomination (§4), so a Wiznitz item can sit under an opis the FamilySearch Wiznitz title never touched.

Ukrainian to use:
> «Прошу видати до читального залу справи фонду 1245, опис 1, №№ 925–975, а також показати опис 1 повністю.»

## 4. MISLABEL EVIDENCE — documented cases, and the methodological consequence

1. **FamilySearch 1378569 (Czernowitz, "Metrical books, 1856–1940").** The catalogue note bundles, under one title, civil transcripts of Jewish **births, marriages, deaths, voter lists, and member lists** for Czernowitz (Fond 1245 opis 15 + Fond 325 material per the parent dossier). One title, several record types and two fonds: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1378569 (PROVEN from the catalogue note).
2. **FamilySearch 1461615 (Wiznitz, "Metrical books, 1918–1930"), file 933.** Labelled "Births 1918–1920"; the user's own frame-by-frame inspection (parent dossier) shows the volume **begins September 1917**. The catalogue's start year is the binder's, not the book's (label PROVEN; the September 1917 start is the user's inspection finding, to be re-photographed under §3.1).
3. **Fond 1245 is not a Jewish fond, not a Czernowitz fond, and not one opis.** FamilySearch notes filmed at DAChO show, inside the same fond number: Jewish Czernowitz-district transcripts under **opis 15** (https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1378649); Orthodox books from **Ataki (Khotin/Bessarabia)** under **opis 13**; Roman Catholic Sadagora transcripts under **opis 14** — with a stray volume **1245-1/947 (births 1901)** filed inside the Sadagora RC set (https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1384439?availability=Family%20History%20Library). Any search keyed to one opis or one denomination label will miss material shelved under the others (PROVEN from the catalogue notes).
4. **DAChO's own metric-book index cross-lists the same books under two fonds.** DAChO's "Покажчик до фондів… про народження, шлюб та смерть" (as of 01.01.2007, reproduced at https://forum.vgd.ru/post/9716/3314/p2967739.htm) lists single parishes' books under both Fond 605 and Fond 1245 op. 13 — the archive's own reference apparatus preserves duplicate/conflicting addresses for the same physical series (PROVEN as reproduced; the underlying index is DAChO's).
5. **Location labels in secondary inventories go stale.** The Routes to Roots inventory (1999) placed the bulk of Czernowitz Jewish records at the **ZAGS (DRACS) archive**; by 2006 the reunion summary already corrected this to the oblast archive, while still listing births/marriages 1926–1929 at the ZAGS archive (https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/reunionposts/archives/chernivtsiarc.html). A "not at DAChO" answer and a "held by DRACS" answer can both be true — for different decades (PROVEN as a documented historical discrepancy).

**Methodological consequence, stated plainly:** in this archive system a catalogue line is a *claim about* a volume, made by whoever bound, filmed, or inventoried it, and the claim is demonstrably wrong in specific, checkable ways (wrong start year, wrong town attribution inside a set, one title covering two fonds, one book carrying two fond addresses, holdings migrating between DRACS and DAChO between inventories). Therefore (a) **absence from a catalogue proves absence from that catalogue, not from the shelf**; (b) every negative that matters must be a **physical** negative — a named person opened the neighbouring volumes and read their first and last entries; and (c) equally, a mislabel can hide a book in *either* direction — the 1897 Wiznitz births, if a volume survives at all, are most likely sitting under a label that says another town, another denomination, or a later start year. Loss is what the record currently proves; where the book went is still an open, checkable question.
