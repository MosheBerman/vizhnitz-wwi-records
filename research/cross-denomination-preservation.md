# Cross-Denomination Preservation and Differential Survival of Vital Records in Wiznitz/Vizhnitz, Bukovina, in WWI

Research date: 2026-11-04. Prepared for the Vizhnitz WWI records project.
Scope: (1) the Austrian Matriken system by denomination, (2) Wiznitz churches and clergy c.1914, (3) documented cases of a church or priest safeguarding Jewish records, scrolls, or property in WWI, (4) movement of registers themselves, (5) a practical repository checklist.

Starting facts, given from prior catalogue work and not re-derived here: for Wiznitz, Roman Catholic metrical books survive 1871-1913 (FamilySearch film 1461618, DAChO fond 1245-1/960-961), Orthodox 1840-1926, Greek Catholic 1876-1921, while Jewish metrical books survive only 1918-1930 (FamilySearch film 1461615). Czernowitz Jewish books by contrast survive for 1897 and surrounding years.

## Bottom line

- Church safeguarding of Jewish sacred property in WWI Bukovina is proven for one case: Czernowitz, 1914, where the Orthodox Metropolitan Archbishop Dr Vladimir von Repta stored the Czernowitz Temple's Torah scrolls in his residence and returned them after the war. Sources in section 3.
- No source found in this pass shows any church, priest, or consistory safeguarding Wiznitz's Jewish metrical registers, and no source found shows Jewish registers of any Bukovina town deposited in a church archive.
- The differential survival itself is a catalogue fact: Christian volumes for Wiznitz in DAChO fond 1245 run through the war years, while the Wiznitz Jewish volumes in the same fond (1245-1/933 to 940) begin in 1918. The mechanism of the Jewish loss is unproven.
- No documented WWI case was found of a parish register, of any denomination, being evacuated with a fleeing priest or deposited in a deanery or consistory for wartime safekeeping in Wiznitz, Bukovina, or Galicia. Documented register movements exist, but they belong to the peacetime duplicate system, to WWII, or to postwar transfers. See section 4.
- The likeliest documentary trail for a deposit of Jewish community property, if one happened, is DAChO fond 325 (Jewish Community of Chernivtsi), which holds inventories of synagogue property and correspondence with authorities, not a parish register fond.

## 1. The Austrian Matriken system by denomination

### 1.1 No civil registry before 1938

- Austria had no civil registration before 1938/39 (Burgenland from 1895). Before that, vital records were kept only by religious bodies: Roman Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed, Uniate (Greek Catholic), Orthodox, and Jewish. Source: Markus Schönherr, "How to Start Researching Your Ancestors in Today's Austria," FamilySearch syllabus, 2023-01-29: https://cms-b-assets.familysearch.org/78/83/8a514b92e2589dd7200aae05b8ef/how-to-start-researching-your-ancestors-in-todays-austria-2023-01-29-syllabus.pdf
- The Austrian Foreign Ministry (BMEIA) states the same in current guidance: until 1938 certificates were issued by local religious communities, and "in towns with small Jewish communities records were kept by the Roman Catholic Church"; files were also transferred to regional (for example diocesan) archives. Source: https://www.bmeia.gv.at/en/austrian-consulate-general-new-york/service-for-citizens/family-and-genealogical-research
- Note on applicability: Wiznitz had a large Jewish community (over 80 percent of the town c.1900, per local-history sources), so the small-community rule, under which a Catholic priest kept the Jewish book, probably does not apply to Wiznitz itself. This is an inference, marked as such.

### 1.2 Who kept what, and the Catholic supervision layer

- Until the Tolerance Act of 13 October 1781, Catholic priests kept parish records for all denominations. After 1781 each church kept its own books, but entries for other denominations still had to be made in the local Catholic parish records until 20 January 1849. Source: BYU / Family History Library, "Genealogical Registers in Austria": https://familyhistory.lib.byu.edu/00000192-d5ab-db7a-a9f2-ffabea320001/genealogicalregistersinaustria-pdf ; older outline: http://files.lib.byu.edu/family-history-library/research-outlines/Europe/Austria.pdf
- For Jews: a royal patent of 1 May 1781 required Jewish birth, marriage, and death registers, with a further decree of 23 July 1787. Examination and verification of the Jewish registers was the duty of the Catholic clergy, the district or local council, in part the police, and sometimes the military or the manor lord. Jewish entries were considered official only from 1 July 1868. Source: BYU outline, same URLs as above.
- Familia Austria (Christina Kaul, "Jüdische Matriken und Familiantenbücher," 2021-09-08) adds two concrete cross-denomination mechanisms: Jewish entries also occur inside Catholic church books (example given: Neugedein/Kdyně in Bohemia, to 1840), and Kontrollmatriken (control registers) were kept by Catholic priests to police the Familianten laws. Galicia was Austrian from 1772, Bukovina from 1774. Source: https://alt.familia-austria.at/images/inhalte/aktuell/Juedische_Matriken_und_Familiantenbuecher_Christina_Kaul_2021-09-08.pdf
- Galicia and Bukovina specifics (secondary, from Suzan Wynne's research as discussed on JewishGen and Gesher Galicia forums): Austrian law allowed Jews to maintain registers under Catholic supervision from 1789, and most Jewish registers date from the 1830s or later. Legislation of 1875 and regulations of 1877 created Jewish registration districts identical to the Kultusgemeinde (community) districts. Each district hired a government-approved registrar, in some cases a rabbi, paid from community taxes. Events had to be registered within 8 days. Registrar approval files survive on microfilm at CAHJP, Jerusalem. Before 1877, Jews in Galicia were required to register vital events with the Catholic diocese, with widespread noncompliance outside a few cities (Przemyśl, Tarnobrzeg, Lemberg, Stanisławów, Kraków). Sources: https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/topic/registration_of_vital_events/70334327 ; https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/message/171005 ; https://www.legacytree.com/blog/vital-records-austria-hungary

### 1.3 Copies and duplicates: where the second copy went

- The same BYU outline states that duplicates of records were sent to the Bezirkshauptmannschaft (district captaincy) or the city Magistrate, and that "the Israelites in Austria delivered their genealogical records to the Bezirkshauptmannschaft or Magistrate, where already earlier duplicates had to be submitted." Protestant copies to the Oberkirchenrat in Vienna were mandatory 1878-1917.
- The duplicate system is directly visible in a surviving collection: FamilySearch's "Austria, Evangelical-Lutheran Church Records, 1783-1991" consists of church book duplicates held at the Evangelischer Oberkirchenrat, Vienna, while the originals remain in the parishes; the duplicate order was given in 1848. Source: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/collection/1393793
- FamilySearch labels the surviving Bukovina volumes "civil transcripts" (German: Standesamtsbücher) of the parish and community registers, microfilmed from originals at the Chernivtsi State Archive. In other words, much of what DAChO fond 1245 holds is a transcript or copy set produced for the civil authority, not necessarily the parish originals. Example, Sadagora Roman Catholic, fond 1245-14/39: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1384439?availability=Family%20History%20Library
- Practical consequence for Wiznitz: a denomination whose books survive in DAChO fond 1245 may survive because its civil transcript set survived, even if a parish original did not, and the reverse is also possible. Survival in fond 1245 is evidence about the transcript chain, not direct evidence about what happened inside a church or synagogue building in 1914-1917. This is an inference from the catalogue descriptions, marked as such.

### 1.4 DAChO fond structure: the differential-survival baseline

- DAChO (State Archive of Chernivtsi Oblast) official fond list: fond 1245, "Books of acts of civil status (metrical books) of present-day Chernivtsi oblast," with opis 1 arranged by raion (opis 4 covers Vyzhnytskyi raion), opis 14 for Chernivtsi and Sadagora Roman Catholic, opis 15 for Chernivtsi and Sadagora Jewish, opis 17 for Chernivtsi; extreme dates vary, 1784-1946. Source: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/opusu/opusu12.html
- A DAChO-derived historical summary (reproduced at pomnirod.ru; treat as DAChO-derived secondary) gives the denominational spread: Orthodox metrical books sit in two fonds, fond 1245 (2,628 units, 1784-1952) and fond 605, the Kishinev (Chișinău) Spiritual Consistory, for Khotyn county only (174 units, 1784-1929). Greek Catholic books sit in fond 1245 only (18 units, 1820-1943). Jewish books sit in fond 1245 only (115 units, 1855-1941), with the Chernivtsi city Jewish books the most complete. The same summary lists Roman Catholic books in three fonds, one of them fond 987; the fond 987 name was cut off in the snippet and is unverified. Source: https://pomnirod.ru/materialy-k-statyam/administrativno-territorialnoe-delenie-rossijskoj-imperii/gubernii-rossii/kovenskaya-guberniya-kovno/metricheskie-knigi/chernigovskij-arhiv-mnogo-chego-otskaniroval-no-vylozhil-na-ftp-91.225.197.22/zrazok-podachi-informacii-do-rozdilu-istorichna-dovidka.html
- DAChO's own Orthodox index (as of 01.01.2007) lists, for the town of Vyzhnytsia, the Church of the Dormition of the Mother of God: births 1840-1889, marriages 1840-1904, deaths 1801-1915, fond 1245 opis 1 and opis 4. The Orthodox series therefore runs unbroken through the start of the war. Source: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/pdf/nashi_vidannya/pokazchik_fondiv.pdf
- The Wiznitz Jewish catalogue entry is confirmed at FamilySearch catalogue 1461615, "Metrical books, 1918-1930," microreproduction of originals at the Chernivtsi State Archive: volumes 1245-1/933 (births 1918-1920), /934 (marriages 1918-1923), /935 (births 1921-1922), /938 (deaths 1922-1930), /940 (marriages 1926-1929). Source: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1461615 . A JewishGen post by Yohanan Loeffler (2020) confirms record group 1245 series 1 files 933-940 only: https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/topic/75641796
- The Czernowitz contrast is confirmed at FamilySearch catalogue 1378569: Czernowitz Jewish births, marriages, and deaths 1856-1940, volumes 1245-15/1 onward; Czernowitz district Jewish births 1901-1923 at 1245-15/14. Sources: https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1378569 ; https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1378649
- Stray-entry lead: a JewishGen / Romania SIG post (Loeffler) reports that the Kitsman (Kotzman) Jewish metrical books 1902-1929 (1245-1/941-944) contain entries from many Bukovina communities, including Wiznitz. Isolated pre-1918 Wiznitz entries may sit inside a neighbouring district's book. Source: https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/topic/70524186
- Galician analogue, TsDIAL Lviv: the archive holds metrical books of the Roman Catholic (2,657 parish books, 1600-1948), Greek Catholic (7,421 books, 1776-1944), Evangelical, and Jewish denominations. Fond 701, the Lwów Jewish Religious Community, is the largest Jewish collection: synagogue registers of birth, marriage, divorce, and death from all of eastern Galicia, 1789-1942. Sources: http://inter-trans.biz/index.php?pg=511&m=1 ; Alexander Dunai in Avotaynu, Winter 2012: https://www.avotaynu.net/backissues/display.html?file=issues/2012_/2012WinterPage09Dunai.rlm&license=2012_

## 2. Wiznitz local institutions and clergy, c.1914

### 2.1 Greek Catholic parish of Ss Peter and Paul (best documented)

Source for this whole subsection, unless noted: UGCC Information Department, "Greek Catholics of Vyzhnytsia," part VII, by historian Andriy Melnychuk, 6 June 2024: https://ugcc.ua/data/ugkts-na-bukovyni-greko-katolyky-vyzhnytsi-chastyna-vii-4972/

- The town c.1914 had a Greek Catholic church, Orthodox churches, a Roman Catholic kostel, and three synagogues.
- The Greek Catholic community was officially founded in 1836 (imperial order of 1833). Before that, its faithful were served by priests from Kuty and Stari Kuty, in Galicia. The first pastor was Fr Ivan Khrystofelimes (28 April 1836 to 4 August 1843).
- Cross-denomination fact, documented for Wiznitz itself: the Greek Catholic and Roman Catholic communities at first shared one wooden chapel, a converted communal granary donated to both communities by the local landlord (didych) Dzhorzhovan. Two denominations sharing one sacred building in Wiznitz is therefore documented for the 19th century, though not for the war years.
- The Greek Catholic church of Ss Peter and Paul: a wooden church built under Fr Ivan Bilous (from 9 April 1869) burned; the masonry church was completed and consecrated in 1880 under Fr Longyn Kovblianskyi (parish priest 19 May 1876 to 26 March 1888).
- Fr Isydor Bobykevych (3 March 1888 to 15 March 1902) was a noted orator who, in the UGCC history's words, won the love, respect, and esteem of representatives of all confessions. Bishop Andrey Sheptytsky's visitation was welcomed in Vyzhnytsia on 2 June 1900.
- Successors: Fr Ivan Berezovskyi (12 April 1902 to 20 March 1907); Fr Mykola Hoyaniuk (20 March 1907 to 15 February 1909); Fr Ivan Lutskyi (16 February 1909 to 1914).
- In 1913 the parish moved from the Suceava deanery to the Chernivtsi deanery. On the eve of WWI the parish of Ss Peter and Paul had 1,415 faithful, over 300 of them in Vyzhnytsia itself, plus 16 daughter villages.
- Named gap: the UGCC history stops at 1914. Fr Lutskyi's successor, and the parish's fate in 1914-1917, are not given. Unknown.

### 2.2 Roman Catholic church (partly unverified)

- A Ukrainian local-history page states that the Roman Catholic kostel of Ss Peter and Paul in Vyzhnytsia was built in 1876 on the site of an older wooden church, at the expense of the Armenian-origin magnate Hryhorii Ayvas, consecrated in 1887, closed by the Soviet authority in 1946, and returned in the 1990s. Source: https://ukrainaincognita.com/chernivetska-oblast/vyzhnytskyi-raion/vyzhnytsya/vyzhnytsya
- A commercial tour site instead names a "Roman-Catholic church of St Anna" in the centre. The dedication (Ss Peter and Paul versus St Anna) is unresolved. Mark both as unverified secondary.
- No Roman Catholic clergy names for Vyzhnytsia c.1914 were found. Unknown.

### 2.3 Orthodox churches

- DAChO's index gives the town's Dormition of the Mother of God church, with the book runs listed in section 1.4. Source: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/pdf/nashi_vidannya/pokazchik_fondiv.pdf
- A tour page adds a wooden church of St Paraskeva standing from 1867 (moved to Bahna village in 1930), and a masonry St Nicholas (Mykolaivska) church begun in the early 20th century opposite the old Orthodox cemetery and consecrated only in 1924. Source: https://chernivtsi-tours.com.ua/vyzhnytsia-protyate-kaminnia&lang=en (commercial secondary; treat details as unverified).
- No Orthodox clergy names for Vyzhnytsia c.1914 were found. Unknown.

### 2.4 The Jewish institutions and the court's departure

- Dr N. M. Gelber's chapter on the Wiznitzer Tzadikim, in Hugo Gold (ed.), Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina, vol. I, pp. 89-90 (JewishGen translation): "When Wiznitz was occupied by the Russians during the First World War (1915) [date as given in that text], they allowed the Tzadik to leave the city with his entire court to go to Romania. So R. Israel Hager traveled through Romania to Hungary where he was directed by the government to live in Nagyvarad (Oradea). Even after the war, R. Israel remained in Oradea." He died in 1936. In Wiznitz he had kept a modest court, run by Jechiel Steinmetz with manager Nisen Just. Source: https://www.jewishgen.org/Yizkor/Bukowinabook/buk1_089.html
- R. Eliezer Hager, a son, spent WWI in Vienna. In 1922, when it was clear R. Israel would not return, Bukovina Jews asked for a son to be sent; Eliezer re-established the great yeshiva in Vizhnitz in 1923. Source (hagiographic, use with care): https://ganzach.org/mentions/for-the-sake-of-martyrs-thrown-into-the-fire-the-righteous-man-who-risked-his-life-to-thousands-of-transylvanian-refugees/
- Note the date tension and do not harmonise it silently: Gelber's translation dates the Russian occupation of Wiznitz to 1915, while Czernowitz was first entered by the Russians on 2 September 1914 (section 3.1). Both dates are reported here as their sources give them.
- Sefer Vizhnitz (the town's Yizkor book): unverified. Repeated JewishGen searches in this pass did not surface a dedicated JewishGen Yizkor translation for Vizhnitz itself; the Hugo Gold Bukovina volumes are a different work and are the source actually used here. It is not claimed that the Yizkor book lacks WWI or clergy content; no translated account from it was located in this pass.

### 2.5 A Bukovina model for a priest's wartime parish record

- Fr Kelestyn Kostetskyi, Greek Catholic parish priest of Chernivtsi 1884-1919 and dean of Bukovina, stayed with his parishioners and lived through all three Russian occupations of the capital. He left "Chronicles of the Greek Catholic parish in Chernivtsi," with diary entries for WWI. The manuscript is held by the Chernivtsi Regional Museum (kraieznavchyi muzei). Source: UGCC Synod, 13 December 2019: https://synod.ugcc.ua/data/chernivetski-hroniky-z-pershoy-svitovoy-viyny-ochyma-svyashchennyka-2081/
- This proves the genre existed in Bukovina: a priest's wartime parish chronicle, surviving in a local museum. Whether an equivalent Vyzhnytsia parish chronicle exists, for any denomination, is unknown. It is a checklist item in section 5.

## 3. Documented safeguarding cases, WWI

### 3.1 Proven: Czernowitz, 1914, Metropolitan Vladimir von Repta and the Temple Torah scrolls

- Hugo Gold (ed.), Geschichte der Juden in der Bukowina, vol. II, p. 28 (JewishGen translation): the Russians under Gen. Evreimow entered Czernowitz on 2 September 1914; a tribute demand was nullified the next day; "The protector of the Jews was the Archbishop Dr. von Repta, who had stored the Torah scrolls in his palace for safekeeping." Source: https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/bukowinabook/buk2_028.html
- The same passage gives the harm side, and both sides are reported here: in November 1914 the retreating Russians took hostages, including Mayor Dr Salo Weisselberger, Dr Mayer Ebner, and Dr Philipp Menczel; and "their Christian fellow citizens had hung holy pictures in their windows in order to direct the plundering Russian soldiers to the Jewish homes."
- Corroboration, Czernowitz-L (Ehpes) summary of Sternberg's Czernowitz history: on the Russian return on 26 November 1914, "most of the town and all buildings of Jewish worship are spared, thanks to the courageous intervention of Dr Vladimir von Repta, the Greek Orthodox Archbishop. He will even have the Tempel's Torah rolls removed into his residence, where he will protect them till the end of the war; he will then give them back to the Great Rabbi of Czernowitz, upon the return of the latter." The Austrians recaptured Czernowitz on 17 February 1915. Source: https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/czernowitz12/testfile2010-2/1319.html?
- Corroboration, scholarly: Fred Stambrook, "The Golden Age of the Jews of Bukovina, 1880-1914," Center for Austrian Studies Working Paper 03-2 (October 2003), citing H. Sternberg in Gold vol. II, p. 32: "when the Russians invaded in 1914, Archbishop Repta of the Orthodox Church safeguarded the Torah and other holy texts from the main Czernowitz synagogue." Source: https://jgaliciabukovina.net/sites/default/files/article_file/Fred%20Stambrook%2C%20The%20Golden%20Age%20of%20%20the%20Jews%20in%20Bukovina%201880-1914.pdf
- Corroboration, photo caption in Gold vol. I: "Archbishop Dr. Vladimir von Repta who rescued the Torah scrolls during World War I." Source: http://www.jewishgen.org/Yizkor/Bukowinabook/bu1P068a.html
- Corroboration, press study: Arnold-Nicholas-Louis Pescaru, "Reflection of the death of Metropolitan Vladimir de Repta in the contemporary press," Anadiss (2026): the April-May 1926 Cernăuți, Iași, and Bucharest press recalled that Repta "directly protected" the Jewish community of Chernivtsi during the war. Repta (born 25 December 1841) was named Archbishop of Cernăuți and Metropolitan of Bukovina and Dalmatia in 1902 and died 24 April 1926. Source: https://anadiss.usv.ro/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2026/08/III.03.-Pescaru-Arnold.pdf
- Pressure on Repta from the occupier (Austrian war press, propaganda; use with caution): the Kurier für Niederbayern of 6 November 1914 reported that the Russians repeatedly threatened Repta to force a pro-Russian pastoral letter, put him under house arrest with a guard, and quartered 200 venereally ill Russian soldiers in the Red Cross hospital in his residence. Source: https://dhz.uni-passau.de/kurier-fuer-niederbayern/1914/11/6.txt
- Scope limit, stated plainly: Repta protected Torah scrolls and holy texts, in the capital. No source found says he, or any churchman, protected metrical registers, and no source found extends the case to Wiznitz. Any Wiznitz application of this precedent is a hypothesis, not a finding.

### 3.2 Proven (lay, not clergy): Markowa, Galicia, 1914, a Catholic family hid a synagogue's Torah scrolls

- Kamil Kopera (historian, Pilecki Institute / Ulma Family Museum of Poles Saving Jews in Markowa), "Goldman family: people helped by Ulmas": Aaron Bezem (Weltz), recalling in the 1990s, said that in 1914 his family entrusted the Szylar family with the care of the Sefer Torah scrolls, "to save the priceless element of Jewish religious life from the Russians conquering the region of Galicia in World War One." The scrolls belonged to the Jewish house of worship in the Goldman family house in Markowa, and the Catholic Szylars hid them. Source: https://muzeumulmow.pl/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Golman-family_people-helped-by-Ulmas_7.09.2023.pdf

### 3.3 An-sky: a mixed clergy picture, known here only from secondary summaries (unverified at primary level)

- Secondary summaries of S. An-sky's wartime memoir (Khurbn Galitsiye / The Enemy at His Pleasure) report three things: (a) An-sky asked a priest to intervene with a Russian officer for eight Jews who had been libelled, arrested, and sentenced to hang, and the priest demurred or refused; (b) "in the Polish churches the priests stirred up the congregations" with incitement against Jews; (c) Christians put icons in their windows so that a house without an icon was marked as Jewish for destruction. Source (secondary blog summary quoting the memoir): https://laraballard.substack.com/p/the-challenge-of-reading-an-skys
- Better-attested An-sky material, via Gabriella Safran, Wandering Soul (Harvard University Press, 2010): at Tuchów on 7 February 1915 the synagogue was destroyed and prayer books lay in scraps on the floor; an old woman from Dębica said Russian soldiers opened the synagogue wall cabinets: "They took the Torah scrolls. They threw them under the horses' hooves."
- The memoir text itself was not opened in this pass. The three items in the first bullet stay labelled secondary and unverified.

### 3.4 Jewish-to-Jewish safeguarding, for contrast (not church safeguarding)

- During WWI the Jews of Lutsk, "under threat of war or expulsions," entrusted their relics to the ethnographer S. An-sky. In 1916 Ben-Tsion Rubshtein's Jewish Historical-Ethnographic Society expedition collected over 1,000 sacred objects, including nearly 500 Torah scrolls, from 25 shtetls. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutsk ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Ethnographic_Expedition

### 3.5 Not evidence: the Bukowsko story

- A RISU article recounts a descendant's recurring dream that her great-grandfather handed Torah scrolls to Christian priests for keeping in a church basement at Bukowsko (Bikofsk). She searched for corroboration and none is reported in the article. Label: unverified family tradition based on a dream. Not a documented case. Source: https://risu.ua/en/torah-and-churches-of-lemkivshchyna-historical-memory-drama_n74825

### 3.6 Harm-side Galician data point

- Wielkie Oczy: in 1910, peasants "incited by a Ukrainian priest" maimed Jewish residents and destroyed Jewish property. Pre-war, from the town's Yizkor-derived history. Source: http://www.shtetlinks.jewishgen.org/WielkieOczy/history_wielkie_oczy.htm

### 3.7 WWII cases, labelled WWII, not WWI

- Metropolitan Andrey Sheptytsky (Greek Catholic, Lviv), 1941-1942: hid Jews in Greek Catholic monasteries and convents and in his own residence, supplied false baptismal papers, and preserved Torah scrolls. Kurt Lewin, son of Lviv chief rabbi Ezekiel Lewin (killed 1941), recalled in his memoir A Journey Through Illusions (1994) Sheptytsky saying of his father's manuscripts: "Bring with you the manuscripts and I will place them in a safe place." Estimates of Jews saved run to 150-200 or more. Sources: https://risu.ua/en/honoring-sheptytsky-s-courage-seven-decades-later_n65686/amp ; https://theamericanscholar.org/in-the-presence-of-people-no-longer-here/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Sheptytsky
- USHMM oral history, Leon Chameides (irn507302): in August 1942 his father, Rabbi Kalman Chameides, met Sheptytsky "as part of an effort to hide Torah scrolls"; Sheptytsky agreed to hide Leon and his brother in a Uniate monastery. Source: https://collections.ushmm.org/search/catalog/irn507302
- Relevance to Wiznitz is institutional only: Sheptytsky was the Greek Catholic metropolitan whose church included the Bukovina Greek Catholic parishes, and he personally visited Vyzhnytsia in 1900 (section 2.1). No WWI-era or WWII-era Sheptytsky safeguarding act in Wiznitz was found.

## 4. Movement of registers themselves (added angle: "records covering movement")

Question asked: are there documented cases of parish registers evacuated with a fleeing priest, deposited in a deanery or consistory for safekeeping during an occupation, and later returned, with repository and fond?

### 4.1 WWI answer: none found

- **None found for WWI.** Searches in English, German, and Ukrainian (terms including "Kirchenbücher/Matriken evakuiert," "Pfarrer Flucht," "евакуація метричних книг," "parish registers evacuated Galicia") produced no documented case, for Wiznitz or for any comparable Galicia or Bukovina town, of a metrical register of any denomination being carried out by a fleeing priest, deposited in a deanery or consistory during an occupation, or returned after one, in 1914-1918. This is stated explicitly as a negative result of this pass, not as proof that no such case exists in unindexed archival files.
- The closest WWI institutional fact found concerns the archive, not the registers: DAChO's own published history states that "with the beginning of World War I the archives actually ceased their activity, as Bukovyna became a battle-field," and that the Bukovina regional archive was liquidated at the beginning of November 1918 with the other Austrian state establishments. No evacuation of its holdings in WWI is described. Source: DAChO history booklet (State Chernivtsi Region Archives, "Documentary Treasury of Bukovyna"): https://old.archives.gov.ua/Eng/Publicat/Chernivci_booklet.pdf
- The closest WWI movement of a custodian is R. Israel Hager leaving Wiznitz with his entire court for Romania (section 2.4). No source found says the court took registers with it, and Gelber's account does not mention records at all. Do not infer a register removal from the court's departure.
- The closest WWI movement of sacred objects into safekeeping is the Repta deposit (section 3.1) and the An-sky expedition's collection of scrolls (section 3.4). Both concern Torah scrolls and ritual objects, not metrical books.

### 4.2 The one register system built to move: Austrian military church books

- The Habsburg army ran a parallel register system, the Militärmatriken, kept by army chaplains for regiments, garrisons, military hospitals, and field hospitals. These books moved with the unit, in effect a mobile parish, and recorded baptisms, marriages, and deaths of soldiers and their families. The primary repository today is the Kriegsarchiv, Vienna (Austrian State Archives); many volumes are digitised via FamilySearch. Source (secondary guide): https://galicia-gen.com/kirchenbucher-of-the-austrian-military-in-the-19th-century/
- Relevance: this is the only Austrian register system documented as travelling with its keeper. It covers soldiers, not the civilian Wiznitz community, and no Wiznitz civilian register is known to have entered it. Included here so the "registers that moved" category is not left empty; it is not a Wiznitz lead.

### 4.3 Documented register movements, other eras (each labelled)

- **The peacetime duplicate flow (Austrian period, to 1918).** The largest documented movement of registers is the routine one described in section 1.3: parish and community originals stayed in place, while duplicates and civil transcripts moved upward, to the Bezirkshauptmannschaft or Magistrate (Jewish and general records), to the Evangelischer Oberkirchenrat in Vienna (Protestant, mandatory 1878-1917), and under Catholic supervision and verification for Jewish books. The DAChO fond 1245 volumes are the surviving end of that flow. Repositories today: DAChO fond 1245; Evangelischer Oberkirchenrat, Vienna (via FamilySearch collection 1393793).
- **WWII, Bukovina (label: WWII).** The Archives of Jewish Bukovina and Transylvania project (Leo Baeck Institute) states, of a Czernowitz secondary school's records: "During World War II many documents from northern Bukovina were 'evacuated' to southern Bukovina or elsewhere in Romania. This evacuation of documents was often incomplete and many documents have gone missing." The school's records were probably evacuated to Suceava after the Soviet takeover of Cernăuți in 1940 and are now at the National Archives, Suceava branch, fond 467, inventory 360. Source: https://jbat.lbi.org/items/show/1636
- **WWII, Bukovina (label: WWII).** A Ukrainian scholarly study of Ukrainian archives in WWII (Ihor Datskiv) records that, at the Romanian withdrawal from Chernivtsi, the whole documentary material of the Chernivtsi oblast archive was packed in bundles and boxes for evacuation; the evacuation into the Romanian interior was not completed, but valuable fonds (primării, gendarmerie, siguranță, tribunal) were removed, the tribunal to Transylvania and the rest to Iași and Bucharest, and a significant quantity of material from the border districts of northern Bukovina was taken to Suceava, Rădăuți, and Câmpulung. This is the documented route by which northern Bukovina paper, in general, reached Suceava. Whether any metrical books were in those consignments is not stated in the source. Source: https://journals.tnpu.ternopil.ua/index.php/history/article/download/2029/1823/3657
- **Post-WWII, Galicia (label: post-1945).** Gesher Galicia's inventory history of AGAD, Warsaw: in the early 1950s a large consignment of archival material arrived in Warsaw from the eastern parts of interwar Poland, by then in the Soviet Union. It included vital (metrical) records and community records of the Roman Catholic, Greek Catholic, Jewish, Protestant, and Orthodox communities, from the Lwów, Stanisławów, and Tarnopol voivodeships among others. The Jewish element was some 5,500 sets of vital records plus index books and community material; the material was stored at the Ministry of Information in Warsaw for about 30 years, and from the early 1980s the books over 100 years old went to AGAD (now fond 300, about 1,750 Jewish bound volumes), the rest to the Warsaw Civil Registration Office (USC), with annual transfers from the USC to AGAD since. Source: https://www.geshergalicia.org/search/census/
- **Post-WWII, Galicia (label: post-1945, secondary).** Genealogy guides likewise state that some Roman Catholic books were evacuated from Lwów after WWII and are now at AGAD, Warsaw. Sources (secondary): https://www.nasiprzodkowie.com/eastern-borderlands/genealogy-research-in-lviv-lwow/ ; https://medium.com/@parishandstone/polish-ancestors-from-galicia-where-their-records-are-today-20a6bedbfcbf
- **Modern transfer notes (label: 2007-2020).** Two documented recent movements show registers still travelling between civil registry offices and state archives: (a) TsDIAL fond 701 holds 377 Galician Jewish registers of 1789-1942 plus 35 registers moved to the archive from civil registry offices in 2007-2010 (Dunai, Avotaynu, Winter 2012: https://www.avotaynu.net/backissues/display.html?file=issues/2012_/2012WinterPage09Dunai.rlm&license=2012_ ); (b) DAChO reported in December 2020 that it was continuing to accept metrical books from the civil registration offices, most recently books of the Zastavna and Vyzhnytsia raion registration offices. A Vyzhnytsia raion transfer of metrical books to DAChO in 2020 is therefore documented, though the notice does not itemise which books. Source: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/archive_news_2020.html
- **Dispersed copies, Bukovina (secondary, unverified in detail).** A Bukovina Society study of the Roman Catholic parish of Sereth (Siret) reports its metrical books and copies dispersed across four repositories: the Zentralstelle für Genealogie (now Staatsarchiv Leipzig), the Suceava branch of the Romanian National Archives, the Chernivtsi state archive, and copies said to be at the Roman Catholic diocese of Iași, with restricted access. Source: https://bukovinasociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/BSA.PDF_Newsletter_2020-Vol.30-No.1-Mar.pdf . Included as a pattern example of one parish's books ending in several countries, not as a verified inventory.

### 4.4 What the movement evidence does and does not do for Wiznitz

- It does establish that Bukovina and Galician registers demonstrably moved, in bulk, in WWII and after, and that Suceava is a documented destination for northern Bukovina records (WWII evacuations) and Iași and Bucharest for archive fonds. A Wiznitz book displaced in a later war could therefore plausibly sit in Suceava without any WWI event being involved.
- It does not provide any WWI mechanism. For the specific question, why the Wiznitz Jewish books begin in 1918 while the Christian transcript series run through the war, no movement record of any kind was found: no evacuation note, no deposit note, no return note, no transfer list. The gap's cause stays unknown.

## 5. Practical checklist: where a church-safeguarded or duplicate Wiznitz Jewish register could plausibly sit today

Ordered by plausibility for this specific question. "Why" states the mechanism, not a claim that the book is there.

1. **DAChO, fond 1245, opis 1 and opis 4 (Vyzhnytskyi raion), and opis 15 (Jewish).** Why: the known Wiznitz Jewish volumes are 1245-1/933-940; a stray volume, a misfiled duplicate, or a neighbouring district book containing Wiznitz entries (the Kitsman 1245-1/941-944 precedent) is the single most likely form a survival would take. Also re-check opis 14 (Roman Catholic) and the fond 987 Roman Catholic holding, because pre-1877 practice and Catholic supervision put Jewish material inside Catholic series elsewhere in the Monarchy. Links: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/opusu/opusu12.html ; https://www.familysearch.org/en/search/catalog/1461615
2. **DAChO, fond 325, Jewish Community of Chernivtsi, opis 1-4, 1780-1944.** Why: the fond holds correspondence with authorities, budgets, and inventories of synagogue property for the Bukovina community administration. If Torah scrolls, registers, or other community property were deposited, removed, or reported lost, this is the fond most likely to record it. It is digitised: 51,165 images at USHMM (irn50122) via the Claims Conference IHDA. Links: https://portal.ehri-project.eu/units/us-005578-irn50122 ; https://collections.ushmm.org/search/catalog/irn50122
3. **The Czernowitz Orthodox consistory / Metropolitan's chancellery records.** Why: the Repta precedent (section 3.1) makes the Orthodox Metropolitan's residence the one church address in Bukovina documented as receiving Jewish sacred property for wartime safekeeping. A deposit receipt, if one existed for any Bukovina community, would be in the Metropolitan's chancellery correspondence. Caution: the DAChO fond number for the Bukovina Orthodox consistory was not verified in this pass. Do not cite a fond number until DAChO's fond list confirms it. Fond list: https://cv.archives.gov.ua/opusu/opusu12.html
4. **Chernivtsi Regional Museum (kraieznavchyi muzei).** Why: it holds Fr Kostetskyi's Czernowitz Greek Catholic parish chronicle with WWI diary entries (section 2.5). Parish chronicles and un-transferred parish material from Vyzhnytsia, of any denomination, are the kind of holding a regional museum accumulates. Ask specifically after Vyzhnytsia parish chronicles and any WWI deposit records.
5. **Greek Catholic consistory records for the Vyzhnytsia parish (Stanislaviv eparchy structures; DAIFO Ivano-Frankivsk and TsDIAL Lviv).** Why: the Vyzhnytsia Greek Catholic parish of Ss Peter and Paul, under Fr Ivan Lutskyi to 1914, reported upward through deanery and consistory (Chernivtsi deanery from 1913; the parish had earlier Galician links, served from Kuty). Visitation reports and parish inventories, which list church property, run through this channel. The UGCC parish history is the entry point: https://ugcc.ua/data/ugkts-na-bukovyni-greko-katolyky-vyzhnytsi-chastyna-vii-4972/
6. **Lviv Roman Catholic archdiocesan archive.** Why: Bukovina Roman Catholic parishes stood under the Lviv archdiocese, and the duplicate flow for Catholic books ran to the diocese (section 1.3). The Wiznitz kostel's duplicate set and any parish correspondence about wartime custody would sit here or at AGAD (point 8).
7. **The Vyzhnytsia parish offices themselves.** Why: in the Austrian system the originals stayed in the parish (section 1.3), and many Galician parish books never left the parish office. The Roman Catholic parish (returned to its church in the 1990s), the Greek Catholic parish of Ss Peter and Paul, and the Orthodox Dormition and St Nicholas parishes are direct enquiry targets for un-transferred originals and parish chronicles.
8. **AGAD, Warsaw, fond 300 (Jewish) and the AGAD church fonds.** Why: the early-1950s consignment (section 4.3) moved Galician Jewish, Roman Catholic, Greek Catholic, Protestant, and Orthodox metrical books to Warsaw. Bukovina was not part of interwar Poland, so a Wiznitz book here is unlikely, but the fond is the documented destination for displaced Galician registers and is the comparison set for differential survival. Link: https://www.geshergalicia.org/search/census/
9. **National Archives, Suceava branch (Romania), including fond 467-type holdings.** Why: Suceava is the documented WWII evacuation destination for northern Bukovina documents (section 4.3), and it holds the south Bukovina metrical collections catalogued by the Archives of Jewish Bukovina and Transylvania project. A Wiznitz volume displaced in 1940-1944, rather than 1914-1918, would most plausibly be here. Link: https://jbat.lbi.org/items/show/1636
10. **CAHJP, Jerusalem.** Why: CAHJP holds microfilms of Czernowitz Jewish community records and the Galician Jewish registrar-approval files (section 1.2), and its index-card catalogue is searchable through the Jewish Galicia and Bukovina association. A Wiznitz-specific CAHJP search has not been done in this pass; it is an open step, not a negative. Link: https://www.jgaliciabukovina.net/node/134889?page=742&order=field_card_code&sort=desc
11. **TsDIAL, Lviv, fond 701 and the consistory fonds.** Why: the Galician analogue and duplicate-practice comparison set, and the archive for the Kuty side of Wiznitz's Greek Catholic history. Bukovina proper is DAChO, not TsDIAL; TsDIAL is listed for comparison and for the Galician cross-border link, not as a likely Wiznitz Jewish register location. Link: https://www.avotaynu.net/backissues/display.html?file=issues/2012_/2012WinterPage09Dunai.rlm&license=2012_
12. **Routes to Roots (Miriam Weiner) town inventory.** Why: the standard town-by-town inventory of Jewish records in the Chernivtsi archives (for Czernowitz: births 1841, 1855-1860, 1860-1930; Sadgura births 1900, 1921-22; Kahal records 1780-1944). Query it specifically for "Vyzhnytsia / Wiznitz" as an open step before concluding the DAChO picture is complete. Links: http://www.kehilalinks.jewishgen.org/sadgura/ReischToronto.html ; https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/reunionposts/archives/chernivtsiarc.html
13. **Kriegsarchiv, Vienna (for completeness only).** Why: the Militärmatriken (section 4.2) are the registers that travelled. Relevant only if a Wiznitz event involved a soldier or a military hospital, not for the community register itself.

Context source, not a safeguarding lead: the JDC Archives hold the WAG (Wiederaufbaugesellschaft für die jüdische Bevölkerung der Bukowina, Czernowitz) reconstruction report, October 1920 to November 1921 (JDC New York Office files 427-432), a post-war Bukovina Jewish reconstruction source: https://archives.jdc.org/our-collections/finding-aids/new-york-office/1921-1932/countries-and-regions/

## 6. Proven / Inferred / Unknown

### Proven (a cited source states it)

- Austria had no civil registration before 1938/39; religious bodies kept the vital records, and duplicates flowed to the Bezirkshauptmannschaft or Magistrate, with Catholic clergy verifying Jewish registers and Jewish entries official only from 1 July 1868. (Schönherr syllabus; BYU outline; BMEIA.)
- The surviving Bukovina volumes at DAChO are labelled civil transcripts of the parish and community registers, and DAChO fond 1245 holds the Wiznitz Jewish books only at 1245-1/933-940, dated 1918-1930, while the Vyzhnytsia Orthodox Dormition series in the same fond runs 1801-1915 and beyond. (FamilySearch catalogues 1461615, 1378569, 1384439; DAChO fond list and Orthodox index.)
- Czernowitz, 1914: Metropolitan Vladimir von Repta stored the Czernowitz Temple's Torah scrolls in his residence for the war's duration and returned them; Christian householders in the same city marked Jewish homes for plunderers with holy pictures. Both halves are in the same Gold vol. II passage and are corroborated separately. (Gold vol. II p. 28; Ehpes/Sternberg; Stambrook 2003; Pescaru 2026.)
- Markowa, Galicia, 1914: the Catholic Szylar family hid the Torah scrolls of the Goldman family house of worship at the Jewish family's request. (Kopera, Ulma Family Museum.)
- Wiznitz c.1914 had a Greek Catholic church (Ss Peter and Paul, masonry, consecrated 1880), Orthodox churches, a Roman Catholic kostel, and three synagogues; the Greek Catholic pastor from 1909 to 1914 was Fr Ivan Lutskyi; the Greek Catholic and Roman Catholic communities had once shared a donated granary chapel. (UGCC history, 2024.)
- R. Israel Hager left Russian-occupied Wiznitz with his court, by Russian permission, via Romania to Nagyvarad, and did not return. (Gelber in Gold vol. I.)
- DAChO's published history: the Bukovina regional archive ceased activity at the start of WWI and was liquidated in November 1918; no WWI evacuation of its holdings is described. (DAChO booklet.)
- Register movements in other eras: the AGAD early-1950s consignment of Galician metrical books of all denominations; WWII evacuations of northern Bukovina documents to Suceava, Iași, and Bucharest; 35 Jewish registers moved from civil registry offices to TsDIAL in 2007-2010; metrical books transferred from the Zastavna and Vyzhnytsia raion registration offices to DAChO in December 2020. (Gesher Galicia; JBAT/LBI; Datskiv; Dunai; DAChO news 2020.)

### Inferred (reasoned from the proven material, not directly sourced)

- The small-community rule (a Catholic priest keeping the Jewish book) probably does not explain Wiznitz, because Wiznitz's Jewish community was large. It may explain smaller Bukovina villages and is worth checking for the district villages whose entries appear in the Wiznitz district books.
- Survival in DAChO fond 1245 tracks the civil transcript chain. The Christian series surviving through 1914-1918 and the Jewish series starting in 1918 may reflect a break in the Jewish transcript or deposit chain rather than, or as well as, destruction of an original book. The evidence does not distinguish these.
- If a church deposit of Wiznitz Jewish property happened on the Repta model, the paper trail would most plausibly be in DAChO fond 325 (synagogue property inventories, community correspondence) or in the Orthodox Metropolitan's chancellery correspondence, not in a metrical fond.
- A Wiznitz book found in Suceava in future would more likely have arrived in the documented WWII evacuations than in any WWI event.

### Unknown (searched, not found)

- Any WWI case, anywhere on the Eastern Front, of a church or priest safeguarding Jewish metrical registers specifically. Scrolls and sacred objects are documented (Czernowitz, Markowa, the An-sky collections); registers are not.
- Any WWI case of a parish register of any denomination evacuated with a fleeing priest, deposited in a deanery or consistory for the duration of an occupation, and returned. Explicitly none found in this pass.
- The fate of Wiznitz's three synagogues, its kostel, and its Orthodox and Greek Catholic churches in 1914-1917, and the names of its Orthodox and Roman Catholic clergy c.1914. Fr Lutskyi's successor is also unknown; the UGCC parish history stops at 1914.
- Whether Sefer Vizhnitz, the town Yizkor book, contains a WWI account of clergy, neighbours, scrolls, or records. No JewishGen translation for Vizhnitz was located in this pass; absence of a translation is not absence of a book.
- The An-sky priest episodes (the refused intervention, the incitement sermons) at primary-text level. Known here only from a secondary summary; the memoir was not opened.
- The DAChO fond number and name for the Bukovina Orthodox consistory, and the full name of DAChO fond 987 (Roman Catholic). Both must be verified against DAChO's fond list before use.
- The dedication of the Wiznitz Roman Catholic church: Ss Peter and Paul (local-history page) versus St Anna (tour operator). Unresolved.
- The correct year of the first Russian occupation of Wiznitz as it affected the Jewish community: Gelber's translation says 1915; the Czernowitz chronology begins September 1914. Left as a source conflict, not harmonised.
