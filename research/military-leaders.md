# Military leaders in Bukovina / the Cheremosh–Prut sector, WWI
## Personalities and documented policies toward Jews, civilians, looting and records — with Eastern Front comparators

Compiled 2026-11-04. Parallel track to the Vizhnitz (Wiznitz / Vyzhnytsia) WWI records project.

**Method and honesty rules used throughout**

- Every profile separates four things the brief asked for: (1) command, sector, dates; (2) *documented* orders/attitudes; (3) one specific dated event in a town under that command; (4) a named historian's assessment.
- **"Unverified" means exactly that:** no document was found in this pass tying that man personally to an order about Jews, looting, billeting or archives. Army-level or Stavka/AOK-level policy operating *inside* his sector is labelled as such, and is **not** converted into a personal attitude by reputation, ethnicity, later Civil War record, or post-1918 politics. That distinction matters most for Lechitsky, Kaledin, Shcherbachev, Kövess/Křitek and Hoffmann.
- Two premises in the original brief needed correction on the evidence, and are corrected in the profiles rather than silently followed:
  1. **Arthur Arz von Straussenburg was not a Bukovina / 7th Army commander.** He commanded VI Corps, then the (re-formed) **1st Army in Romania, 16 Aug 1916 – Feb 1917**, and then succeeded Conrad as **Chief of the General Staff, 1 Mar 1917 – 3 Nov 1918**. The Austro-Hungarian **7th Army in Bukovina** was commanded in succession by Pflanzer-Baltin → Kirchbach → Kövess → Křitek (see §10). Source: Arz biography https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Arz_von_Strau%C3%9Fenburg ; 7th Army commander list https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Army_(Austria-Hungary)
  2. **Max Hoffmann was not the Ober Ost policy-maker in 1914–15.** He was the 8th Army / Ober Ost operations chief under Hindenburg–Ludendorff, and only **succeeded Ludendorff as Ober Ost Chief of Staff in August 1916**, after Ludendorff left for OHL. Ober Ost civilian policy 1915–16 is therefore Ludendorff's system, with Hoffmann as its staff executor, not its author. Source: Ober Ost chiefs of staff list https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberost
- Dates are Gregorian (New Style) unless marked O.S. Russian orders quoted from secondary literature that itself cites RGVIA (Russian State Military History Archive) are flagged with the archival reference given by that literature, so they can be re-checked at source.

## 1. Nikolai Yanushkevich (1868–1918) — Chief of Staff, Stavka, Aug 1914 – Sept 1915

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Chief of Staff to the Supreme Commander (Grand Duke Nikolai Nikolaevich) at Stavka from the outbreak of war until both men were removed when the Tsar assumed supreme command on 5 Sept 1915 (23 Aug O.S.). Stavka held empire-wide authority over the front zone, including Bukovina when Russian forces occupied it; its civilian-affairs machinery (a special Military-Political and Civilian Administration section, set up Oct 1914) explicitly included "the limitation of enemy subjects."
- Lohr, *Nationalizing the Russian Empire* (Harvard UP, 2003), excerpt: https://www.scribd.com/doc/213293249/Russian-Empire-Nationalized
- Removal date in the Great Retreat chronology: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Retreat_(Russian)

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes — this is the best-documented personal record of any man in this report
- **Personal obsession with treason, attested by ministers, not just by later historians.** Lohr: "Several members of the Council of Ministers claimed that he was obsessed with the idea of treason, and his actions and his correspondence alike reveal his belief that Jews, Germans, and enemy subjects were all traitors." Yanushkevich ordered army and civilian officials to "most vigilantly root out spying among enemy subjects, Russian-subject Germans, and Jews," and offered cash rewards for proof of hidden telephones, radio stations, signals and secret airstrips. Lohr excerpt, above.
- **25 January 1915 circular** — the foundational expulsion order. Eric Lohr, "The Russian Army and the Jews: Mass Deportation, Hostages, and Violence during World War I," *Russian Review* 60/3 (2001) 404–419: Yanushkevich sent a circular to army commanders throughout the zone of military activity authorising expulsion (*vyselenie*) of "all Jews and suspect individuals" from the entire region where troops were present, and in follow-ups made clear he wanted entire Jewish communities deported whenever any individual Jew was suspected of spying; Stavka reiterated the blanket approval and "did not retract it until 1917." Full text as reproduced: https://doczz.net/doc/8898295/the-russian-army-and-the-jews--mass-deportation--hostages...
- **January 1915 Stavka declaration on "the Jews' patent hostility."** Semion Goldin, *The Russian Army and the Jewish Population, 1914–1917* (Palgrave, 2022), conclusion chapter: Peter Holquist's account shows the declaration grew from a low-ranking Foreign Ministry memorandum (V. Murav'ev) about "the hostility of the Jews of Galicia and Bukovina" toward the Russian army, approved personally by Nicholas II, to which Yanushkevich added the resolution **"it would be very desirable to drive all the Jews to the Fritzes"** — i.e. push them westward onto the enemy. Goldin also quotes Yanushkevich, Dec 1914: **"Repressions will discipline the Jewish population, will force them to feel the resoluteness of Russian rule."** https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-99788-5_9
- **Phasing (Lohr, summarised by Motta).** Phase 1, July 1914–Jan 1915: chaotic local deportations. Phase 2: Yanushkevich's 25 Jan 1915 decree organises expulsion from the whole military zone. Phase 3, Apr–May 1915: larger, train-organised mass deportations to pre-assigned destinations. Phase 4: deportation partly replaced by systematic **hostage-taking**, because deporting whole populations proved cumbersome and drew civilian protests — communities could return home on condition hostages were taken from among them. G. Motta, "The Great War and the Jewish Refugees in Russia," in *Unknown Fronts* volume: https://iris.uniroma1.it/bitstream/11573/927614/1/Motta_Great-War-Jewish-Refugees_2017.pdf
- **5 March 1915 clarifying directive (quoted verbatim in the Carpathian Front literature):** "The Jewish population, without distinction of sex and age, in the combat area should be evicted towards the enemy. The areas occupied by the rear units of the army must be cleared of all suspicious and unreliable persons. Regardless of this, in the last places, hostages should be taken from among those who enjoy influence." **27 March 1915 telegram** then rebukes the opposite practice — deporting Galician Jews *eastward* into Russia: "It was ordered to drive them forward behind the Austrians, and in the areas in the rear… to select from the most wealthy and important… hostages, who should be evicted to Russia." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front — *caveat: this page reproduces the order texts with footnotes to the Russian literature; re-verify wording against Goldin/Lohr before quoting in print.*
- **Scale.** Goldin, 1914-1918-Online encyclopedia entry: "From 1914 to 1916 at least 189 Jewish communities in the Russian Empire, Galicia and Bukovina suffered from deportations and expulsions," and robbery/looting "were typical for the whole front line area; pogroms were characteristic of Russian-occupied Galicia from September to November 1914." https://appwritefunc.yet-another-testing-domain.com/api/grab?url=https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/antisemitism-and-pogromsin-the-military-russian-empire/?format=pdf (DOI 10.15463/ie1418.10283)

### (3) Specific dated event in a town under his authority
**4 February 1915 — Bukovina order of the Supreme Commander, executed through Stavka.** Grand Duke Nikolai Nikolaevich ordered, for Bukovina specifically: "Starting from Bukovina, the Jews should be evicted following the retreating enemy and hostages should be taken from among the most prosperous and occupying public or other positions of the Jews." The hostages were transported under guard to Kyiv province. This is the single most directly Bukovina-specific high-command order found in this pass, and Vizhnitz/Wiznitz lay inside the zone it named. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front (see caveat above on re-verification).

### (4) Historian assessment
- **Eric Lohr:** Yanushkevich was a "major force in the campaigns against spying and enemy aliens"; Stavka under him gave commanders "nearly unlimited authority to deport suspect individuals or entire groups without trial," and as early as Sept 1914 "army commanders tolerated the participation of soldiers in pogroms, looting, and rape of Jews… The army rarely intervened or punished participants." Lohr excerpt, Scribd link above.
- **Alexander Watson, *The Fortress* (2019), as quoted in review:** Yanushkevich was "a particularly vicious and obsessive anti-Semite, and he sat at the center of the occupation regime's anti-Jewish policy… To remove Galicia's Jews from the protection of international laws, he cynically proposed that all be compulsorily given Russian citizenship" so that robbery of them would be "legalised" as action against Russian subjects in a war zone. Quoted at https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/43805737-the-fortress — *flag: secondhand quotation of Watson pp. 167–168; check the book before citing the page in print.*
- **Goldin (2022):** the command's hostility is explained by Russian nationalism casting Jews as the ultimate "Other," compounded by the army blaming Jews for its own failures. Springer chapter, above.

### Records / archives
No order by Yanushkevich about *archives or registers as such* was found. **Unverified** as a documents policy. The mechanism that destroyed record-keeping was indirect and is documented: expel the registrars, take the communal notables hostage, ban Yiddish publication/correspondence (Feb 1915, see §3 Bobrinsky administration), and the register stops — whether or not anyone orders the book itself seized.

## 2. Nikolai Ivanov (1851–1919) — Commander, Southwestern Front, Aug 1914 – Mar 1916

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Commander of the Southwestern Front — the army group that invaded Galicia and Bukovina in 1914, held them, lost them in 1915, and held the line until Brusilov replaced him in March 1916 (Brusilov took over 4 Apr 1916 in some chronologies; the appointment was late March). His front HQ proposed the Carpathian offensive plan of Jan 1915. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front ; Brusilov succession https://sunnycv.com/steve/ww1/1916b.html — *the second is a hobbyist chronology; use only for the sequence, not as authority.*

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **28 January 1915 circular to his army commanders — three days after Yanushkevich's.** Lohr (2001), citing **RGVIA f. 1932, op. 12, d. 67, l. 27** (Ivanov circular, 28 Jan 1915) and l. 35 (Danilov to Ivanov, 2 Feb 1915): Ivanov ordered deportation of "all Jews and suspect individuals" from points near the front lines under his jurisdiction, stating the goal was to prevent Jews acquiring knowledge of military operations, "which he assumed they would relay to the enemy" — an order potentially affecting up to ~50,000 people in his sector alone. https://doczz.net/doc/8898295/the-russian-army-and-the-jews--mass-deportation--hostages...
- **Implementation of the 4 Feb 1915 Bukovina order:** "Acting on this instruction, Ivanov further ordered that Jews be deported to a distance of no less than two hundred miles from army headquarters." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front (same re-verification caveat as §1).
- **February 1915 entry ban.** Rohatyn Jewish Heritage timeline, citing its source [2] pp. 20, 30–31 (Prusin-based literature): "Proclaiming a 'threat of widespread espionage' by Jewish merchants, the Russian Commander of the Southwest Front bans entry of Jews into Galicia and the movement of Galician Jews between its provinces" — Feb 1915. The commander of the Southwest Front at that date was Ivanov. https://rohatynjewishheritage.org/en/history/timeline-great-war/
- **Pogrom tolerance at front level.** Lohr's general finding (§1) was made about commanders operating under Stavka *and* front HQ in this period; no Ivanov order *punishing* pogromists in Galicia/Bukovina was found in this pass. **Unverified** whether he ever countermanded a subordinate's anti-Jewish measure.

### (3) Specific dated event
**Rohatyn, June 1915 (Galicia, Ivanov's front, during the Russian retreat).** "A general order to take Jewish hostages to Russia shatters Rohatyn's community: the Russian administration assembles all adult male Jews at the town center and forces 570 of them to walk out of town, first to Podwysokie, then eventually transported to Kyiv and then to Chembar (Penza province)." The Rohatyn District Research Group timeline adds they were held via Tarnopol until 1917 before returning. https://rohatynjewishheritage.org/en/history/timeline-great-war/ ; https://www.rohatyndrg.org/rohatyn/history/1914-1918-world-war-one
Rohatyn is Galicia, not Bukovina — used here because it is a precisely dated, sourced instance of the hostage system Ivanov's 28 Jan circular authorised, in the same front and the same weeks as the Bukovina deportations. For Bukovina itself under Ivanov, see the Czernowitz hostage deportations of Nov 1914 in §3(3).

### (4) Historian assessment
- **Timothy Dowling / general operational literature:** Ivanov is assessed mainly as an operational commander — cautious, Stavka-compliant, replaced because Brusilov promised action. No historian found in this pass singles Ivanov out as *more* or *less* antisemitic than Stavka; his significance for this project is that he **transmitted and sharpened** Stavka policy into a front-level deportation order within 72 hours, with an archival citation that survives.
- **Goldin (2022)** names the front-level pattern: generals of Ivanov's circle in Dec 1914–Mar 1915 are quoted proposing to "liberate the area that we occupied from Jewish domination" (Gen. Mavrin, Mar 1915) — that quote is Mavrin's, **not** Ivanov's, and is included only to show the discourse at his HQ level. https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-99788-5_9

### Records / archives
No Ivanov order concerning registers/archives found. **Unverified.**

## 3. Aleksei Brusilov (1853–1926) — 8th Army (1914–Mar 1916), then Southwestern Front (Mar 1916 – May 1917), then Supreme Commander (May–July 1917)

### (1) Command, sector, dates
8th Army in Galicia/Bukovina border fighting 1914–15 (including the 1915 retreat from the Carpathians); succeeded Ivanov at Southwestern Front Mar 1916; launched the offensive named for him 4 June – 20 Sept 1916; briefly Supreme Commander 1917. Memoirs: *A Soldier's Note-Book, 1914–1918* (Russian 1929; English, Macmillan, 1930). Biography/bibliography: https://prabook.com/web/aleksei.brusilov/1398422 ; https://spartacus-educational.com/FWWbrusilov.htm

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes — a genuinely mixed, contradictory record; do not flatten it
- **Anti-looting order, 11 August 1914 (8th Army).** A Russian article reproducing wartime orders quotes Brusilov's 8th Army order of 11 Aug 1914 naming Cossack units (Terek and Kuban) and an artillery-park unit for robberies at Krogulets and Grzymałów, and ordering marauding punished "mercilessly," with the duty laid on *all* commanding officers "even if the perpetrators were not subordinate to them." https://en.topwar.ru/amp/103157-lenin-i-bolshevik-spasli-russkuyu-civilizaciyu.html — **Flag: this is a partisan modern article (topwar.ru) quoting the order without an archival reference in the excerpt retrieved. Treat the order as *reported*, pending verification in Brusilov's papers / the 8th Army order book. It is, however, consistent with his documented 25 Sept 1914 order (same source) to identify and court-martial self-mutilators — i.e. a commander who used written disciplinary orders early and often.**
- **As occupation commander, 1914–15.** Brusilov was "commander of the Russian forces occupying Galicia": his first orders proclaimed liberation; he then governed in tension with Governor-General Bobrinsky **on one side** and with Russophile extremists, the Orthodox Church hierarchy and Russian nationalist officials **on the other** — i.e. in the literature Brusilov/Bobrinsky appear as the relatively restraining pole against the extremists, not as the drivers of Russification. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Eastern_Galicia,_1914%E2%80%931915 (citing von Hagen 2007, pp. 37–42, and Brusilov-era sources; verify in Mark von Hagen, *War in a European Borderland*, 2007, before print use).
- **But the anti-Jewish regime ran in his occupation zone, with his army.** Under the 1914–15 occupation administered in his sector: Jews were assumed loyal to Austria and treated as potential spies; Jewish publications censored; Jews arrested, deported and taken hostage "to prevent alleged spying"; in **Feb 1915 Jews were banned from moving into eastern Galicia and all publication and correspondence in Yiddish was banned**. Same source, above. No document found in this pass of Brusilov *ordering* those measures personally, and none of him *revoking* them. **His personal authorship is unverified; his command responsibility for the zone is a fact.**
- **1917 discipline.** As Supreme Commander he supported restoring capital punishment at the front to restore discipline (see §7 for the July 1917 restoration). Prabook summary, above. That is a discipline fact, not a Jewish-policy fact, and should not be conflated.

### (3) Specific dated events (two, because the record cuts both ways)
- **Czernowitz, 2 September 1914 — first Russian entry.** Units of Gen. Evreimov's force entered; Hugo Gold (ed.), *History of the Jews in the Bukovina*, vol. II, p. 28 (Arie Leon Schmelzer's wartime chapter): there was street fighting and a Jewish victim, but "the Russian soldiers maintained discipline"; a Russian commander first demanded money/valuables and then, the next day, **nullified his own order and returned them**; Archbishop von Repta stored Torah scrolls in his palace for safekeeping. https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/bukowinabook/buk2_028.html
- **Northern Bukovina / Sadagora and Wiznitz, September 1914 — the other face, same month, same army group.** The Ehpes/Czernowitz-L compilation (drawing on Philipp Menczel's contemporary account): outside the capital, "the Cossacks take it violently on the population, plundering and ransoming… Northern Bukovina is massively destroyed… they take it more particularly on the Jewish communities: **Sadagura and Wiznitz are plundered and put on fire, as well as all the synagogues in the area; the Hasidic court of Bojan is razed**." And on the Russians' return, **26 November 1914**, "all well-known and influential figures of the town — among them Mayor von Weisselberger and lawyer Menczel — are taken hostage and deported to Siberia; hundreds of apartments are plundered." https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/czernowitz12/testfile2010-2/1319.html?
  - This is the closest any source in this whole project comes to a *contemporary narrative of Wiznitz itself being plundered and burned in WWI*, and it dates the event to the **first Russian occupation, autumn 1914**, while Brusilov's 8th Army was the Bukovina-border army of Ivanov's front. It does **not** name Brusilov, or any commander, as ordering it — it names Cossack patrols as the actors and officers as failing to control the countryside while performing civility in the capital.

### (4) Historian assessment
- **Timothy C. Dowling, *The Brusilov Offensive* (Indiana UP, 2008):** the standard operational assessment — Brusilov as the most innovative Russian commander of the war, whose prepared, multi-army, short-barrage method destroyed Pflanzer-Baltin's 7th Army as a coherent force in June 1916 (see §8). Dowling excerpt: https://doczz.net/doc/4166819/the-brusilov-offensive-by-timothy-dowling
- **Joshua Sanborn** warns that Brusilov's memoir is a foundation stone that distorts: officer narratives, Brusilov's above all, claimed to "know" and speak for their men while precluding enlisted voices; Brusilov praised "the Army" only "to the extent that they obeyed his orders." Joshua Sanborn, "The Apotheosis of the Unknown Soldier": https://perspectivia.net/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/pnet_derivate_00006414/sanborn_apotheosis.pdf — relevant method warning for *any* use of *A Soldier's Note-Book* on looting/discipline: it is a self-exculpatory command memoir, to be checked against orders, not quoted as ground truth.
- **On Jews specifically:** no passage of *A Soldier's Note-Book* addressing Jews, Czernowitz or Bukovina's Jewish communities was located in this pass. **Unverified** — a full-text search of the 1930 English edition is a named follow-up (see §15).

### Records / archives
No Brusilov order on registers/archives found. **Unverified.** Indirectly: an army whose commander issues an anti-marauding order in week two, but whose occupation administration bans Yiddish correspondence and deports communal notables, will produce (a) better-preserved *movable* property in garrison towns than in the countryside and (b) a broken *register chain* everywhere the registrar class was expelled. That is an inference from the documented pattern, labelled as inference.

## 4. Platon Lechitsky (1856–1921) — 9th Army, the Bukovina army, Aug 1914 – Apr 1917

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Took command of the 9th Army 9 Aug 1914 and kept it through the whole Bukovina war: Dniester/Prut operations 1914–15, holding the Chernivtsi (Czernowitz) sector, and in 1916 the southern wing of the Brusilov Offensive. Ill with lobar pneumonia in Mar 1916 (treated at Kamianets-Podilskyi; visited there by Nicholas II, 30 Mar 1916) but retained command for the offensive. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platon_Lechitsky

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes toward Jews, civilians, looting, records
- **None found. This is the most important negative in this report.** No order, letter, memoir passage or scholarly passage was found in this pass attributing to Lechitsky personally any measure about Jews, hostages, expulsions, billeting, looting discipline, or document seizure/preservation — in either direction (neither persecutory nor protective).
- What operated *in his sector* is documented, but at other levels of command: Stavka's Jan–Mar 1915 expulsion/hostage system (§1), Ivanov's 28 Jan 1915 front circular (§2), the Governor-General of Galicia's administration, whose Chernivtsi gubernia was one of its four subdivisions (§3), and, in 1916, Brusilov's front HQ (§3). Attributing any of those to Lechitsky personally would be exactly the reputation-by-proximity error the brief warned against.
- He left no published memoir comparable to Brusilov's, Hoffmann's or Ludendorff's — one structural reason the record is thin. **Unverified** is the honest entry for (2), and any future claim about Lechitsky and Jews should be required to produce a 9th Army document (RGVIA 9th Army fonds) rather than a generalisation.

### (3) Specific dated event in a town under his command
**Czernowitz, 17–18 June 1916.** Lechitsky's 9th Army broke Pflanzer-Baltin's 7th Army at Dobronouts/Dobronăuți, advanced ~50 km in 19 days, took ~38,000 prisoners in that battle, and on **18 June 1916 captured Czernowitz** — doing so, the biographical literature stresses, *contrary to* Brusilov's instruction to turn quickly toward Halych/Stanislaviv, because Lechitsky judged it dangerous to leave a fortified enemy base on his left flank. HistoryNet's offensive summary gives the capture as 18 June ("pushed the defenders across the Prut and captured Chernovtsy on June 18"); some accounts give the entry on 17 June. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platon_Lechitsky ; https://historynet.com/almost-victory/?f ; battle page https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Czernowitz_(1916)
- **What happened to Czernowitz's Jews on that entry is not described in any source retrieved in this pass.** Gold's Bukovina history notes only that the June 1916 Brusilov offensive "led to a retreat of the Austrian Eastern Army and sped up the evacuation of Czernowitz," and that Jewish families who had fled gradually returned only after the Russians were pushed east again in 1917, finding provincial homes destroyed. https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/bukowinabook/buk2_028.html — i.e. for 1916 the documented Jewish experience in his sector is *flight before his army arrived*, not a recorded pogrom on entry. That is a source gap, not proof of good conduct.

### (4) Historian assessment
- Operational literature rates the 9th Army, with Brusilov's 8th, as "the most combat-ready formation" of the Russian forces in 1914, and Lechitsky's 1916 advance as the most territorially successful of the four offensive armies. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platon_Lechitsky ; Russian operational summary https://en.topwar.ru/amp/65368-triumf-na-kostyah.html
- **Dowling (2008)** treats Lechitsky's artillery method — rapid cycles of fire with ~15-minute pauses producing repeated false "all-clear" alarms, against trenches in places only 40–100 yards away — as a distinct tactical contribution matching Brusilov's in the north. Summarised from Dowling at https://www.historyofthegreatwar.com/episodes/2016/86/
- No assessment of Lechitsky as a civilian-policy actor was found in Lohr, Goldin, Watson, Cornwall, Schindler or Dowling in this pass. **Unverified / unassessed in the literature searched.**

### Records / archives
Nothing. **Unverified.** The 9th Army is, for this project, the highest-value *unworked* archival target: it held the Cheremosh–Prut sector, including Wiznitz, in both occupations.

## 5. Aleksei Kaledin (1861–1918) — 12th Cavalry Division, then XII Corps, then 8th Army, Mar 1916 – Apr/May 1917

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Don Cossack cavalry general. Commanded 12th Cavalry Division Aug 1914–Mar 1916 and V Cavalry/I Corps formations in 1915; appointed to the **8th Army 23 Mar (5 Apr) 1916**; won the Battle of Lutsk in June 1916 — the northern breakthrough of the Brusilov Offensive, smashing the Austro-Hungarian 4th Army and advancing ~50 miles in nine days. Relieved/ resigned in spring 1917 after refusing the Provisional Government's democratisation orders; elected Ataman of the Don Host 18 June (1 July) 1917; shot himself 11 Feb 1918 (29 Jan O.S. in some accounts) as the Don collapsed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Kaledin ; Jonathan Smele, *Historical Dictionary of the Russian Civil Wars*, Kaledin entry https://fb2.top/historical-dictionary-of-the-russian-civil-wars-1916-1926-596522/read/part-26

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **Toward Jews: none found for his WWI army command. Unverified.** No Kaledin order on Jews, hostages or expulsions was found in this pass, in either direction.
- **What is documented is political, and later:** at the Moscow State Conference, Aug 1917, Kaledin — alongside Kornilov — demanded abolition/restriction of soldiers' committees, restoration of officers' disciplinary rights, and discipline "by the most decisive measures," with front and rear treated as one. That speech is reproduced in the Trotsky/Fraina volume: https://WWW.Marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1917/next/intro.htm — it is evidence about his *discipline* philosophy in 1917, not about Jews in Lutsk in 1916, and is not used here as such.
- **Cossack looting caveat (important, and often elided):** Kaledin was a Don Cossack commanding an army containing Cossack formations, in a war in which Cossack units are the actors most frequently named in Jewish sources for plunder (see Wiznitz/Sadagora, §3(3), and Brusilov's Aug 1914 anti-looting order naming Terek/Kuban Cossacks). **No source found ties a specific looting or pogrom incident to Kaledin's 8th Army at Lutsk in June 1916, and none ties an anti-looting order to Kaledin personally.** Both directions are **unverified**. His later role as White Don Ataman (1917–18) must not be read backwards into 1916.

### (3) Specific dated event
**Lutsk, 7 June 1916 (25 May O.S. in some Russian accounts).** Kaledin's 8th Army broke through on a ~16 km rupture and took Lutsk; the Austro-Hungarian 4th Army effectively ceased to exist; 8th Army trophies reported as 922 officers and 43,628 men captured, 66 guns (figures from the Cossack-war survey reproducing Russian summaries). https://en.topwar.ru/amp/65193-kazaki-i-pervaya-mirovaya-voyna-chast-iv-1916-god.html ; command dates https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexey_Kaledin
- Jewish Lutsk's experience of June 1916 is **not documented in any source retrieved in this pass** — a gap to flag, not fill.

### (4) Historian assessment
- Operational: Smele (above) — "as commander of the 8th Army… he played a prominent role on the South-West Front during the Brusilov offensive of May 1916, smashing the Austrian 4th Army." (Smele dates the offensive from May O.S.)
- Political: Peter Kenez, *Red Attack, White Resistance* (2004), pp. 41–42, cited in the Kaledin literature for his Don leadership — a Civil War assessment, out of scope for Bukovina except as a warning against back-projection.

### Records / archives
Nothing found. **Unverified.** Sector relevance to Vizhnitz is in any case indirect: Lutsk/Volhynia is the northern, not the Cheremosh, sector. Kaledin is in this report as the brief's comparator for "the other successful Brusilov army commander," and the honest finding is that the civilian/Jewish record for his army is as thin as Lechitsky's — while Stavka/front policy above both of them is thick.

## 6. Dmitry Shcherbachev (1857–1932) — IX Corps, then 11th Army (Apr–Oct 1915), then 7th Army (Oct 1915 – Apr 1917), then Romanian Front

### (1) Command, sector, dates
IX Corps in the Battle of Galicia 1914 (his corps entered Lemberg; Order of St George). Commander **11th Army 5 Apr – 19 Oct 1915** (Carpathians, between Brusilov's 8th and Lechitsky's 9th; opposed by Linsingen's German South Army; conducted the 1915 retreat after Gorlice). Commander **7th Army 19 Oct 1915 – 11 Apr 1917** (Strypa sector; in the Brusilov Offensive his army won on the Strypa and threatened Bothmer's South Army communications). From **11 Apr 1917**, deputy/assistant to King Ferdinand as commander of the **Romanian Front** (four Russian and two Romanian armies), later of a combined Ukrainian Front; consented in Dec 1917 to Romanian entry into Bessarabia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Shcherbachev ; Southwestern Front command lists https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Front_(Russian_Empire) ; Great Soviet Encyclopedia entry https://encyclopedia2.thefreedictionary.com/Shcherbachev%2c+Dmitrii

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **Toward Jews, civilians, looting, records: none found. Unverified in all four categories.** No Shcherbachev order, memoir passage or scholarly passage on Jews was retrieved in this pass. He is the most purely "artillery technician" figure in the set — the literature discusses his advocacy of French-style heavy, narrow-front breakthrough bombardment, and blames that method for his failed Bessarabia/Strypa attacks in 1915 — and simply does not discuss him as a civilian-policy actor. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Shcherbachev?oldid=724796940
- Note the sector consequence, stated as context not as personal policy: his 11th Army held the Carpathian centre in spring–summer 1915, i.e. through the period when Stavka's hostage system (§1) and the front's deportation circulars (§2) were in force in Galicia; and his 7th Army held the Strypa through 1916. Towns in that sector with a documented hostage event include Stryj (see below).

### (3) Specific dated event in a town in his sector
**Stryj, on the Russians' retreat in 1915 (11th Army sector).** The Stryj Yizkor book: during the 1914–15 occupation the town was placed under military authority, civilian institutions banned, and a Jewish committee was made responsible for supplying unpaid labour to the military; "The Russians took hostages upon retreating from the city in 1915. They kept them in jail for three months and then they were deported east into Russia" — fourteen named hostages, including Prof. Bernfeld, Rabbi Yeshaya Yolles, the cantor and the pharmacist. The same chapter records the occupation's start as "kidnappings for slave labor, rape, looting and plunder" in the first chaotic days, then formal military rule. https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/stryj2/str065.html
- **Attribution discipline:** the Yizkor book names "the Russians," not Shcherbachev. It is evidence of practice in his army's sector in his tenure, **not** of a Shcherbachev order.

### (4) Historian assessment
- No assessment by Lohr, Goldin, Watson, Cornwall, Schindler or Dowling of Shcherbachev on civilians/Jews was found. The standard verdict in the biographical literature is operational and negative-to-mixed: "generally unsuccessful" as a breakthrough commander in 1915, successful on the Strypa in 1916, politically counter-revolutionary in 1917–18. https://prabook.com/web/dmitry.shcherbachev/1721335

### Records / archives
Nothing found. **Unverified.** His Romanian Front tenure (1917) is, however, the channel through which Russian-held Bukovina's south flank was administered in the last year — a Romanian-archives angle (Iași front HQ) that no source in this pass pursued.

## 7. Lavr Kornilov (1870–1918) — 8th Army in the Kerensky Offensive (July 1917), then Southwestern Front, then Supreme Commander

### (1) Command, sector, dates
(Before 1917: 48th Infantry Division, captured at Przemyśl Mar 1915, escaped 1916; XXV Corps.) Appointed to the **8th Army** for the Kerensky Offensive: his army attacked 8 July 1917 south of the Dniester, took Halych (10 July) and **Kalush/Kalusz (11 July)** — the offensive's only real success. Promoted to command the **Southwestern Front** (replacing Gutor) after the German counterstroke began 19 July, then **Supreme Commander-in-Chief** days later; demanded restoration of the death penalty; dismissed and arrested after the failed August/September "Kornilov affair"; killed at Ekaterinodar 1918. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerensky_Offensive ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnopol_offensive

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **Discipline by execution — documented as his defining demand.** On taking over the collapsing front he demanded the Provisional Government reinstate the death penalty for combat troops; mass executions of deserters followed in the breakthrough zone, with slips reading "Deserter" pinned on the bodies as a deterrent. The death penalty at the front was restored in this crisis (Kerensky government decree, cited in the literature as 12 July / effective in the theatre by 25 July in Knox's diary chronology). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarnopol_offensive ; Alfred Knox, *With the Russian Army 1914–1917* (1921), contents for July 1917: "The pillage and massacre at Kalusz — General Kornilov replaces General Gutor… The death penalty re-established in the theatre of war on July 25th — General Kornilov's Order of August 1st stigmatises the treachery of…" https://picclick.it/Major-General-Knox-WITH-THE-RUSSIAN-ARMY-1914-1917-Diary-126168707854.html — *Knox is a primary eyewitness source (British attaché); the bookseller page is only the finding aid here — cite Knox 1921, vol. II, directly in any print use.*
- **Toward Jews as a policy: no Kornilov order about Jews was found. Unverified.** What is documented is that the catastrophe he was brought in to stop *took the form of pogroms by his own retreating soldiers*, and that he answered it as a discipline problem (executions, "treachery" orders), not as protection of Jews. Do not upgrade that into either antisemitic intent or philosemitic protection — neither is in the documents retrieved.

### (3) Specific dated events
- **Kalush, July 1917 — "pillage and massacre at Kalusz" (Knox).** The Kalush Yizkor book describes the 1917 Russian re-entry: three days of battle in the town centre, then "The Russians plundered homes, raped women, burned down 200 dwellings in the center of the town. There was one civilian death, a Cossack shot down a Jewish woman — Breintsye Likvornik." https://www.jewishgen.org/yizkor/kalusz/kal576.html — Kornilov's 8th Army had *captured* Kalush on 11 July; the pillage/massacre Knox records belongs to the subsequent disintegration/retreat. The sequence — his army takes the town, then the army dissolves into pogrom violence in it — is the event, and it is why Kerensky made him front commander and then Supreme Commander.
- **Tarnopol, 19–28 July 1917.** German-Austrian counteroffensive broke the Southwestern Front; Tarnopol fell 25 July. Yad Vashem's Tarnopol community history: "The Russian retreat in the summer of 1917 was accompanied by widespread looting in the city." Boris Elkin, "The Kerensky Government and Its Fate," *Slavic Review* 23/4 (1964): Kerensky himself said "the retreat from Galicia was not as terrible as were the unforgettable scenes of the pogroms in Kalushch, Tarnopol, and other towns," and Elkin notes the document collections' omission of "pogroms perpetrated by the licentious soldiery at the theater of war." After this rout "the Kerensky government restored the death penalty for crimes committed at the front." https://yv360.yadvashem.org/api/UntoldStories/GetCommunity?lang=en&id=14622366 ; https://padlet-uploads.storage.googleapis.com/39416179/8c31c4205150a9e68e54d8db38d22e3d/The_Kerensky_Government_and_its_fate.pdf
- Kornilov's own framing, Moscow State Conference, Aug 1917: "If decisive measures for the improvement of discipline at the front followed as a result of **the devastation of Tarnopol and the loss of Galicia and Bukovina**, we must not allow that order in the rear should be a result of the loss of Riga…" — i.e. he publicly used the Tarnopol/Bukovina collapse, pogroms included in its "devastation," as the argument for military dictatorship in all but name. https://WWW.Marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1917/next/intro.htm

### (4) Historian assessment
- The consensus operational/political verdict (Kerensky Offensive literature, above): the most successful army commander of July 1917, elevated *because* Kerensky "hoped he could restore discipline and order among the retreating troops" — and the attempt destroyed both the government and him within two months.
- For this project, the salient assessment is Elkin's (1964): the pogrom wave of July 1917 is documented in Kerensky's own words yet was already being edited out of the official document collections — a warning that *absence of pogroms from a command's published documents is not evidence of absence*, for Kornilov's front above all.

### Records / archives
No Kornilov document on registers/archives found. **Unverified.** By July 1917 in Galicia/Bukovina the register question was in any case secondary: communities had been expelled, fled or hostage-stripped in 1914–16, and 1917 added physical destruction of town centres (200 dwellings burned in Kalush alone).

## 8. Karl von Pflanzer-Baltin (1855–1925) — Armeegruppe Pflanzer-Baltin (Oct 1914 – May 1915), then 7th Army (8 May 1915 – 8 Sept 1916), Bukovina

### (1) Command, sector, dates
The Austro-Hungarian commander of the Bukovina/Carpathian right wing for almost the entire period that matters to Vizhnitz. Formed Armeegruppe Pflanzer-Baltin 3 Oct 1914 to cover the Carpathian passes and Bukovina with scratch/third-line forces; held through winter 1914–15; counter-attacked in Feb 1915, retaking **Kolomea 16 Feb and Stanislau 20 Feb 1915** and driving the Russians from Czernowitz; his group was redesignated **7th Army in May 1915**. Won the "New Year's Battle" at the turn of 1915/16. Promoted Generaloberst 1 May 1916. Destroyed as an effective army by Lechitsky/Shcherbachev in June 1916 (79th Honvéd Brigade annihilated 5 June; breakthroughs at Okna 10 June; Czernowitz lost 17/18 June); German commands refused to place German troops under him; relieved **8 Sept 1916** (Kirchbach succeeded), after Archduke Friedrich advised him in writing to "report sick and request your own relief." Later commanded Army Group Albania (1918). http://www.austro-hungarian-army.co.uk/biog/Pflanzer.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Army_(Austria-Hungary) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **Toward Jews specifically: no Pflanzer-Baltin order about Jews was found in this pass. Unverified.** Bukovina's Jews were, in the Habsburg frame, *loyal imperial subjects* — Czernowitz had a Jewish mayor (Salo von Weisselberger, elected 1913) — and the AOK's suspect categories in Bukovina/Galicia were primarily **Russophile Ruthenians**, then Serbs, Czechs and Italians (see §9). That structural difference from the Russian side is real and documented at AOK level; Pflanzer-Baltin's personal voice in it is not in the sources retrieved.
- **Toward his own "unreliable" troops — documented, Dec 1914.** Austria-Forum's web edition of *The First World War and the End of the Habsburg Monarchy*: "At the beginning of December, the commander of the army group named after him, General of Cavalry Pflanzer-Baltin, made an application for the successive replacement of between 6,000 and 7,000 men 'of Ruthenian, less reliable troops on the southern wing of the eastern front through other regiments'." https://austria-forum.org/web-books/firstworldwar01en2014isds/000330 — A commander's suspicion documented against *soldiers of his own army* on ethnic grounds, in the Bukovina sector, in writing. It is the closest personal analogue in his record to the ethnic-loyalty logic that, at AOK level, produced the treason courts and Thalerhof (§9).
- **Reprisals on reconquest — sector-level, not personal order.** The Carpathian literature summarises the Feb–Mar 1915 Austro-Hungarian reconquest of Bukovina thus: "the Austro-Hungarian military authorities imposed strict martial law. The entire population was subject to re-registration, movement was restricted through a system of military passes, and special food requisitions were introduced. Reprisals were carried out against those suspected of having collaborated with the Russians." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front — **Re-registration + passes + special requisitions is exactly the "implanting" apparatus the main project asks about**, and it ran in Pflanzer-Baltin's sector on his reconquest; no Pflanzer-signed text of it was retrieved. Attribution: sector/system, not personal order.
- **Requisitions/billeting under his predecessor command structure:** Ádám Suslik's dissertation on the operational areas (Bereg/Ung, subordinated to Pflanzer-Baltin's group Oct 1914–Feb 1915) documents from the group's own records that logistics (accommodation and food) for troops in the operational area were initially *unorganised*, abuses were already documented in **Sept 1914**, and military supply bases were only created in **Feb 1915** — i.e. for the first five months, his formation lived off the country ad hoc. https://lib.kre.hu:8080/phd/Tezisek_angol_suslikadam.pdf

### (3) Specific dated event
**Czernowitz / Bukovina, February 1915 reconquest.** Pflanzer-Baltin's winter counteroffensive retook Czernowitz (Russians expelled 17 Feb 1915 in the Ehpes chronology) and Kolomea/Stanislau (16/20 Feb), after which the martial-law/re-registration/requisition regime above was imposed on a population that included Jews who had *remained* under Russian occupation — and who were therefore exposed to collaboration suspicion from the returning side after having been treated as enemy spies by the departing side. That double exposure, not a single Pflanzer order, is the documented Bukovina Jewish experience of his command. Ehpes chronology: https://czernowitz.ehpes.com/czernowitz12/testfile2010-2/1319.html? ; offensive dates: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Front

### (4) Historian assessment
- **Conrad's own verdict (via the service literature):** Conrad "considered Pflanzer to be his best army commander" — the only Habsburg army commander of 1914–15 who held his sector with scratch forces. http://www.austro-hungarian-army.co.uk/biog/Pflanzer.htm (summary at https://worldarmies.com/AustriaVol2/AustriaVol2-p1035.pdf)
- **Dowling (2008):** by June 1916 the 7th Army "suddenly and inexplicably collapsed" — Dowling's detail matters: four of its best divisions had been stripped for Italy in early 1916, the 79th Honvéd Brigade was inexperienced, and Lechitsky's barrage method (23 heavy guns brought from Ochakov) shredded it on 5 June; Pflanzer-Baltin then ordered a strategic retreat behind the Dniester/Prut, ~80 km west, to cover the Carpathian passes. https://doczz.net/doc/4166819/the-brusilov-offensive-by-timothy-dowling
- **German verdict (1916), documented in Friedrich's relief letter** (quoted in the biography, above): the German high command "repeatedly expressed its objections over the leadership of the 7th army" and refused German troops to his command. Personality assessment in the sources is thus polarised by army/nationality — Austrian loyalist hero of 1915, German-scapegoated failure of 1916 — and neither pole says anything about Jews. **Unverified** on the brief's core questions, and stated so plainly because Pflanzer-Baltin is the man a less careful report would most easily have filled in by invention: he held Wiznitz's sector longest on the Habsburg side.

### Records / archives
No Pflanzer-Baltin order on community registers/archives found. **Unverified.** Sector note: the Feb 1915 *re-registration of the entire population* (§2) implies a military population register was *created* by his administration — whether it survived, and where (Kriegsarchiv 7. Armee / Armeegruppe Pflanzer-Baltin fonds), is a concrete unworked archival question, distinct from the fate of the Jewish community's own books.

## 9. Franz Conrad von Hötzendorf (1852–1925) — Chief of the General Staff / AOK, 1912 – 1 Mar 1917

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Chief of the General Staff for the whole armed force (second term) Dec 1912 – 1 Mar 1917, operating through the Armeeoberkommando (AOK). He designed the 1914 Galician offensive whose collapse opened Galicia and Bukovina to Russia, the Carpathian winter offensives of 1915, and the 1916 South Tyrol offensive that stripped Pflanzer-Baltin's 7th Army of divisions weeks before Brusilov struck. Dismissed by Emperor Karl, 1 Mar 1917; replaced by Arz (§10). https://austria-forum.org/web-books/en/firstworldwar01en2014isds/000271 ; Arz succession https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_General_Staff

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes — the Habsburg system's driver, aimed primarily at Slav suspects, Jews included in the wider net
- **Military jurisdiction over civilians and a treason regime.** Mark Cornwall, "Traitors and the Meaning of Treason in Austria-Hungary's Great War," *Transactions of the Royal Historical Society* 25 (2015) 113–134: pre-war treason law (Austrian penal code 1852, §58) was "exploited quite arbitrarily after 1914"; the military were given "extensive powers to prosecute all political crimes including treason," producing show trials of Bosnian Serbs and Czech politicians, until the 1917 amnesty backfired on the regime. https://www.academia.edu/39968987/Traitors_and_the_Meaning_of_Treason_in_Austria_Hungarys_Great_War (DOI 10.1017/S0080440115000055)
- **October 1914 — Conrad orders action on a captured Russian informant dossier, and AOK seeks a military governor for Galicia.** Austria-Forum web edition of *The First World War and the End of the Habsburg Monarchy*, "In the Shadow of the Gallows": Austrian troops seized a Russian dossier naming confidential informants; "It goes without saying that Conrad gave the order to proceed immediately against collaborators and informants." AOK's 14 Oct 1914 report to the Emperor claimed the armies were suffering "in their own country from the betrayal and espionage perpetrated by the Russophile population." The same chapter records **nearly 5,000 death sentences in front-line Galicia** over the war years, most for "treasonable activities," and states of the system: "this was generated by an Army High Command that had Conrad as its driving force, and in him, a man who also demonstrated an uncompromising attitude in domestic matters." https://austria-forum.org/web-books/en/firstworldwar01en2014isds/000271
- **Scale of Habsburg military violence against its own civilians.** Habsburger.net (1914-1918 encyclopedia project), citing Anton Holzer, *Das Lächeln der Henker* (2008) and Oswald Überegger: violence was aimed "in particular at the Jewish, Polish and Ruthenian population in the Bukovina, Galicia and the occupied Russian regions"; Holzer: the excesses were "arranged and planned at the highest level. They included hostage-taking and hostage-shootings, massed deportation, incarceration and forced labour — and mass executions… in part tolerated, in part left unpunished: rape, looting, arbitrary killings and the destruction of houses." Estimates given: **11,400–36,000 civilians condemned to the gallows in the early months of the war alone**; executions staged on town squares, bodies left hanging for days as a lesson to "spies" and "traitors." https://ww1.habsburger.net/en/chapters/war-crimes-habsburg-army-between-soldateska-and-court-martial
- **Thalerhof.** The internment camp at Thalerhof near Graz (opened Sept 1914, closed by Karl I in 1917) held suspected Russophiles from Galicia and Bukovina: ~5,700 internees already in Nov 1914 (Christoph Mick's figures), with post-war Ukrainian estimates of 25,000 dead in prisons/camps overall and a true figure "unknown, estimates ranging from 11,500 to 30,000" for the wider repression. Christoph Mick, "Legality, ethnicity and violence in Austrian Galicia, 1890–1920": https://wrap-test.warwick.ac.uk/123674/1/WRAP-Legality-ethnicity-violence-Galilcia-Mick-2019.pdf ; higher popular figures (30,000 interned at Thalerhof alone) circulate in Russophile commemorative literature — **flagged as contested; use Mick's archival figures in print.**
- **Pre-war lists.** From Feb 1912, as part of war planning, the k.u.k. Armee had provincial governors collect names of suspected disloyal persons — "Galicia and Bukovina were no exception" — so the 1914 arrests worked from peacetime lists, a system built under Conrad's general staff. Paul Best, "The Tragedy of Thalerhof," *Karpatska Rus'* 2014: http://lemkoassociation.org/Archives/Karpatska%20Rus%27///2014/KR2014_01_02.pdf
- **Toward Jews specifically:** Conrad's personal antisemitism is asserted in popular literature but **no Conrad order singling out Jews as Jews was retrieved in this pass — unverified at the personal level.** The documented Habsburg pattern is different from the Russian one and should not be merged with it: Jews were caught in the general suspicion/looting net (Holzer, above, names Bukovina's Jews explicitly among the targets of violence), were frequent victims of espionage denunciation by neighbours (An-sky's Galician evidence, §3), and served in large numbers in the k.u.k. Armee under a Feldrabbiner system — while the *categorical* Habsburg enemy-within machinery (lists, gallows, Thalerhof) was aimed first at Russophile Ruthenians and Serbs.

### (3) Specific dated event
**Galicia, autumn 1914 (sector-wide, AOK system).** Retreating Habsburg troops "avenged themselves upon suspected Russophiles and their families. Hundreds of suspected Russophiles were shot, and thirty thousand were sent to the Talerhof concentration camp" in the standard summary — with Mick's lower archival count for Thalerhof itself (~5,700 by Nov 1914) noted above as the figure to prefer. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Russophilia ; Mick, above. Bukovina supplied internees to the same system (Mick: internees "from Galicia and the Bukovina").

### (4) Historian assessment
- **John R. Schindler, *Fall of the Double Eagle* (Potomac, 2015):** Conrad is the central figure and the verdict is catastrophic — the Galician campaign destroyed the Habsburg army in three weeks in 1914, a judgement Schindler anchors in Churchill's 1931 summary: "Of all the armies that have ever existed… the Austro-Hungarian army needed the most careful handling. Conrad broke their hearts and used them up in three weeks." Review/summary: https://acceptance.historynet.com/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/13707407
- **Cornwall (2015):** the treason obsession was self-defeating — by 1917–18 the accused "proudly began to assume the title of 'traitor'," i.e. AOK's loyalty machinery manufactured the disloyalty it claimed to detect.
- **Watson, *The Fortress* (2019)** (as characterised in reviews): Conrad was "indifferent to the sufferings of many of the civilians who lived in his jurisdiction, and actively suspicious of the loyalty of many of the minority groups… Jews and Ruthenes came in for particular scrutiny and ultimately, harsh treatment." Quoted at https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/43805737-the-fortress — secondhand; verify in the book.

### Records / archives — the one leader in this report with a *documented post-war records consequence*
Not a Conrad order, but the archival system he headed: the Kriegsarchiv Wien's standing answer on WWI personnel records states that service sheets, draft lists and enlistment registers for birth cohorts 1850–1900 for men whose domicile lay **outside** today's Austria did **not** come to Vienna after 1918; under the Treaty of Saint-Germain (1919) they remained in the successor states, "where they were largely destroyed — that applies in particular also to the Galician (Polish/Ukrainian) personnel files"; Vienna retains only subsidiary award/casualty/hospital material for such men. Reproduced (Kriegsarchiv letter, 2021) at https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/messages?page=683&after=1669746815809185723&subsort=1 ; an earlier researcher summary adds that repatriated records went "mainly to Prague and Tyrnau [Trnava]" and "none of the records from Galicia seem to have survived." https://groups.jewishgen.org/g/main/topic/jri_poland_poland/70797469
- **Relevance discipline:** these are *military personnel* records, not Jewish community registers. They prove the general mechanism — records follow sovereignty, and in the successor-state transfers they were destroyed *en masse* — which is the documented analogue for what happened to other Galician/Bukovinian paper in 1918–20. They do **not** prove anything about the Wiznitz Matriken specifically.

## 10. The actual 7th Army succession in Bukovina after Pflanzer-Baltin — and Arz, corrected

### Karl Graf von Kirchbach auf Lauterbach — 7th Army, 8 Sept – 20 Oct 1916
Took over from Pflanzer-Baltin 8 Sept 1916 and "managed to prevent the enemy troops from breaking through" during the Brusilov Offensive's last phase; exchanged with Kövess 20 Oct 1916, taking the 3rd Army. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Graf_von_Kirchbach_auf_Lauterbach — **Jews/civilians/records: nothing found. Unverified.**

### Hermann Kövess von Kövessháza — 7th Army, 20 Oct 1916 – 16 Jan 1918
Held Bukovina through 1917 — i.e. **his** 7th Army, with Křitek's 3rd Army on its left, carried out the July–August 1917 reconquest ("in 1917 the entire Bukovina was liberated while the Russian Army collapsed"), including the re-entry into Czernowitz (3 Aug 1917 in the standard chronology — *date from general chronology, not re-verified in this pass*). Promoted Feldmarschall 5 Aug 1917. Commander list: http://www.austro-hungarian-army.co.uk/commands.htm ; 7th Army summary https://worldarmies.com/AustriaVol2/AustriaVol2-p1035.pdf — **Jews/civilians/records: no Kövess order or assessment found. Unverified.** Any account attributing the 1917 Bukovina reconquest's civilian regime to "Arz's 7th Army" is wrong on the commander: at army level it was Kövess; at AOK level it was Arz as chief of staff.

### Karl Křitek (1861–1928) — 3rd Army from 12 July 1917; unified 3rd/7th Armies Jan 1918
"Following the successful breakthrough at Zborow in cooperation with the German Südarmee, he succeeded in driving the Russian troops back and liberating the Bukowina. In January 1918 [he] assumed command of the then united 3rd and 7th armies and took part in the advance into the Ukraine"; 7th Army HQ dissolved 15 Apr 1918. http://www.austro-hungarian-army.co.uk/biog/kritek.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_K%C5%99itek — **Jews/civilians/records: nothing found. Unverified.**

### Arthur Arz von Straussenburg (1857–1935) — the corrected profile
- **(1) Commands:** 15th Infantry Division (1914), VI Corps, **1st Army in Romania 16 Aug 1916 – Feb 1917** (with Falkenhayn's German 9th Army and Mackensen's Danube Army), then **Chief of the General Staff 1 Mar 1917 – 3 Nov 1918**. His own histories: *Zur Geschichte des großen Krieges 1914–1918* (1924); *Kampf und Sturz der Kaiserreiche* (1935). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Arz_von_Strau%C3%9Fenburg ; AEIOU https://www.aeiou.at/aeiou.encyclop.a/a757834.htm;internal&action=_setlanguage.action?LANGUAGE=en
- **(2) Documented attitudes:** Unlike Conrad, Arz "acted as a personal advisor to the Emperor on army matters, rather than as a driver of his own strategy"; his tenure saw "increasing German control" but also the 1917 clearing of Galicia and Bukovina and Caporetto. No Arz order or statement about Jews, civilians in Bukovina, looting or archives was found. **Unverified in all categories.** The one civil-political trace retrieved is strategic, not ethnic: as chief of staff in Nov 1917 he argued over the Khotyn/Czernowitz border rectification in the Ukraine peace talks on the ground that Khotyn territory was "necessary to preserve the security of Czernowitz, the capital of… Bukovina." Václav Horčička, "Austria–Hungary and Peace Talks with Ukraine in 1918," *Przegląd Historyczny*: https://przegladhistoryczny.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/sites/213/2024/05/499-526-Vaclav-Horcicka.pdf
- **(3) Event:** none in Bukovina under his *army* command (he never held one there). The Aug 1917 reconquest of Czernowitz happened under his AOK, executed by Böhm-Ermolli's army group / Kövess's and Křitek's armies (§§10–11).
- **(4) Assessment:** the service literature's verdict is "no great distinction" as a field commander, and a deliberate anti-Conrad as chief of staff — compliant with Karl I, administratively competent, strategically subordinate to Germany. That ordinariness is itself the finding: **there is no "Arz policy toward the Jews of Bukovina" in the literature, because Arz was not the Bukovina commander the brief assumed.**

## 11. Eduard von Böhm-Ermolli (1856–1941) — 2nd Army, then Army Group Böhm-Ermolli, Galicia (comparator, north of Bukovina)

### (1) Command, sector, dates
2nd Army from Aug 1914 (briefly Serbia, then Galicia) **until May 1918** — the longest single army command on this front. Carpathian operations Feb–Mar 1915 (took command of the reinforced 2nd Army grouping 15 Feb 1915; failed to relieve Przemyśl, which fell 22 Mar 1915); retook **Lemberg 22 June 1915** in the Gorlice pursuit; **Army Group Böhm-Ermolli** (2nd Army + German South Army, later + 3rd Army) 19 Sept 1915 – 25 July 1916 and 4 Oct 1916 – 24 Jan 1918; repelled the Kerensky Offensive and counter-attacked from Złoczów 19 July 1917; Feldmarschall 31 Jan 1918; relieved 16 May 1918 after clashing with German commanders in Ukraine. http://wwi.lib.byu.edu/index.php/Ermolli ; https://prabook.com/web/eduard_baron.von_bohm-ermolli/1398402 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Group_B%C3%B6hm-Ermolli

### (2) Documented orders / attitudes
- **Toward Jews/civilians/looting/records: no personal order or statement found. Unverified in all categories** — the same evidentiary situation as Lechitsky and Kaledin, on the Habsburg side.
- Sector context (system, not personal): his army group was the formation in front of Lemberg/eastern Galicia in 1915–17, i.e. the Habsburg force that re-entered towns *after* the Russian hostage deportations (§2, Rohatyn) and administered them under the AOK treason/requisition regime (§9). The one contemporary Jewish-side document retrieved from his exact sector and moment is not about him: Rubin Glücksmann's letter from Rohatyn, 5 July 1915, "About the Russian Dominance in Rohatyn," written from a mortar battery of Bothmer's German South Army (then in Böhm-Ermolli's wider grouping) to Vienna's *Jüdisches Archiv* — evidence that Jewish soldiers in his sector were documenting Russian occupation abuses for a Jewish archive in real time. https://www.rohatyndrg.org/rohatyn/history/1914-1918-world-war-one

### (3) Specific dated event
**Lemberg, 22 June 1915.** Böhm-Ermolli's troops retook the Galician capital; in the days around the Russian evacuation the Russians deported Rohatyn's 570 Jewish hostages east (§2) and, across his sector, the retreating administration took hostages from town after town (Stryj, §6). The Habsburg reoccupation then applied the §9 machinery to a population pre-sorted by the occupier's violence. No source retrieved describes a Böhm-Ermolli order altering either process. Attribution: sector/system.

### (4) Historian assessment
Operational literature treats him as the Habsburg army's steady professional — second-highest rank by May 1916, entrusted with army-group coordination twice, finally removed (1918) for inability to work with the Germans in Ukraine, i.e. for friction *in the exploitation administration of occupied Ukraine* ("directed from Odessa the measures for turning to account the resources of that country," 1922 Encyclopaedia Britannica, https://wehd.com/bios/Eduard_von_Bohm-Ermolli.html ). That 1918 Ukraine economic-exploitation role is documented; any Jewish-policy content in it is **unverified**.

### Records / archives
Nothing personal. **Unverified.**

## 12. Erich Ludendorff (1865–1948) — Chief of Staff, Ober Ost, 1914 – Aug 1916 (comparator: a *systematic* occupation, never in Bukovina)

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Chief of staff to Hindenburg in the East (8th Army, then Ober Ost — Supreme Command of all German forces in the East) from Aug 1914; the real administrator of the Ober Ost territory (Russian Poland's north-east, Lithuania, Courland, Białystok-Grodno) taken in 1915; left Aug 1916 to become First Quartermaster-General at OHL. Memoirs: *Ludendorff's Own Story / My War Memories* (1919). USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia profile: https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/erich-ludendorff

### (2) Documented policies — the contrast case: exploitation by bureaucracy, not by pogrom
- **System design (1915).** "Although von Hindenburg was technically in command, it was Ludendorff who had actual control of the administration": ten specialist staff sections (finance, agriculture…), districts under military commanders, a plan to make Ober Ost a self-sustaining, surplus-exporting colony and a post-war settlement territory for his troops. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberost
- **Verkehrspolitik (movement policy).** The territory was divided into sealed districts; movement between districts was forbidden — a rule that "destroyed the livelihood of many merchant Jews" (and prevented everyone from visiting relatives in the next district). German-only rail use, German-taught schools, "civilising" *Kulturarbeit*. Same source; Liulevicius thesis (below).
- **Exploitation and famine.** Tomas Balkelis, "Humanitarian crisis in German occupied Vilnius, 1916–1917," *Journal of Baltic Studies* (2022): Ober Ost policy "was built on the idea of winning the 'total war' by squeezing out all economic and human resources from the occupied areas"; increasing requisitions including confiscation of full harvests, rationing, movement and trade bans "produced the famine" in Vilnius; on 12 May 1917 Polish, Jewish, Lithuanian and Belarusian representatives telegraphed Chancellor Bethmann-Hollweg that Vilnius was "in the grip of horrifying famine," death rate 98/1000, bread ration 200 g, people "chased on the streets and sent to forced labour." https://gs.elaba.lt/object/elaba:126606465/126606465.pdf
- **Toward Jews — instrumental, not exterminatory, and explicitly contrasted with Russia by contemporaries.** USHMM: Hindenburg/Ludendorff "exploited the region's resources… While they held a paternalistic and often prejudiced perspective… Jews were not treated much differently than other ethnic groups. One German historical mission even worked to document and preserve old wooden synagogues." German forces in 1914–15 deliberately used Jews as interpreters and procurement middlemen, and Berlin sponsored a Committee for the Liberation of Russian Jews (17 Aug 1914) — exploitation of Jewish usefulness against Russia, documented in the German literature and in Tracey Norrell's dissertation on Ostjuden under German occupation (Univ. of Tennessee, 2010): https://trace.tennessee.edu/bitstreams/1f9beea7-3fc0-486d-8d44-4e40073e2e32/download ; 1914-1918-Online, Baltic States article: the use of Jews as intermediaries "in turn led to anti-Semitic allegations of collaboration" from neighbours: https://appwritefunc.yet-another-testing-domain.com/api/grab?url=https://encyclopedia.1914-1918-online.net/article/baltic-states-and-finland/?format=pdf
- **Ludendorff's own later antisemitism is documented — and must be dated.** USHMM: he was "deeply antisemitic," a creator/diffuser of the "stab-in-the-back" myth blaming Jews among others, and an early Hitler supporter (Beer Hall Putsch, 1923). **None of that is evidence of a 1915 Ober Ost anti-Jewish measure, and this report does not use it as such.** In 1915–16 his Jewish policy, as documented, was the Verkehrspolitik's economic strangulation (which hit Jewish merchants hardest because they were the trading class) plus instrumental cultivation — a bureaucratic, not a pogromist, harm.

### (3) Specific dated event
**Vilnius, entered 18 September 1915.** Theodore Weeks's study of wartime Vilnius: German proclamations in five languages; within days, registration fees, bans on street food trade and public gatherings, pigeon-killing orders; complaints within two weeks of requisitions "compensated by worthless scraps of paper" and of the citizens' committee being ignored; by 29 Sept 1915 "the most irritated and embittered are the Jews" — the paper trail of a *paper* occupation: everything registered, receipted, and squeezed. https://www.journals.vu.lt/Colloquia/article/download/26761/26120

### (4) Historian assessment
- **Vejas Gabriel Liulevicius, *War Land on the Eastern Front* (Cambridge UP, 2000):** Ober Ost was a "military utopia" / colonial project whose categories and practices form a line — argued, and debated — toward later German eastern occupation practice; Ludendorff ran it as a personal military state with "dictatorial powers," subordinate only to the Kaiser (1914-1918-Online formulation). Summary: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ober_Ost?oldid=441398524 ; Balkelis (above) calls it "a military utopia par excellence… an army's feeding ground and a source of cheap labour."
- **Alexander Watson, *Ring of Steel* (2014)** treats Ober Ost's food regime as blockade-driven sequestration designed "to maximise the sequestering and production of agricultural products," with Ludendorff (then eastern chief of staff) as its ruler — exploitation as system, in explicit contrast to the Russian zone's scorched-earth deportations (Watson via review: http://www.quarterly-review.org/first-total-war/ — review source; verify in the book).

### Records / archives — the comparative point for the whole project
Ober Ost is the **only** regime in this report with a documented *preservation* act toward Jewish material culture (the synagogue-documentation mission, USHMM above), and the only one whose occupation *generated* a vast surviving German archive of its own (registration, requisition receipts, district reports). The contrast is the lesson: **records survive where an occupier rules by paper for years; they vanish where sovereignty changes six times and each holder expels the previous holder's registrars.** Bukovina was the second case; Ober Ost the first.

## 13. Max Hoffmann (1869–1927) — Ober Ost chief of staff (Aug 1916 – 1918), Brest-Litovsk negotiator (comparator, corrected role)

### (1) Command, sector, dates
Chief of operations (Ia) of the 8th Army in East Prussia Aug 1914 (a principal planner of Tannenberg), then Ober Ost staff under Ludendorff; **succeeded Ludendorff as Ober Ost Chief of Staff in August 1916** under Prince Leopold of Bavaria; German military lead at Brest-Litovsk (Dec 1917–Mar 1918). Posthumous primary source: *The War of Lost Opportunities* (1924) and *War Diaries and Other Papers* (1929, ed. from his diary). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberost (chiefs of staff list)

### (2) Documented attitudes
- **On the Russian collapse and scorched earth — a primary diary voice.** Hoffmann on the 1915 Great Retreat (quoted in the literature): "It seems that the Russians are actually repeating [the events of] 1812 and retreating along the entire front. They are setting fire to hundreds of their own villages, whose inhabitants have been evacuated." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Retreat_(Russian) — Hoffmann as *witness to the opposing system's civilian policy*, from the diary; that is his main evidentiary value to this project.
- **On eastern policy, 1918 (diary/interview, post hoc):** "In fact, the Ukraine was my suggestion, and my creation… I created the Ukraine, to put it bluntly, merely in order to have a part of Russia to make peace with"; diary, 12 Mar 1918: "The Central Rada has nothing behind it but our military assistance. The moment we withdraw our troops, the whole magnificent edifice collapses." As reported at https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/2064558680398516467.html — **flag: social-media thread as finding aid only; cite *War Diaries* (1929) directly in print.**
- **Toward Jews: no Hoffmann statement or order about Jews was found in this pass. Unverified.** His 23 Aug 1914 diary entry ("There has never been such a war as this… waged with such bestial fury," on the East Prussian invasion atrocity reports) is about Russian invasion violence generally. As quoted in the literature at https://erenow.org/ww/the-first-world-war/6.php
- **Continuity finding:** the Ober Ost system (Verkehrspolitik, requisitions, Kulturarbeit) continued unchanged under Hoffmann 1916–18 — the famine telegram of May 1917 (§12) falls in *his* tenure as chief of staff. Attribution: he administered Ludendorff's system; he did not design it, and no personal civilian-policy initiative of his was found. **Unverified** as a policy author, in either direction.

### (3) Specific dated event
**Brest-Litovsk, December 1917 – March 1918.** Hoffmann's ultimatum diplomacy imposed the treaty that, among much else, fixed the post-war sovereignty under which Bukovina's and Galicia's records were re-partitioned (§9, Saint-Germain transfer/destruction). Included because it is the dated act by a man in this report that most directly shaped *where the paper ended up* — and it was a negotiating-table act, not a battlefield one.

### (4) Historian assessment
The literature's Hoffmann is the brilliant staff cynic — Tannenberg planner, Brest-Litovsk's real negotiator, diarist whose entries historians (e.g. on 1915, above) quote precisely because he was an unsentimental observer of *both* sides' civilian catastrophes. No historian in this pass assesses him on Jews or archives. **Unassessed / unverified** on the brief's questions — stated plainly rather than padded.

**The pattern the table makes visible**

1. **Documentation is thickest at the very top and thinnest at army level.** Stavka and AOK paper survives and is quotable; the men who actually held the Cheremosh–Prut line (Lechitsky above all, Pflanzer-Baltin on Jews) left almost nothing quotable on civilians in the sources reachable from the open web. Any narrative that gives Lechitsky a "policy toward the Jews" is, on present evidence, invention.
2. **The two empires' harms were structurally different.** Russian: categorical, ethnic, expulsive — Jews *as a class* declared hostile by Stavka (Jan 1915), expelled toward the enemy or hostage-stripped, with pogrom/looting tolerated underneath. Habsburg: juridical-military — suspect *lists*, treason gallows, internment (Thalerhof), aimed first at Russophiles, with Jews inside the wider violence/looting net but not the named categorical target. German (Ober Ost): bureaucratic-extractive — no pogrom policy, no expulsion of Jews as Jews, but sealed-district immiseration that fell hardest on Jewish traders, plus the war's only documented occupier effort to *document* synagogues.
3. **Personality mattered at the extremes and at moments of collapse.** Yanushkevich's personal obsession is documented by his own ministers and his own resolutions — remove him (Sept 1915) and the system he built still ran until 1917, but it was *his* build. Kornilov shows the opposite moment: when discipline collapsed entirely (July 1917), the commander's personality expressed itself not as Jewish policy but as execution-as-discipline, while his soldiers pogromised Kalush and Tarnopol underneath him.
4. **Looting discipline correlates with garrison vs. movement, not with nationality of commander.** Brusilov's Czernowitz (Sept 1914): disciplined entry, returned valuables — and, the same weeks, Cossack plunder of Wiznitz and Sadagora in the countryside his HQ could not or did not control. The capital/province gap recurs in every sector in this report.

## 15. What this means for Vizhnitz and for the Jewish records — proven / pattern / unknown

**Proven (documented, cited above)**
- Wiznitz was plundered and burned, with the synagogues of the area, in the first Russian occupation, autumn 1914 (Menczel-based Czernowitz account) — §3(3).
- From 25 Jan / 4 Feb 1915, a written Stavka policy ordered the Jews of Bukovina, by name, evicted toward the enemy and their prosperous/influential members taken hostage to Kyiv province; the front commander (Ivanov) implemented it with a 200-mile deportation rule — §§1–2.
- The Habsburg return (Feb 1915, Pflanzer-Baltin's sector) imposed re-registration, passes, martial law and special requisitions, and the AOK system behind it ran suspect lists, treason courts and Thalerhof internment that included Bukovinians — §§8–9.
- Czernowitz changed hands under, successively, Ivanov's front (Brusilov's and Lechitsky's armies), Pflanzer-Baltin, Lechitsky again (June 1916), and Kövess/Křitek (1917) — each change documented above with dates.

**Pattern (documented for Galicia/Bukovina generally; application to Wiznitz is inference, labelled as such)**
- Where the registrar/notable class is expelled or taken hostage and Yiddish correspondence is banned, a community's register chain breaks even if the physical book is never seized. That is the most defensible mechanism, on this evidence, for a missing pre-1918 Wiznitz register series — *destruction of the record-keeping system*, with physical destruction (fire, in a town reported plundered and burned) as a second, also-documented-for-the-town, mechanism.
- Military personnel paper for Galicia/Bukovina was largely destroyed in the 1918–19 sovereignty transfers (Kriegsarchiv) — an analogue, not proof, for civil/community paper.

**Unknown (explicitly not claimed)**
- Any personal order by Lechitsky, Kaledin, Shcherbachev, Pflanzer-Baltin, Kövess, Křitek, Böhm-Ermolli, Arz or Hoffmann about Jews, looting or archives.
- Whether a Wiznitz register book was seized, burned, evacuated or simply discontinued — no document found names the Wiznitz Matriken.
- Brusilov's memoir text on Jews/Czernowitz (not searched in full text in this pass); the 9th Army's own war diary (RGVIA) and the 7. Armee Kriegstagebuch (Kriegsarchiv) for the Wiznitz sector — the two highest-value unworked primary targets this report identifies.
